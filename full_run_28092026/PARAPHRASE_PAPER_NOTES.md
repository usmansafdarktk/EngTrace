# Presenting the paraphrase experiment (Q5) in the paper

Notes for the authors, 2026-10-01 (D-165). Every figure below is in `results/RESULTS.md` (Q5 and the
decoding repeats), `PARAPHRASE.md`, `PARAPHRASE_REVIEW.md` or `TRACE_REVIEW_paraphrase.md`, all
generated by committed scripts; the record of how the arm was made is D-157, D-161 and D-162, and
`PARAPHRASE_AUDIT.md` rechecks every number from the artifacts. Nothing here should be typed into the
paper from memory: cite the file.

## What the experiment is, in one paragraph

A wording-robustness test on a fixed subsample. Each of 450 items (the 1st, 6th and 11th instance of
every one of the 150 templates) was rewritten once by Mistral Large 3, a model from a family on neither
the roster nor the judge's side, under rules that keep every number, unit, symbol, formula, acronym and
part in place; a script checked each rewrite (numbers as a multiset, every technical token, part labels,
word similarity at most 0.75, length, no preamble), after restoring the original's ASCII notation where
the writer had used Unicode the original lacks; a domain expert of the item's branch, from the fifteen
who certified the templates, then confirmed each pair is the same problem with the same answer and adds
nothing. The eleven models answered the paraphrases under the main run's prompt, settings and scoring,
against the original items' gold and milestones, on the same served checkpoints as the main run. Q5
reports, per model, the paired difference paraphrase minus original (answer score, E3 coverage,
E5-strict), with template-level bootstrap intervals and a sign-flip test corrected across models, and
across models the stability of their ordering.

## What to state

1. **The result, as a bound.** For ten of the eleven models the change in answer score between the
   original and the reworded problem lies within ±5 points at 90% confidence; for the eleventh,
   Qwen3-235B-2507, the interval reaches −6.7 points. No model's change is significant after correction
   across the eleven. The same holds, with the same magnitudes, for the milestones the trace reaches (E3)
   and for the judge's strict verdict on the reasoning (E5). Say "within ±5 points", not "unaffected".
2. **What the bound answers.** The reviewers' objection was that fixed template wording lets a model
   recognise the problem type and reproduce a memorised method; the templates have been public since
   January. A drop of five points or more from rewording alone is ruled out for ten models. That is the
   wording-memorisation reading of the contamination question; it is answered by the same measurement.
3. **The ordering.** The models' order on the paraphrases agrees with their order on the originals with
   tau 0.636; the sampling-noise distribution of tau at this arm's size has its 5th percentile at 0.636
   and its median at 0.783. State that the agreement is at the lower edge of what sampling noise alone
   produces, and that the top five models lie within 0.012 of one another on the originals, so their
   order is a near-tie in either arm. Report the tiers as stable and the order within the top tier as
   not resolved by this arm.
4. **The two small decreases.** GLM-5.3-flash and Claude Sonnet 5 have 90% intervals wholly below zero
   (−0.020 and −0.018). State them as small decreases the data favour, within the 5-point bound and not
   significant after correction; do not call them robust and do not call them failures.
5. **Qwen3-235B-2507.** The largest point decrease (−3.1 points), the only model whose interval reaches
   past the margin, and a change about four times its own run-to-run spread on the repeat items. State
   that for this model a 5-point drop is not ruled out. It is one model of eleven and the test is
   two-sided at 95%; do not build a finding on it.
6. **The arm is smaller than planned, and why.** 277 pairs over 115 templates instead of 450 over 150.
   134 items got no paraphrase that kept their notation and terms through three attempts (the writer
   spells acronyms out, writes `=` and `%` as words, drops qualifiers, or copies nearly verbatim); the
   experts removed 39 more. Industrial engineering is the most affected (43 of 90 kept). Say so in the
   section, not only in the limitations: the conclusion holds for the templates that can be reworded
   without loss, which are the less formula-bound ones.
7. **The expert step was necessary.** 12% of the pairs that passed every script check were rejected by
   the experts, almost always for a dropped or altered qualifier that changes the physics or the asked
   quantity ("solid" shaft, "uniform flow", "approximately normal", "the magnitude of the force", "normal"
   versus "standard" boiling point). This is evidence for the method and a warning for anyone who
   paraphrases technical benchmarks by script alone. One sentence, with the counts.
8. **Provenance.** One writer, one prompt for all 450 (the final one of four; the earlier three are
   recorded in D-157 and their attempts set aside), the manifest records each paraphrase's hash and the
   prompt's, the experts saw and the models received the same text, and every served checkpoint equals
   the main run's.

## What not to state

- Not "the models are robust to paraphrasing" or "unaffected by wording": the test bounds the effect at
  5 points for ten models; it does not show zero, and effects of one to three points are not excluded.
- Not "no contamination": the paraphrases keep every number and the problem structure, so the test
  rules out memorised **wording**; a model that has learned a template's **method** from the public
  templates would pass it. Say "no evidence of wording-level memorisation".
- Not "the ranking is stable": tau 0.636 is at the 5th percentile of the noise distribution, not in its
  middle. "The tiers hold; the order within the top tier is not resolved" is what the data support.
- Not "rewording moves a model less than re-running it": true for two of the four repeated models, not
  for Qwen3-235B-2507, and the repeat spreads are themselves small (at most 2 points). If the comparison
  is used at all, give all four rows.
- Not "450 items" or "150 templates" anywhere in Q5; not "the plan's 450" without the 277.
- Not the roster-wide tau noise floor of 0.881 as the comparison for this arm: it rests on halves of 7
  or 8 items per template; the floor at the arm's size (0.783 median) is the right one. Both are in the
  results; cite the second.
- Not the E3 intervals off zero as findings: three columns of eleven, no multiplicity adjustment applied
  to that column; the answer-score column is the pre-registered test.
- Not the median 41 seconds per item as a quality claim either way; it is recorded, not interpreted.
- Not a per-branch conclusion: the branches differ in how many items survived, not in a measured
  wording effect, and no per-branch test was planned or run.

## The margin, honestly

The ±5-point margin was fixed on 2026-10-01 after the point estimates (all within ±3.2 points) were
known and before the 90% intervals were computed. It equals the plan's largest detectable paired
difference (5.1 points at 15% discordance, `ANALYSIS_PLAN.md` Q5), which is the only reason it is 5
and not another number. Say in the paper that the equivalence margin is the plan's detectable
difference and that it was set after the point estimates; a reader who objects can read the intervals
directly, and the primary test (the sign-flip test with Holm) was pre-registered and is unchanged.

## Suggested wording

**For the robustness section.** "To test whether the fixed template wording inflates scores, we rewrote
one instance in five of every template (450 items) with a model from a family outside both the roster
and the judge, under rules that preserve every number, unit, symbol and part; 316 rewrites passed
scripted checks and a domain expert of the item's branch confirmed 277 of them as the same problem
with the same answer (Appendix X gives the losses by template and branch). On these 277 pairs, the
paired change in answer score between original and paraphrase lies between −3.1 and +1.6 points across
the eleven models, with no change significant after Holm correction; for ten models the 90% interval
lies within ±5 points, the plan's smallest detectable difference, and for Qwen3-235B-2507 it reaches
−6.7. The milestones reached (E3) and the judge's verdict on the reasoning (E5) change by the same
small amounts. The models' ordering agrees across the two arms with Kendall's τ = 0.64 (95% CI 0.46 to
0.87), at the lower edge of the τ distribution that sampling alone produces at this sample size
(median 0.78); the top five models lie within 1.2 points of one another, so their order is a near-tie
in either arm. We conclude that a drop of five points or more from rewording alone is ruled out for ten
of the eleven models, and that the tiers of the ranking, not the order within the top tier, are stable
under rewording."

**For the limitations.** "The paraphrase test covers 115 of the 150 templates: the writer could not
reword 134 items while keeping their notation and terms, and the experts rejected 39 pairs for a
qualifier the rewrite dropped or altered, so the most formula-bound templates, and industrial
engineering in particular, are under-represented. It tests memorised wording, not memorised methods:
numbers and problem structure were kept. The equivalence margin of five points was fixed after the
point estimates were known. One paraphrase per item from one writer was used."
