## What the run is, in one paragraph

Eleven models answered the 2,250 items of the frozen pool (150 certified templates x 15 instances, drawn
from a private seed and never published; D-114 to D-119) once each, under the Appendix P prompt, at each
provider's default decoding, with a 32,768-token ceiling (16,384 for Muse Glimmer, its only eligible
endpoint's cap). Every trace was scored by a deterministic stack validated against 15 experts' labels on
the pilot: a three-way answer check (correct / partial / incorrect), milestone coverage derived from the
template's own computed quantities (E3), an arithmetic digit rule on every claim the trace displays, and
two judged stages with MiMo-V2.5-Pro, a model from no roster family: E5, which judges only the milestones
E3 could not settle, and a step router that judges every step the digit rule does not flag. The analysis
was fixed before the data (`ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`, D-117): the template is the unit, every interval resamples
templates, every family of tests is Holm-corrected, an unusable trace scores 0 and a partial answer 0.5.
After the run, a reading of the verdicts found three answer-check misreadings the pilot templates never
exercised; they were measured, read verdict by verdict and adopted (D-169), and the stores re-scored. The
headline is the answer score; the reasoning scores describe what the traces reach and where they slip.

## What to state

1. **The headline is the answer score, with its interval and the template as the unit.** Say what a
   point is (correct 1, partial 0.5, incorrect or unusable 0; the fully-solved rate beside it). The top
   five models lie within 0.009 of one another and no pair among them separates after correction; the
   next model (GLM-5.3, 0.948) owes most of its distance to 100 empty traces at the output ceiling; the
   remaining five lie between 0.814 and 0.886 and separate from the top six. Report tiers, not an order
   within the top tier, and say that 32 of 55 pairwise differences hold.
2. **The reasoning score is milestone coverage, and say exactly what it measures.** E5-strict coverage is
   the share of the gold derivation's intermediate quantities that a trace states or that the judge finds
   reached; it measures progress through the derivation, not the absence of error. On the pilot's
   correct-answer traces with a flawed step the milestone evaluators scored below chance (RESULTS_X1
   Finding 5), and a model that states fewer intermediate values scores lower coverage without reasoning
   worse. With that said: coverage runs from 0.816 to 0.923, the intervals of the top eight overlap, and
   the order on coverage differs from the order on answers (Claude Sonnet 5 first on coverage and third on
   answers; DeepSeek V4.1 Flash first on answers and sixth on coverage). Report both orderings and their
   intervals. Do not call the difference a trade-off or a decoupling: no test of it was planned, the
   intervals overlap, and the May version's "trade-off between numeric precision and trace fidelity" is not
   carried over (D-149, NEXT_CYCLE_REVIEW 9.3 item 13).
3. **Where the process scores add information is on the wrong answers.** On traces that score 0 the
   models still reach 46% to 85% of the milestones (readable traces), against a chance floor of 10% to
   22%, so most wrong answers are late failures, not absent derivations. The attribution table says which
   component points at a wrong answer: a MISSING milestone or a router flag on most of them for the weaker
   models (79% and 90% for gpt-oss-20b), on fewer for the stronger ones (24% and 40% for Claude Sonnet 5,
   whose 50 wrong answers are mostly complete derivations with a wrong value). State these as shares of
   traces with a flag, not as diagnoses.
4. **The digit rule: report the rate, its precision, and why it does not rank models.** Among fully solved
   traces, the share with at least one arithmetic claim the rule cannot reconcile runs from 0.5%
   (DeepSeek) to 22.5% (Qwen3-235B-2507). The rule's precision on this roster is 0.905, measured by a
   domain expert on 190 flags after three corrections; its recall on the pilot's labels is 0.427, so the
   rates are flags, not counts of slips. And it reads a different amount of arithmetic per model (2.8 to
   9.8 claims per trace), so a low rate can mean little was read: Qwen's 22.5% comes with 9.8 claims per
   trace, DeepSeek's 0.5% with 3.3. Give the claims-per-trace column beside the rate, and do not say
   "model X makes fewer arithmetic errors than model Y".
5. **The router: the only component with any signal on a misstated rule, and a flag is a flag.** The
   judge flags 1.1% (DeepSeek) to 22.0% (GPT-5.4 mini) of fully solved traces. On the pilot's labels the
   router finds 60% of the steps the experts call incorrect at precision 0.71, but inside correct-answer
   traces 36% at 0.70, and on planted conceptual defects MiMo catches about a third. So "a flagged step in
   x% of correct traces" is what the data say; "x% of correct answers rest on flawed reasoning" is not.
6. **The cliff, per model with its interval.** Every model scores lower on Advanced than on Easy
   templates (+0.055 to +0.199). The gap is significant after Holm, under a test valid for unequal
   variances, for four models: the four lowest-scoring ones, three of which ran without extended
   reasoning. For the top five the gaps are 6 to 7 points with intervals that mostly exclude zero but do
   not survive correction. Report the count under Welch (4 of 11) with the planned permutation's count
   (9 of 11) beside it and say why the planned test is liberal here (D-146); report the detectable gap;
   and say that GLM-5.3's gap is mostly an output-ceiling effect (its empties fall on Advanced templates;
   the variations table gives the gap with unusable rows left out).
7. **Decoding, plainly.** Each model ran at its provider's default. Four wrote no reasoning tokens at all
   (Gemma 4 26B, Qwen3-235B-2507, GPT-5.4 mini, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite) and are four of the five lowest
   scores; seven did. The roster therefore does not compare closed with open models, or small with large,
   at equal reasoning effort, and the paper must not read the closed tier's scores as the models' ceiling.
   Appendix P states the setting per model, the token ceiling, Muse's 16,384 cap, and the served endpoints.
8. **Unusable traces are scored 0, and where they fall.** 246 of 24,750 traces (1.0%) ended without a
   readable answer, 243 of them empty at the output ceiling; 153 fall on six templates, four iterative by
   construction (`qr_policy_one_iteration`, `work_isothermal_virial`, `normal_depth_iteration`,
   `adiabatic_flame_temperature`, `aoq_ati_rectifying`, `vdw_solve_for_volume`). On those templates the
   score measures finishing within the ceiling as well as solving; say so where they are reported, and give
   the unusable rate per model beside the score (Q1) and the score with unusable rows excluded
   (Sensitivity), where the ordering changes (tau 0.636) because GLM-5.3's 100 empties stop counting.
9. **Consistency within a template is a finding in itself.** The strongest models solve all 15 instances
   of 85% to 91% of the single-path templates; the weaker ones of 36% to 48%, and solve some but not all
   instances of about half of them, which on a single-path template is a failure on the numbers, not the
   method. Report the all / some / none shares (Q4) for both template groups.
10. **Failure grows with depth.** The wrong-answer rate on items whose gold derivation has six or more
    milestones is 0.04 to 0.29 against 0.00 to 0.05 on one-milestone items, for every model. Descriptive;
    say that milestone count is the template's, not a difficulty label.
11. **The evaluator section states the validation and the corrections.** The pilot figures (300 traces,
    15 experts, three per trace, kappa 0.78 between and 0.83 within; answer check 0.982 / 0.927; E3 F1
    0.923; E5 F1 0.958; digit rule 0.817 / 0.427; router 0.707 / 0.603; MiMo on planted defects) are the
    evidence. Then: the check was corrected after the first results and again after a reading of the
    finished run's verdicts (D-137 to D-139, D-147, D-169), each correction measured on the gold, on the
    pilot's labels and on every full-run trace before adoption; none moved a pilot verdict against the
    experts; D-169 moved 238 of 24,750 verdicts, 234 to correct, every one read; one wrong credit remains,
    a coefficient within 0.2% of the target, and is a stated limit. The forms D-169 reads occur in none
    of the pilot templates, so the experts' agreement figures do not vouch for them: say that.
12. **Scoring statements the reader needs.** Symbolic answers are scored by the numbers they state (D-138;
    nine templates; the Sensitivity table gives the pool without them, +0.002 to +0.011 per model);
    `array` items and scalar lines stating two quantities are verified on the last computed value
    (`vdw_solve_for_volume`, `levenspiel_plot_interpretation`, `standing_wave_formation`,
    `finite_convolution`; D-168); 84 multipart items are checked on fewer quantities than the question's
    labelled parts (D-145); a partial verdict means some but not all targets match (514 roster verdicts,
    278 on non-multipart items).
13. **Branch, domain and level tables are descriptive.** Give them with a sentence saying no interval or
    test accompanies them yet (`docs/EVALUATION_NEXT_STEPS.md` A2 adds intervals). Do not say "chemical
    engineering is hardest": the lowest branch differs by model (civil for gpt-oss-20b at 0.673, chemical
    for Gemma at 0.734, electrical for DeepSeek at 0.954). Thermodynamics is the lowest domain for nine
    of eleven models (0.517 to 0.922); signals_and_systems is not, now that its two pi-fraction templates
    are read correctly (0.883 to 1.000).
14. **Repeats.** Four cheap models, 300 items, three repeats: the score's SD across repeats is 0.004 to
    0.013, and 86% to 94% of items get the same verdict every time. The other seven models have no
    decoding-variance figure; say so rather than generalise.
15. **Provenance in one sentence.** The pool is frozen with per-item hashes and a committed seed
    commitment, the prompt is hashed on every trace, the store's CONFIG records the commit and the
    evaluators' hashes, and `results.json` records what produced each table; the private seed and the
    pool are released at publication.

## What not to state

- Not "model X is the best": the top five are within 0.9 points and no pair among them separates.
- Not "trace fidelity" or "reasoning quality" for milestone coverage: it is coverage of the gold's stated
  intermediates. Not "a trade-off between accuracy and reasoning": no test of it was planned and the
  intervals overlap.
- Not "x% of correct answers rest on flawed reasoning" from the digit rule or the router: the figures are
  flag rates with known precision (0.905; 0.70) and recall below one half.
- Not a ranking of models by arithmetic reliability from the digit rule: the rule reads 2.8 to 9.8 claims
  per trace depending on the model's style.
- Not "the complexity cliff holds" without the count under Welch (4 of 11) and the per-model intervals;
  not the planned permutation's 9 of 11 as the headline.
- Not "closed models underperform open ones": four models ran without reasoning; the comparison is not at
  equal effort.
- Not "chemical engineering is hardest" or any branch ordering without the intervals that are still to be
  added; not domain means as findings.
- Not the pilot's evaluator agreement as validation of the forms D-169 corrected: those forms are absent
  from the pilot.
- Not "2,250 unique problems": 2,250 instances, 2,250 distinct questions, 150 templates (D-116).
- Not May's Table 1 numbers beside these: the old pool, roster and check are not reproducible (D-105,
  NEXT_CYCLE_REVIEW 4.3). Say why the numbers moved, do not compare them.
- Not "no E0 at scale" as a limitation without the reason: unlabelled full-run E0 numbers would have added
  cost without evidence (D-105); the evaluator comparison belongs to the labelled slice.
- Not the set-aside Qwen3.8-27B in any table (D-132).

## Suggested wording

**For the results, first paragraph.** "Table N gives each model's answer score, the mean over 2,250 items of
1 for a correct final answer, 0.5 for a partially correct one and 0 otherwise, with a 95% interval from
resampling the 150 templates. Five models lie between 0.967 and 0.976 and no pair among them differs after
Holm correction over the 55 pairs; GLM-5.3 (0.948) follows, with 100 traces that ran out of output
tokens scored 0; the remaining five lie between 0.814 and 0.886. 32 of the 55 pairwise differences hold at
a Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 (a sign-flip permutation over per-template differences). Every model scores lower
on Advanced than on Easy templates, by 5.5 to 19.9 points; the gap survives correction for the four
lowest-scoring models and not for the others (Table N+1 gives each gap with its interval and the smallest
gap the design detects)."

**For the reasoning scores.** "Milestone coverage, the share of the gold derivation's intermediate
quantities a trace states or the judge finds reached, runs from 0.816 to 0.923 across the roster (Table M).
It measures how far a derivation goes, not whether it is sound: in the expert study, milestone evaluators
could not tell a correct-answer trace with a flawed step from a clean one. Its ordering differs from the
answer score's, with Claude Sonnet 5 first on coverage and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash first on answers, but the
intervals overlap and we do not read a trade-off into it. On the traces that score 0, models still reach
46% to 85% of the milestones against a chance floor of 10% to 22%, so most wrong answers are late failures.
Among fully solved traces, the digit rule finds an arithmetic claim it cannot reconcile in 0.5% to 22.5%
(precision 0.905 on 190 flags read by a domain expert; it reads 2.8 to 9.8 claims per trace, so these rates
do not rank the models), and the router's judge flags a step in 1.1% to 22.0%."

**For the evaluator section.** "The answer check, the milestone matcher and the digit rule were validated
against 15 experts' labels on 300 traces (agreement 0.982 on the answer, F1 0.923 on milestones, precision
0.817 at recall 0.427 on arithmetic slips; Appendix M). The check was corrected twice after the run on a
reading of its verdicts, each correction measured on the gold solutions, the labelled traces and every
full-run trace before adoption and recorded; the second moved 238 of 24,750 verdicts, 234 of them to
correct, in forms (pi-fractions, a quantity stated in two units) that the pilot templates do not contain and
that the experts therefore did not arbitrate. One wrong credit introduced by it is known and stated."

**For the limitations.** "Four models ran without extended reasoning at their providers' defaults, so the
roster does not compare models at equal effort. 1.0% of traces ended without a readable answer and score 0,
concentrated on six templates, four of them iterative, where the score also measures finishing within the
token ceiling. Milestone coverage credits stated intermediates and is blind to a misstated rule; only the
judged router has any signal there, at about a third of planted defects. The digit rule's flags are 90%
real on this roster but it finds under half of the slips the experts marked. Symbolic answers are scored by
the numbers they state, and array items and lines stating two quantities on the last of them. The top five
models are not separable at this size; 150 templates detect answer-score differences of about 1 to 4 points
between models and Easy-to-Advanced gaps of 7 to 15 points."
