\section{Error Analysis}
\label{appendix:error_analysis}

What kind of error produces a wrong answer, and are the top tier's remaining incorrect verdicts
errors at all?
This section gives the taxonomy, the sample, and the readings
behind~\autoref{subsec:error_analysis}, then the templates whose wording or answer form, rather
than a model's error, yields incorrect verdicts.

\paragraph{Taxonomy.} The six categories follow the stages of an engineering derivation, from the
most to the least fundamental:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Hallucination:} a value, constant, or equation that exists in no engineering reference.
\item \emph{Setup or assumption:} the problem is framed wrongly before any equation applies, such as
a wrong boundary condition or an ignored constraint.
\item \emph{Formula or principle:} the setup is right, but the governing equation or principle is
wrong.
\item \emph{Unit or dimension:} a missing conversion, mixed unit systems, or a value in the wrong
unit.
\item \emph{Sign or direction:} the wrong sign or physical direction for a quantity.
\item \emph{Calculation:} every earlier stage is right, and the arithmetic or algebra is wrong.
\end{enumerate}
A domain expert answers the six questions in this order and stops at the first yes, so the label is
the most fundamental error and not a downstream symptom; two further options are no error (the
answer is correct, or the question admits it) and incomplete.

\paragraph{Sample and Readings.} We read 160 wrong answers, 40 from each of four models chosen to
contrast the tiers, each a readable response whose final answer the check scores incorrect; for each
model we draw the 40 at random across levels in proportion to its wrong answers and spread them over
its templates, 79 templates in all.
Three domain experts of the wrong answer's branch, from the fifteen who certified the templates,
read each full response without the model's name.
\autoref{tab:errors} gives the readings by model and by level with the agreement.
Beyond what~\autoref{subsec:error_analysis} reads from it, the table shows that unit and dimension
errors are nearly absent (1 of 480 readings) and sign errors rare (11, in two models), that 10 of
the 13 hallucination readings are \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}'s, and that agreement is high for every model
and lowest for \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} (Fleiss' $\kappa$ 0.839 against 0.930 to 0.968), whose errors
spread across the conceptual categories.
The categories describe sampled wrong answers, not rates over all responses, and the Advanced
column's 38\% no-error share comes largely from \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, whose wrong answers are
mostly Advanced and mostly no error.

\paragraph{Templates Whose Wording Does Not Pin the Answer.} Three chemical domain experts read the
two Advanced chemical templates on which the top tier's wrong answers concentrate, and three domain
experts of each template's branch read six templates on which many wrong answers land between 0.2\%
and 5\% of the gold value:
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Compression work} (\texttt{work\_isothermal\_virial}): all three answer that the wording
decides neither between the closed-system and the flow reading, whose values differ by more than
the tolerance, nor between the two forms of the truncated virial equation, so the question has no
single correct answer and all the responses shown are correct under one reading.
\item \emph{Flame temperature} (\texttt{adiabatic\_flame\_temperature}): two answer that standard
data sources differ by more than the tolerance and one that they agree only within one source, two
that the gold method is the standard one and one that it is not, and all three that some of the
responses shown are correct under some reading.
\item \emph{The six near-miss templates:} for each, all three answer that the question has one
correct answer and that the response shown, within 5\% of the gold value, is not correct.
\end{itemize}
We therefore report the level gap with and without the two chemical templates.
Of the top five models' 171 incorrect verdicts, 91 fall on three templates: 38 on the
compression-work template, 34 on one symbolic template (\texttt{ber\_estimation\_mary}), and 19 on
the flame-temperature template.
Two further sources of incorrect verdicts concern the answer's form rather than its value: 17
templates prescribe the digits of the answer, so a value within the tolerance but not at the
prescribed digits is incorrect by the question's own terms (311 incorrect verdicts across the eleven
models, 120 of them within 0.2\% of the target on 11 of these templates); and we score the nine
templates with symbolic answers by the numbers they state.
Four templates whose answers can be read off the question's wording stay in the evaluation set,
since leaving them out moves FAC by at most 0.004.
