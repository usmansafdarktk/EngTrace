\section{Error Analysis}
\label{appendix:error_analysis}

What kind of error produces a wrong answer, and are the first five's remaining incorrect verdicts
errors at all?
This section gives the taxonomy, the sample, and the readings
behind~\autoref{subsec:error_analysis}, then the near misses and the incorrect verdicts that
concern the answer's form rather than its value.

\paragraph{Taxonomy.} The six categories follow the stages of an engineering derivation, from the
most to the least fundamental:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Hallucination:} a value, constant, or equation that exists in no engineering reference.
\item \emph{Setup or assumption:} the problem is framed wrongly before any equation applies, such as
a wrong boundary condition or an ignored constraint.
\item \emph{Formula or principle:} the setup is right, but the governing equation or principle is
wrong.
\item \emph{Unit or dimension:} a missing conversion, mixed unit systems, or a value in the wrong
unit.
\item \emph{Sign or direction:} the wrong sign or physical direction for a quantity.
\item \emph{Calculation:} every earlier stage is right, and the arithmetic or algebra is wrong.
\end{enumerate}
A domain expert answers the six questions in this order and stops at the first yes, so the label is
the most fundamental error and not a downstream symptom; two further options are no error (the
answer is correct, or the question admits it) and incomplete.

\paragraph{Sample and Readings.} We read 137 wrong answers from four models that span the FAC order
(\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}),
each a readable response at the providers' default settings whose final answer the check scores
incorrect; we draw up to 40 per model at random, across levels in proportion to the model's wrong
answers and spread over its templates, 77 templates in all.
Three domain experts of the wrong answer's branch, from the fifteen who certified the templates,
read each full response without the model's name.
Unit and dimension errors are nearly absent and sign errors rare, in two models only; every
hallucination reading is \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}'s; and agreement, high for every model, is lowest for
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, whose errors spread across the conceptual categories (\autoref{tab:errors}).
The categories describe sampled wrong answers, not rates over all responses, and they describe the
responses at the providers' default settings, not those of the three models at matched settings.

\paragraph{Near Misses.} On each of six templates where many wrong answers land between 0.2\% and
5\% of the gold value, the near miss shown to the domain experts is the model's error, not the
wording's.
Three domain experts of each template's branch read these templates
(\texttt{annulus\_flowrate}, \texttt{best\_hydraulic\_rectangular\_section},
\texttt{manning\_rectangular\_discharge}, \texttt{pfr\_volume\_changing\_rate},
\texttt{pitzer\_correlation\_z}, and \texttt{vdw\_solve\_for\_volume}), and for each, all three
answer that the question has one correct answer and that the response shown, within 5\% of the
gold value, is not correct.

\paragraph{Incorrect Verdicts and the Answer's Form.} About a quarter of the first five's incorrect
verdicts fall on three templates, and most of these lie within 0.2\% of a target whose digits the
question prescribes: of the 126, 18 fall on \texttt{qr\_policy\_one\_iteration}, 9 on
\texttt{single\_sampling\_oc\_point}, and 7 on \texttt{adiabatic\_flame\_temperature}; on the first
two, 24 of the 27 lie that close.
Two sources of incorrect verdicts concern the answer's form rather than its value.
First, 17 templates prescribe the digits of the answer, so a value within the tolerance but not at
the prescribed digits is incorrect by the question's own terms (240 incorrect verdicts across the
eleven models, 108 of them within 0.2\% of the target on 11 of these templates).
Second, four of the nine templates with symbolic answers are scored by the numbers they state, while
the other five also accept an equivalent expression (\autoref{appendix:scoring}); the first five's
symbolic incorrect verdicts all fall on one of these five, \texttt{ber\_estimation\_mary}.
Changing one clause of the rule at a time (the absolute-value clause, the last-digit term, the
prescribed digits, or proportional partial credit) moves a model's FAC by at most 0.030 and keeps
Kendall's $\tau$ with the ordering as scored at 0.782 or above (\autoref{tab:scoring_variants}).
Four templates whose answers can be read off the question's wording stay in the evaluation set,
since leaving them out moves FAC by at most 0.002: \texttt{levenspiel\_plot\_interpretation},
\texttt{line\_balancing\_heuristic}, \texttt{system\_properties\_memory\_causality}, and
\texttt{system\_property\_linearity}.
