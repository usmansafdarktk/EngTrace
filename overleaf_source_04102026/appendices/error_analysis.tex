\section{Error Analysis}
\label{appendix:error_analysis}

This section supports the Error Analysis of~\autoref{subsec:error_analysis}: the taxonomy and the
reading protocol, the sample, the readers' agreement, the results by model and by level, and the
templates whose wording does not pin the answer.

\subsection{Taxonomy and Protocol}
The taxonomy has six categories, ordered from the most to the least fundamental stage of an
engineering derivation: hallucination (a value, constant, or equation that exists in no engineering
reference); setup or assumption error (the problem is framed wrongly before any equation is applied:
a wrong boundary condition, an ignored constraint, or a misidentified configuration); formula or
principle error (the setup is right, but the governing equation or principle is wrong); unit or
dimensional error (a missing conversion, mixed unit systems, or a value substituted in the wrong
unit); sign or direction error (the wrong sign or physical direction for a quantity); and
calculation error (every earlier stage is right, and the arithmetic or algebra produces the wrong
result).
A reader answers the six questions in this order and stops at the first yes, so that the label is
the most fundamental error present and not a downstream symptom, and pastes the shortest excerpt of
the working that shows it.
Two options cover what the six do not: no error (the answer is correct, or the question admits it)
and incomplete (the working stops before an answer).
Three domain experts of its branch, from the fifteen who certified the templates, read each wrong
answer behind an opaque code and without the model's name.

\subsection{Sample}
We read 160 wrong answers, 40 from each of four models chosen to contrast the tiers:
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} from the top tier, and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b} from the lower one.
A wrong answer is a readable response whose final answer the check scores incorrect; for each model
the 40 are drawn at random across levels in proportion to the model's wrong answers at each level
and spread round-robin over its templates (\autoref{tab:error_sample}), 79 templates in all.

\subsection{Agreement and Results}
The three readers agree closely: Fleiss'~$\kappa$ of 0.930 over the 480 readings (0.839 to 0.968 per
model).
\autoref{tab:error_by_model} gives the readings and the majority labels by model,
and~\autoref{tab:error_by_level} the readings by level; no reader used the incomplete option.
The categories describe sampled wrong answers, 40 per model, and are not rates over all responses.
Formula or principle errors are 18\% of the readings on Intermediate and Advanced problems against
3\% on Easy ones, where calculation slips are 69 of 78 readings.

\subsection{Templates Whose Wording Does Not Pin the Answer}
Three chemical domain experts read the two Advanced chemical templates on which the top tier's wrong
answers concentrate, and three experts of the template's branch read each of six templates on which
many wrong answers land between 0.2\% and 5\% of the gold value; \autoref{tab:template_readings}
gives the questions and the answers.
For the compression-work template, the question names neither the closed-system nor the flow reading
of the work, nor which two-term form of the virial equation, and the readings differ by more than
the tolerance, so the experts call the answer not unique and the responses shown correct under one
reading; for the flame-temperature template, standard data sources differ by more than the
tolerance, so the answer depends on the data source the question leaves open.
For the six near-miss templates, the experts confirm that the answer is unique and that a response
landing within 5\% but outside the tolerance is not correct.
The level gap of~\autoref{subsec:results} is therefore reported with and without the two chemical
templates.
Two further sources of incorrect verdicts at the top are a matter of form: 17 templates prescribe
the digits of the answer in the question, and the check requires them, which accounts for 311
incorrect verdicts across the eleven models, 120 of them within 0.2\% of the target
(\autoref{tab:exact_digits}); and the nine templates with symbolic answers are scored by the numbers
they state.
Of the top five models' 171 incorrect verdicts, 38 are symbolic, 27 within 0.2\% on a template that
prescribes the digits, and 57 on the two chemical templates: 122 of the 171.
Finally, four templates whose answers can be read off the question's wording stay in the evaluation
set, since FAC moves by at most 0.004 without them (\autoref{tab:sensitivity}).
