\section{Error Analysis}
\label{appendix:error_analysis}

What kind of error produces a wrong answer, and are the first five's remaining incorrect verdicts
errors at all?
This section gives the taxonomy, the sample, and the readings
behind~\autoref{subsec:error_analysis}, then the near misses and the incorrect verdicts that
concern the answer's form rather than its value.

\paragraph{Taxonomy.} The six categories follow the stages of an engineering derivation, from the
most to the least fundamental:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Hallucination:} a value, constant, or equation that exists in no engineering reference.
\item \emph{Setup or assumption:} the problem is framed wrongly before any equation applies, such as
a wrong boundary condition or an ignored constraint.
\item \emph{Formula or principle:} the setup is right, but the governing equation or principle is
wrong.
\item \emph{Unit or dimension:} a missing conversion, mixed unit systems, or a value in the wrong
unit.
\item \emph{Sign or direction:} the wrong sign or physical direction for a quantity.
\item \emph{Calculation:} every earlier stage is right, and the arithmetic or algebra is wrong.
\end{enumerate}
A domain expert answers the six questions in this order and stops at the first yes, so the label is
the most fundamental error and not a downstream symptom; two further options are no error (the
answer is correct, or the question admits it) and incomplete.

\paragraph{Sample and Readings.} We read 137 wrong answers from four models (19 from
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, 39 from \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, 39 from \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and 40 from
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}), chosen to contrast the tiers, each a readable response at the providers'
default settings whose final answer the check scores incorrect; we draw up to 40 per model at
random, across levels in proportion to the model's wrong answers and spread over its templates, 77
templates in all.
Three domain experts of the wrong answer's branch, from the fifteen who certified the templates,
read each full response without the model's name.
\autoref{tab:errors} gives the readings by model and by level with the agreement; for each level it
gives a category's share of all readings and its share of the readings that name an error, so the
error mix can be compared across levels apart from the answers the experts find correct.
Beyond what~\autoref{subsec:error_analysis} reads from it, the table shows that unit and dimension
errors are nearly absent (1 of 411 readings) and sign errors rare (11, in two models), that 7 of
the 7 hallucination readings are \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}'s, and that agreement is high for every model
and lowest for \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} (Fleiss' $\kappa$ 0.835 against 0.917 to 1.000), whose errors
spread across the conceptual categories.
The categories describe sampled wrong answers, not rates over all responses, and they describe the
responses at the providers' default settings, not those of the three models at matched settings.

\paragraph{Near Misses.} To see whether the wording explains answers that land close to the gold
value, three domain experts of each template's branch read six templates on which many wrong answers
land between 0.2\% and 5\% of the gold value (\texttt{annulus\_flowrate},
\texttt{best\_hydraulic\_rectangular\_section}, \texttt{manning\_rectangular\_discharge},
\texttt{pfr\_volume\_changing\_rate}, \texttt{pitzer\_correlation\_z}, and
\texttt{vdw\_solve\_for\_volume}).
For each of the six, all three answer that the question has one correct answer and that the
response shown, within 5\% of the gold value, is not correct, so the near miss shown on each of
these templates is the model's error, not the wording's.

\paragraph{Incorrect Verdicts and the Answer's Form.} Of the first five models' 99 incorrect
verdicts, 28 fall on three templates: 13 on \texttt{qr\_policy\_one\_iteration}, 8 on
\texttt{primary\_consolidation\_settlement}, and 7 on \texttt{newsvendor\_normal\_demand}.
Of the same 99, 27 lie within 0.2\% of a target whose digits the question prescribes and 3 are
symbolic answers scored by the numbers they state; over 11,250 responses, the first five lose 231.5
answer points in all, 102 to responses with no readable final answer and 30.5 to 61 partial answers
at half credit.
Two sources of incorrect verdicts concern the answer's form rather than its value.
First, 17 templates prescribe the digits of the answer, so a value within the tolerance but not at
the prescribed digits is incorrect by the question's own terms (311 incorrect verdicts across the
eleven models, 120 of them within 0.2\% of the target on 11 of these templates).
Second, four of the nine templates with symbolic answers are scored by the numbers they state, while
the other five also accept an equivalent expression (\autoref{appendix:scoring}).
Scored under other rules, the absolute-value clause, the last-digit term, the prescribed digits and
proportional partial credit each move a model's FAC by at most 0.030 and keep Kendall's $\tau$ with
the ordering as scored at 0.927 or above (\autoref{tab:scoring_variants}).
Four templates whose answers can be read off the question's wording stay in the evaluation set,
since leaving them out moves FAC by at most 0.004: \texttt{levenspiel\_plot\_interpretation},
\texttt{line\_balancing\_heuristic}, \texttt{system\_properties\_memory\_causality}, and
\texttt{system\_property\_linearity}.
