\section{Full Results}
\label{appendix:results}

This section holds the tests and measures behind~\autoref{subsec:overall_performance}
and~\autoref{subsec:difficulty_level}.
Intervals resample templates, every family of tests is Holm-corrected, and a difference that does
not hold comes with the smallest difference the design detects at 80\% power
(\autoref{appendix:protocol}).

\paragraph{Pairwise Comparisons.} Of the 55 pairs, 32 differ on FAC after Holm correction under the
sign-flip test over templates: no pair inside the top five, \texttt{GLM-5.3} only from
\texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}, each of the lower five from every model of the top six, and inside the lower
five only \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} from \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}.
Every non-significant FAC difference lies below what its pair detects (0.010 to 0.065).
On MC, 28 pairs differ: the three highest models, \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{Kimi K3}, and
\texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}, do not separate, while \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} and
\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, which FAC does not separate, do; Kendall's
$\tau$~\citep{kendall1938} between the two orderings is 0.709 (95\% interval 0.514 to 0.855).
Strict FAC, which scores a partial answer 0, separates 31 pairs, all but one of FAC's
(\texttt{gpt-oss-20b} against \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}); a Wilcoxon test~\citep{wilcoxon1945}
separates 29 on MC; and McNemar's exact test~\citep{mcnemar1947}, which treats the 2,250
instances as independent, separates 40.

\paragraph{Scoring Rules.} Halving or doubling the answer tolerance swaps two pairs of models each,
none of which differs after correction ($\tau = 0.927$ against the ordering as scored).
Requiring a correct rounding at the displayed precision rather than accepting one unit either way,
crediting a requested quantity stated in the body but not on the answer line, and leaving out the
four templates answerable from their wording or the nine with symbolic answers move no model's FAC
by more than 0.034 and keep $\tau$ at 0.964 or above.
The ordering changes most when only readable answers count ($\tau = 0.636$), chiefly because
\texttt{GLM-5.3}'s 100 empty responses then drop out.

\paragraph{Coverage and What Marks a Wrong Answer.} \autoref{tab:coverage} gives, per model,
coverage on the wrong answers against its chance floor, the share of wrong answers with a milestone
the judge rules missing, the share of milestones the judge decides, and the precision of the
arithmetic flags of~\autoref{tab:main_results}.
The flag rates do not rank the models: the two checks together recall fewer than half of the flawed
steps inside correct answers (\autoref{appendix:validation}), the precision varies across models,
and the arithmetic check reads more calculations where a model writes more arithmetic out.
Coverage does not rise with the amount a model writes: Spearman's $\rho$~\citep{spearman1904}
between a template's mean coverage and its mean number of steps is $-0.11$ to $-0.26$ for every
model.

\paragraph{Difficulty Level and Depth.} \autoref{tab:level_gap} gives every model's level means,
its Easy-minus-Advanced gap with its tests, and the share of responses scored 0 by the number of
milestones in the gold trace.
We read Welch's test, since the permutation of level labels assumes equal spread across levels and
the Advanced level, with fewer templates, varies more; under that permutation the gap holds for
nine models.
Over the responses with a readable answer, \texttt{GLM-5.3}'s gap is $+0.010$ (Holm-adjusted Welch
$p$ 0.587), so its gap as scored is mostly an output-ceiling effect.
The share scored 0 counts empty responses at the output ceiling as well as wrong answers.
