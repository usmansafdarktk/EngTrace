\section{Full Results}
\label{appendix:results}

This section holds the tests and measures behind~\autoref{subsec:overall_performance}
and~\autoref{subsec:difficulty_level}.
Intervals resample templates, every family of tests is Holm-corrected, and a difference that does
not hold comes with the smallest difference the design detects at 80\% power
(\autoref{appendix:protocol}).

\paragraph{Pairwise Comparisons.} Of the 55 pairs, 38 differ on FAC after Holm correction under the
sign-flip test over templates: inside the top five, only \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} from
\texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}; \texttt{GLM-5.3} from \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash},
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{Kimi K3}, and \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}; each of the lower five from
every model of the top six; and inside the lower five, \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} from
\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, and \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}.
Every non-significant FAC difference lies below what its pair detects (0.013 to 0.064).
On MC, 28 pairs differ: the three highest models, \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash},
and \texttt{Kimi K3}, do not separate, while \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} and
\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, which FAC does not separate, do; Kendall's
$\tau$~\citep{kendall1938} between the two orderings is 0.782 (95\% interval 0.564 to 0.891).
Strict FAC, which scores a partial answer 0, separates 34 pairs and agrees with FAC on 51 of the 55;
a Wilcoxon test~\citep{wilcoxon1945} separates 29 on MC; and McNemar's exact
test~\citep{mcnemar1947}, which treats the 2,250 instances as independent, separates 45.

\paragraph{Matched Settings.} \autoref{tab:matched} gives FAC at the providers' defaults and at
matched settings, where \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, and
\texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} reason at medium effort (\autoref{appendix:decoding}), each with the tier
letters of its own family of pairwise tests.
At matched settings the five highest models are \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash},
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{Kimi K3}, \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}, and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, and
Kendall's $\tau$ between the orderings at the providers' defaults and at matched settings is 0.709.
Reasoning raises all three re-run models, and none of the three changes is bounded within the
margin of~\autoref{appendix:protocol}; \autoref{appendix:experiments} reads them beside the
reasoning-effort experiment.
The expert readings of wrong answers (\autoref{appendix:error_analysis}) are of the responses at the
providers' defaults, so they do not describe the three models at matched settings.

\paragraph{Consistency Within a Template.} \autoref{tab:single_path} counts, per model, the
templates whose instances it solves on all, on some, and on none, separately for the templates whose
instances all follow one derivation and for the others.
No model fails every instance of more than a few templates, and each lower-tier model solves some
but not all instances of more single-path templates than any top-six model; the table shows how
consistent a model is within a template, not why.

\paragraph{Scoring Rules.} Halving the answer tolerance swaps no pair of models and doubling it
swaps one, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} and \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and no swapped pair differs
after correction ($\tau = 1.000$ and $0.964$ against the ordering as scored).
Requiring a correct rounding at the displayed precision rather than accepting one unit either way,
crediting a requested quantity stated in the body but not on the answer line, and leaving out the
four templates answerable from their wording or the nine with symbolic answers move no model's FAC
by more than 0.033 and keep $\tau$ at 0.964 or above.
The ordering changes most when only readable answers count ($\tau = 0.709$), chiefly because
\texttt{GLM-5.3}'s 99 empty responses then drop out.
\autoref{tab:scoring_variants} changes one clause of the final-answer rule at a time, and
\autoref{appendix:scoring} reads it.

\paragraph{Coverage and What Marks a Wrong Answer.} \autoref{tab:coverage} gives, per model,
coverage on the wrong answers against its chance floor, the share of wrong answers with a milestone
the judge rules missing, the share of milestones the judge decides, and the precision of the
arithmetic flags of~\autoref{tab:main_results}.
The flag rates do not rank the models: the two checks together recall fewer than half of the flawed
steps inside correct answers (\autoref{appendix:validation}), the precision varies across models,
and the arithmetic check reads more calculations where a model writes more arithmetic out.
Coverage does not rise with the amount a model writes.
Spearman's $\rho$~\citep{spearman1904} between a template's mean coverage and its mean number of
steps is $-0.09$ to $-0.26$ for every model; the slope of MC on the number of values a response
displays is $+0.0015$ (MC per 10 numeric values shown; 95\% interval 0.000 to 0.003) with template
fixed effects, and within models Spearman's $\rho$ runs from $-0.24$ to $-0.07$.

\autoref{tab:coverage_variants} computes MC under four definitions.
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} and \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} differ after Holm's correction as scored
(Holm-adjusted $p$ 0.049), by matching alone (0.001), and on intermediate milestones only (0.027),
but not under route-adjusted coverage (1.000), where the milestones the judge rules not needed leave
the denominator: what separates them is how closely a response follows the gold derivation's route,
not how far it gets.
Their MC gap as scored, $+0.023$, is of the size of the judge's own variation: re-judging a sample
with a second judge shifts a model's coverage by $-0.018$ to $+0.019$
(\autoref{appendix:validation}).
\autoref{tab:judged_steps} gives the judged step flags, whose precision~\autoref{appendix:readings}
reports, and~\autoref{tab:flag_precision} classifies every arithmetic flag on a correct answer: 11
of the 3,409 (0.3\%) are carried precision, a printed result that is a correct rounding of the
value recomputed from the unrounded values earlier in the response, and none of the 171 slips the
domain expert confirmed is.

\paragraph{Difficulty Level and Depth.} \autoref{tab:level_gap} gives every model's level means,
its Easy-minus-Advanced gap with its tests, and the share of responses scored 0 by the number of
milestones in the gold trace.
We read Welch's test, since the permutation of level labels assumes equal spread across levels and
the Advanced level, with fewer templates, varies more for nine of the eleven models; under that
permutation the gap holds for six models.
Welch's $p$ in~\autoref{tab:level_gap} is Holm-adjusted over the eleven models.
Over the responses with a readable answer, \texttt{GLM-5.3}'s gap is $-0.004$ (Holm-adjusted Welch
$p$ 1.000), so its gap as scored comes from its empty responses at the output ceiling.
The share scored 0 counts empty responses at the output ceiling as well as wrong answers.
\autoref{tab:depth_model} therefore relates a wrong answer to the depth of the gold derivation over
readable responses only, with the answer kind as a covariate: the slope of the wrong-answer rate on
the milestone count holds after Holm correction for five of the eleven models over readable
responses, the four weakest among them, and for the other six it does not hold after correction.
