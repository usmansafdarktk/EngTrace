\section{Full Results}
\label{appendix:results}

This section holds the tests and measures behind~\autoref{subsec:overall_performance}
and~\autoref{subsec:difficulty_level}.
Intervals resample templates, every family of tests is Holm-corrected, and a difference that does
not hold comes with the smallest difference the design detects at 80\% power
(\autoref{appendix:protocol}).

\paragraph{Pairwise Comparisons.} Of the 55 pairs, 36 differ on FAC after Holm correction under the
sign-flip test over templates: inside the first five, \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} from
\texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}, \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} from \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, and
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} from \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}; \texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B} from none of them;
27 of the 30 pairs between the first six and the last five; and inside the last five,
\texttt{GLM-5.3} from \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, \texttt{GLM-5.3} from \texttt{gpt-oss-20b},
\texttt{Gemma 4 26B} from \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B} from \texttt{gpt-oss-20b},
\texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} from \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, and \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} from
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}.
Every non-significant FAC difference lies below what its pair detects (0.013 to 0.039).
On MC, 15 pairs differ, each of them also on FAC: the three highest models,
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}, and \texttt{Kimi K3}, do not separate, nor do
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} and \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, which FAC does not separate either;
Kendall's $\tau$~\citep{kendall1938} between the two orderings is 0.709 (95\% interval 0.345 to
0.818).
Strict FAC, which scores a partial answer 0, separates 29 pairs and agrees with FAC on 48 of the 55;
a Wilcoxon test~\citep{wilcoxon1945} separates 17 on MC; and McNemar's exact
test~\citep{mcnemar1947}, which treats the 2,250 instances as independent, separates 45.

\paragraph{Matched Settings.} \autoref{tab:matched} gives FAC at matched settings, the configuration
reported throughout, where \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, and
\texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} reason at medium effort (\autoref{appendix:decoding}), and at the providers'
defaults, each with the tier letters of its own family of pairwise tests.
At the providers' defaults, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and
\texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} return no reasoning tokens and score 0.852, 0.872, and 0.878; the
five highest models are \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{Kimi K3},
\texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}, and \texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}, and Kendall's $\tau$ between the orderings at
the providers' defaults and at matched settings is 0.709.
Reasoning raises all three models run with it (Holm-adjusted $p$ $<$0.001), and for
\texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} and \texttt{Gemma 4 26B} the 90\% interval lies wholly above $+0.05$, the
margin of~\autoref{appendix:protocol}; \autoref{appendix:experiments} reads them beside the
reasoning-effort experiment.
The expert readings of wrong answers (\autoref{appendix:error_analysis}) are of the responses at the
providers' defaults, so they do not describe the three models at matched settings.

\paragraph{Consistency Within a Template.} \autoref{tab:single_path} counts, per model, the
templates whose instances it solves on all, on some, and on none, separately for the templates whose
instances all follow one derivation and for the others.
No model fails every instance of more than a few templates, and the two lowest models,
\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, solve some but not all instances of more
single-path templates than any other model; the table shows how consistent a model is within a
template, not why.

\paragraph{Scoring Rules.} Halving the answer tolerance swaps one pair of models and doubling it
swaps two, and no swapped pair differs after correction ($\tau = 0.964$ and $0.927$ against the
ordering as scored).
Requiring a correct rounding at the displayed precision rather than accepting one unit either way,
crediting a requested quantity stated in the body but not on the answer line, and leaving out the
four templates answerable from their wording or the nine with symbolic answers move no model's FAC
by more than 0.028 and keep $\tau$ at 0.964 or above.
The ordering changes most when only readable answers count ($\tau = 0.636$), chiefly because
\texttt{GLM-5.3}'s 99 empty responses then drop out.
\autoref{tab:scoring_variants} changes one clause of the final-answer rule at a time, and
\autoref{appendix:scoring} reads it.

\paragraph{Coverage and What Marks a Wrong Answer.} \autoref{tab:coverage} gives, per model,
coverage on the wrong answers against its chance floor, the share of wrong answers with a milestone
the judge rules missing, the share of milestones the judge decides, and the precision of the
arithmetic flags of~\autoref{tab:main_results}.
The flag rates do not rank the models: the two checks together recall fewer than half of the flawed
steps inside correct answers (\autoref{appendix:validation}), the precision varies across models,
and the arithmetic check reads more calculations where a model writes more arithmetic out.
Coverage rises by at most 0.004 (MC per 10 numeric values shown) with the amount a model writes.
Spearman's $\rho$~\citep{spearman1904} between a template's mean coverage and its mean number of
steps is $-0.09$ to $-0.28$ for every model; the slope of MC on the number of values a response
displays is $+0.0020$ (MC per 10 numeric values shown; 95\% interval 0.0005 to 0.0041) with template
fixed effects, and within models Spearman's $\rho$ runs from $-0.21$ to $-0.07$.

\autoref{tab:coverage_variants} computes MC under four definitions.
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} and \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} differ after Holm's correction by matching
alone (Holm-adjusted $p$ 0.001) and on intermediate milestones only (0.032), but not as scored
(0.069) or under route-adjusted coverage (1.000), where the milestones the judge rules not needed
leave the denominator: what separates them is how closely a response follows the gold derivation's
route, not whether it reaches the quantities its own route needs, a reading that rests on the
judge's not-needed rulings, which the experts did not read.
Matching alone, which the judge does not touch, separates them ($+0.042$, Holm-adjusted $p$
0.001); re-judging a sample with a second judge shifts a model's coverage by $-0.018$ to $+0.019$
(\autoref{appendix:validation}).
The judge adds 0.019 to 0.045 to a model's coverage over matching alone, and matching alone
separates more pairs (20 against 15 of the 55).
\autoref{tab:judged_steps} gives the judged step flags, whose precision on the expert study
\autoref{tab:validation_results} reports, and~\autoref{tab:flag_precision} classifies every
arithmetic flag on a correct answer: 12
of the 3,077 (0.4\%) are carried precision, a printed result that is a correct rounding of the
value recomputed from the unrounded values earlier in the response, and none of the 171 slips the
domain expert confirmed is.

\paragraph{Difficulty Level and Depth.} \autoref{tab:level_gap} gives every model's level means,
its Easy-minus-Advanced gap with its tests, and the share of responses scored 0 by the number of
milestones in the gold trace.
We read Welch's test, since the permutation of level labels assumes equal spread across levels and
the Advanced level, with fewer templates, varies more for nine of the eleven models; under that
permutation the gap holds for four models.
Welch's $p$ in~\autoref{tab:level_gap} is Holm-adjusted over the eleven models.
Over the responses with a readable answer, \texttt{GLM-5.3}'s gap is $-0.004$ (Holm-adjusted Welch
$p$ 1.000), so its gap as scored comes from its empty responses at the output ceiling.
The share scored 0 counts empty responses at the output ceiling as well as wrong answers.
\autoref{tab:depth_model} therefore relates a wrong answer to the depth of the gold derivation over
readable responses only, with the answer kind as a covariate.
Pooled over the models, each further milestone multiplies the odds of a wrong answer by 1.21 (95\%
interval 1.09 to 1.33); per model, the slope of the wrong-answer rate on the milestone count holds
after Holm correction for three of the eleven models over readable responses,
\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, and for the
others it does not hold after correction.
