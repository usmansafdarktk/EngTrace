\section{Difficulty Labeling}
\label{sec:appendix_difficulty}

Three domain experts of the template's branch set its difficulty level; one is its author and the
other two did not write it.
Each expert first scores the template on their own, from 1 to 3 on conceptual complexity,
mathematical sophistication, and procedural depth, counting only what the solver must supply: a
formula or method that the question states does not count.
Each dimension takes the median of the three scores, and the sum of the three medians sets the
level: 3--4 Easy, 5--6 Intermediate, and 7--9 Advanced.
When a template's instances follow several reasoning paths, the experts score each path, and the
template takes the median level of its paths.
After the experts have scored, the panel of~\autoref{sec:appendix_domain_validation} rates every
template with the prompt below as a second check; a template whose panel level differs from the
experts' level goes back to its three experts for review, and the panel sets no level itself.
The experts fix the levels before any evaluated model runs and never change them on the basis of
evaluation results.

\begin{tcolorbox}[
    colback=gray!5,
    colframe=gray!40,
    boxrule=0.4pt,
    arc=2pt,
    left=6pt,
    right=6pt,
    top=6pt,
    bottom=6pt,
    breakable
]
\ttfamily\small
You are an expert academic and senior engineering educator at a top-tier engineering university (like MIT or Caltech). You have decades of experience designing undergraduate courses and examinations for ABET-accredited programs. \\

Your task is to rate the difficulty of an engineering problem template for a student who has just completed the core undergraduate course in its domain. Rate only what the student must supply: if the question states a formula, method, or principle, do not count it. \\

Rate the template on three dimensions, each on a scale of 1 to 3: \\

Conceptual complexity: \\
1 = one principle, applied directly \\
2 = several principles from one domain, or one modelling choice (e.g., flow regime, configuration, control volume) \\
3 = principles from several domains, or assumptions the student must choose and justify \\

Mathematical sophistication: \\
1 = direct substitution into formulas (arithmetic, powers, roots, logarithms) \\
2 = rearrangement, simultaneous or nonlinear equations, or probability beyond a single formula \\
3 = calculus, differential equations, or an iterative or numerical solution \\

Procedural depth: \\
1 = 1-2 dependent intermediate quantities \\
2 = 3-5 dependent intermediate quantities \\
3 = 6 or more dependent intermediate quantities \\

Sum the three scores and assign a level: 3-4 = Easy, 5-6 = Intermediate, 7-9 = Advanced. \\

If the instances follow different reasoning paths, rate each path separately. \\

Please evaluate the following template: \\

\paragraph{Variation Within a Template.}
We measure how far the 15 evaluation-set instances of a template differ in three respects, each with
numbers masked (\autoref{tab:template_variation}): the question wording, in which a different
material or fluid counts as a different wording; the reasoning path, that is, the lines of the gold
trace that contain an equation, in order, with the names that vary between questions also masked;
and the form of the answer.
In 92 of the 150 templates, the instances follow two or more reasoning paths, and the other 58
follow a single path (chemical 18, electrical 5, civil 14, industrial 10, mechanical 11).
The same 92 templates show more than one path in 500 further draws, so the evaluation set misses no
template's second path; counting a name change inside an equation as a different path raises the
count to 96 templates.
Two instances of a template are near-duplicates when their questions share the wording and every
number lies within 5\% of its counterpart; the evaluation set holds 61 such pairs, in 14 templates.

\paragraph{Drawing the Evaluation Set.}
For each template, we generate its first 100 draws from the private 128-bit seed, 15,000 draws in
all, and skip 549 repeated questions, 35 rounding ties (instances whose gold trace prints a value
lying exactly halfway between two roundings), and one question already used to validate the
evaluator, so 14,415 remain.
We group the remaining draws of a template by reasoning path and answer form and take the 15
instances round-robin across the groups, in order of first appearance, so that no group is repeated
before every group has been used.
101 templates have more than one group, and 18 templates have more groups than their 15 instances
can cover.
All 2,250 questions are distinct.
We publish the SHA-256 hash of every instance and of the seed, and will release the seed at
publication so that anyone can regenerate the evaluation set and check it against the hashes.
