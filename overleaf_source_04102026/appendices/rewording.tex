\section{Robustness to Rewording}
\label{appendix:rewording}

This section supports the Rewording paragraph of~\autoref{subsec:results}: the design of the test,
the scripted and expert checks, and the results in full.

\paragraph{Design.}
Fixed template wording could let a model recognize a problem type and reproduce a memorized method,
so we test whether the wording alone moves the scores.
For every template we select three of its 15 instances (450 instances) and rewrite each once with an
LLM from a family outside both the evaluated models and the judge, under rules that keep every
number, unit, symbol, formula, acronym, and labeled part in place and change only the wording.
A script checks each rewrite (the numbers as a multiset, every technical token, the part labels, a
word similarity to the original of at most 0.75, the length, and no preamble), restores the
original's notation where the writer used symbols the original lacks, and gives the writer three
attempts.
A domain expert of the instance's branch then reads each passing pair and keeps it only if the
rewrite is the same problem with the same answer and adds nothing; the experts rejected 12\% of the
pairs that passed every scripted check, almost always for a dropped or altered qualifier that
changes the physics or the asked quantity, such as whether a shaft is solid or a flow is uniform.
The eleven models answer the paraphrases under the main run's prompt, settings, and scoring, on the
same served checkpoints, against the original instances' gold answers and milestones.
\autoref{tab:rewording_funnel} gives the funnel from the 450 instances to the 277 kept pairs on 115
templates: the 29 templates with no passing rewrite are formula-bound ones whose technical tokens
the writer could not keep in place, and six more lost their last kept pair at the expert step, so
the test covers the templates that can be reworded without loss, and industrial engineering is the
least covered branch.

\paragraph{Results.}
\autoref{tab:rewording_results} gives, per model, the paired change paraphrase minus original in FAC
and in MC with and without the judge, with 95\% intervals, the Holm-adjusted $p$ of the sign-flip
test over templates, the smallest change the design detects, and the 90\% interval against the
$\pm 0.05$ margin, which equals the paired difference the design was planned to detect and was fixed
after the point estimates were known.
No change is significant after correction; ten of the eleven 90\% intervals lie within the margin,
and \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}'s reaches $-0.067$.
Two 90\% intervals lie wholly below zero (\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} and \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}) and
one wholly above (\texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}): small changes the data favor within the bound, which
the corrected tests do not support, and which we do not call findings.
Kendall's $\tau$ between the models' ordering on the originals and on the paraphrases is 0.673 (95\%
CI 0.455 to 0.881).
Sampling noise alone, measured by splitting each template's kept pairs into two random halves and
ordering the models on each, gives a median $\tau$ of 0.782 (quartiles 0.721 to 0.855, 5th
percentile 0.624), so the observed agreement sits at the lower edge of the noise distribution: the
tiers hold, and the order within the top tier is not resolved by this test.
\autoref{tab:rewording_repeats} sets the rewording change beside run-to-run decoding noise for the
four models with decoding repeats; the rewording change lies within the repeats' spread for three of
them and beyond it for \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}.
