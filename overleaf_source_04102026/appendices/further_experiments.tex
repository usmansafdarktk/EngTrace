\section{Further Experiments: Reasoning Effort, Flagship Anchors, Open Book, and Open Tool}
\label{appendix:experiments}

Four experiments extend the evaluation, each answering one question it leaves open, and
\autoref{subsec:reasoning_equations_tools} reads them.
Each runs on the originals of the 450-instance subset (three instances per template) with the
evaluation's prompt, output ceiling, and scoring, and changes one thing.
We pair each experiment with the evaluation's responses on the same instances
(\autoref{tab:experiments}) and read the 90\% interval against the $\pm 0.05$ margin, beside a
sign-flip test over templates, Holm-corrected within the experiment, and the smallest change the
experiment detects at 80\% power.

\paragraph{Reasoning Effort.} Do the two closed models that return no reasoning tokens at their
providers' defaults, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} and \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, score differently
when they reason?
Each answers the subset again with the serving API's reasoning-effort parameter set to medium and
nothing else changed.
The setting lifts \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} beyond the margin on FAC and on MC and lowers its
arithmetic flags on correct answers, with its largest point change on Advanced templates, whose
level intervals overlap those of the other levels; it moves \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} by less
than the experiment detects.
Where the setting works, it changes the derivation and not only the answer: more milestones
stated, fewer slips behind correct answers.
One of the two models moves, so the setting does not close the lower tier's gap in general.

\paragraph{Flagship Anchors.} Does a flagship exceed the top tier, or is the ceiling an artifact of
a roster without one?
\texttt{GPT-5.4} and \texttt{DeepSeek V4 Pro}, the two flagships that pass the selection rule
(\autoref{appendix:models}), answer the same subset at their providers' defaults, and
\texttt{GPT-5.4}, whose default returns no reasoning tokens, also with reasoning at medium effort;
an anchor is a reference point outside the pairwise tests, so no test is run against it.
Neither exceeds the top tier: \texttt{DeepSeek V4 Pro} scores 0.968 and \texttt{GPT-5.4} 0.941 at
its default and 0.980 with reasoning, against 0.964 to 0.976 for the top five on the same instances,
whose intervals contain both reasoning anchors; on Advanced templates the anchors score 0.87 to 0.95
and the top five 0.92 to 0.95.
The ceiling is therefore not for want of a flagship.
The one paired test among the anchors, \texttt{GPT-5.4} with reasoning against its default, is the
last row of~\autoref{tab:experiments} and shows the reasoning-effort result again.

\paragraph{Open Book.} Does recall of the governing equations limit the scores?
For the 135 templates whose code states their governing equations, the question carries them as a
handbook block, one per line, with no data tables or constants (405 instances); one top-tier model
and two lower-tier ones, one closed and one open-weights, answer: \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5},
\texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}.
The equations lift \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, partly because fewer of its responses run out of room
(the change on the 388 instances it answered in both runs is $+0.043$), and leave the two closed
models within the margin, while MC rises for all three, consistent with the equations prompting the
responses to state the intermediates.
Recall of the equations is thus part of what bounds the weakest model and no part of what bounds
the closed ones.
The experiment supplies equations and no data tables, so it bounds what formula recall adds and not
what a property lookup would.

\paragraph{Open Tool.} Does arithmetic execution limit the scores?
The model may call a Python interpreter with NumPy, SciPy, and SymPy up to 8 times before one final
turn without it; each script runs isolated, with no credentials, a 20 s limit, and its output
truncated at 4,000 characters, behind a filter that refuses file-system, network, process, and
introspection access.
The two closed models answer, since no endpoint that meets the selection rule serves
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}'s tool calls.
The interpreter changes neither model's FAC or MC beyond the margin, though
\texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}'s arithmetic flags on correct answers fall.
Whether to call it is the model's choice, \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} on 66\% of the instances and
\texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} on 19\%, and both score lower on the instances where they call it than where
they do not: the models reach for the tool on the problems they find harder, and the experiment
bounds what an offered tool adds, not what required use would.
No experiment says where the remaining errors lie; that is the question of the expert reading
in~\autoref{subsec:error_analysis}.
