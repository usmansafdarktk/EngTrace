\section{Further Experiments: Reasoning Effort, Flagship Anchors, Open Book, and Open Tool}
\label{appendix:experiments}

Four experiments extend the evaluation, each answering one question it leaves open, and
\autoref{subsec:reasoning_equations_tools} reads them.
Each runs on the originals of the 450-instance subset (three instances per template) with the
evaluation's prompt, output ceiling, and scoring, and changes one thing.
Where a model answers both with and without the change, we pair its two responses to each instance
(\autoref{tab:experiments}) and read the 90\% interval against the $\pm 0.05$ margin, beside a
sign-flip test over templates, Holm-corrected within the experiment, and the smallest change the
experiment detects at 80\% power.
Apart from that one change, the experiments run at the providers' defaults, as do the responses
they are paired with.

\paragraph{Reasoning Effort.} Do the two closed models that return no reasoning tokens at their
providers' defaults, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} and \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, score differently
when they reason?
\texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} does, in its derivation as well as its answer, and
\texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} changes by less than the experiment detects.
Each answers the subset with the serving API's reasoning-effort parameter set to medium and nothing
else changed.
The setting lifts \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}'s FAC beyond the margin, raises its MC by 0.057 (95\%
interval 0.031 to 0.085), and lowers its arithmetic flags on correct answers; its largest point
change in FAC is on Advanced templates, though the intervals of the three levels overlap.
Over all 2,250 instances, the matched settings (\autoref{appendix:decoding}) give the same
comparison for these two models and \texttt{Gemma 4 26B} (\autoref{tab:matched}).
There, the FAC gain of \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} holds, though it is smaller than the subset
experiment detects.
The derivation changes chiefly for \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, whose MC rises by about as much as on the
subset, while that of the other two moves by at most 0.016.

\paragraph{Flagship Anchors.} Does a flagship exceed the first five, or is the ceiling an artifact
of evaluating none?
The flagships do not score clearly above the first five.
\texttt{GPT-5.4} and \texttt{DeepSeek V4 Pro}, the two flagships that pass the selection rule
(\autoref{appendix:models}), answer the same subset at their providers' defaults, and
\texttt{GPT-5.4}, whose default returns no reasoning tokens, also with reasoning at medium effort;
an anchor is a reference point outside the pairwise tests, so no test compares it with the
evaluated models.
\texttt{DeepSeek V4 Pro} scores 0.968 and \texttt{GPT-5.4} 0.951 at its default and 0.997 with
reasoning, against 0.967 to 0.990 for the first five on the same instances; the highest anchor
run lies inside the interval of \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, and on Advanced templates the anchors
score 0.89 to 1.00 and the first five 0.94 to 0.99.
Reasoning lifts a flagship as it lifts \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}: with it, \texttt{GPT-5.4} gains over
its own default and sheds most of its arithmetic flags (\autoref{tab:experiments}).

\paragraph{Open Book.} Does recall of the governing equations limit the scores?
Recall is part of what limits the weakest model, \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, and supplying the equations
leaves \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} within the margin.
For the 135 templates whose code states their governing equations, the question carries them as a
handbook block, one per line, with no data tables or constants (405 instances); three models
answer: \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} from the first five, and the closed \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} and the
open-weights \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, which score lower at the providers' defaults.
The equations lift \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, partly because fewer of its responses run out of room (the
change on the 388 instances it answered in both runs is $+0.044$), and MC rises for all three,
consistent with the equations prompting the responses to state the intermediates.
The experiment supplies equations and no data tables, so it bounds what formula recall adds and not
what a property lookup would.

\paragraph{Open Tool.} Does arithmetic execution limit the scores?
An offered interpreter moves neither \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} nor \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} beyond the
margin in FAC or MC, though \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}'s arithmetic flags on correct answers fall.
The model may call a Python interpreter with NumPy, SciPy, and SymPy up to 8 times before one final
turn without it; each script runs isolated, with no credentials, a 20 s limit, and its output
truncated at 4,000 characters, behind a filter that refuses file-system, network, process, and
introspection access.
Only these two closed models answer, since no endpoint that meets the selection rule serves
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}'s tool calls.
Whether to call the interpreter is the model's choice, \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} on 66\% of the
instances and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} on 18\%, so the experiment bounds what an offered tool adds, not
what required use would.
