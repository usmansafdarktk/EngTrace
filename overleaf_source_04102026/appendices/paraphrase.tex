\section{Paraphrase Test}
\label{appendix:paraphrase}

Because every instance of a template shares its wording, a model could recognize a problem from the
wording alone and reproduce a method it has memorized for it.
This section gives the design and the results of the paraphrase test
of~\autoref{subsec:overall_performance}.

\paragraph{Design.} We select three of every template's 15 instances (450 instances) and paraphrase
each once with \texttt{Mistral Large 3}~\citep{mistral2025mistral3} (the writer), an LLM from a
family outside both the evaluated models and the judge, keeping every number, unit, symbol,
formula, acronym, and labeled part in place.
A paraphrase must pass two checks:
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Scripted:} the numbers as a multiset, every technical token, the part labels, a word
similarity to the original of at most 0.75, the length, and no preamble, with three attempts per
instance and the original's notation restored where the writer used symbols the original lacks.
\item \emph{Expert:} a domain expert of the instance's branch keeps a pair only if it is the same
problem with the same answer and adds nothing.
\end{itemize}
Of the 450 instances, 314 pass the scripted checks and the domain experts keep 275 pairs on 114
templates; the templates left out are mostly formula-bound ones whose technical tokens the writer
does not keep in place.
The expert step is needed: the experts reject 39 pairs that pass every scripted check, mostly for
a dropped or altered qualifier that changes the physics or the asked quantity.
The eleven models answer the kept paraphrases under the evaluation's prompt, settings, and scoring.

\paragraph{Results.} On the 275 kept pairs, no change in FAC holds after correction.
Read against the $\pm 0.05$ margin, the 90\% interval lies within it for every model but
\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, the two models whose paired differences the
design measures least precisely: the smallest difference it detects at 80\% power is 0.052 and
0.079 for them and at most 0.044 for the others (\autoref{tab:paraphrase}).
MC changes by at most 0.023 in either direction, so the derivation is at least as robust to the
wording as the answer.
The paraphrases reorder the models within the range sampling noise produces, though at its high end:
their ordering agrees with the originals' at Kendall's $\tau$ 0.722 (95\% interval 0.449 to 0.844),
while two disjoint draws of 275 original instances with the same template mix agree with a median
$\tau$ of 0.807 (quartiles 0.748 to 0.860; 5th percentile 0.647).
For the same two models, the paraphrase change also exceeds decoding noise: of the four models
decoded three more times on 300 instances (\texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite},
\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}), FAC varies by a standard deviation of 0.003 to
0.008 across repeats, and the paraphrase change lies beyond the spread of the repeats' changes for
\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} and within it for the other two.
The test bounds memorized wording, not memorized methods, since the numbers and the problem
structure are kept, and it covers only the 114 templates that can be paraphrased without loss.
