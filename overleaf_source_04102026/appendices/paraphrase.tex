\section{Paraphrase Test}
\label{appendix:paraphrase}

Because every instance of a template shares its wording, a model could recognize a problem from the
wording alone and reproduce a method it has memorized for it.
This section gives the design and the results of the paraphrase test
of~\autoref{subsec:overall_performance}, which bounds how much the wording is worth.

\paragraph{Design.} We select three of every template's 15 instances (450 instances) and paraphrase
each once with \texttt{Mistral Large 3}~\citep{mistral2025mistral3}, an LLM from a family outside
both the evaluated models and the judge (the writer), keeping every number, unit, symbol, formula,
acronym, and labeled part in place.
A paraphrase must pass two checks:
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Scripted:} the numbers as a multiset, every technical token, the part labels, a word
similarity to the original of at most 0.75, the length, and no preamble, with three attempts per
instance and the original's notation restored where the writer used symbols the original lacks.
\item \emph{Expert:} a domain expert of the instance's branch keeps a pair only if it is the same
problem with the same answer and adds nothing.
\end{itemize}
Of the 450 instances, 314 pass the scripted checks and the domain experts keep 275 pairs on 114
templates; most rejections are for a dropped or altered qualifier that changes the physics or the
asked quantity, which the scripted checks had passed, and the templates left out are mostly
formula-bound ones whose technical tokens the writer does not keep in place.
The eleven models answer the kept paraphrases under the evaluation's prompt, settings, and scoring.

\paragraph{Results.} On the 275 kept pairs, no change in FAC holds after correction.
Read against the $\pm 0.05$ margin, which equals the paired difference the design detects at 80\%
power, the 90\% interval lies within it for every model but \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} and
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b} (\autoref{tab:paraphrase}).
Four 90\% intervals lie wholly on one side of zero (\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash},
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, and \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} below, \texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B} above):
small changes the data lean toward but the corrected test does not support.
The models' ordering on the paraphrases agrees with their ordering on the originals with Kendall's
$\tau$ 0.807 (95\% interval 0.547 to 0.844), which is what sampling noise alone gives, since two
random halves of the same pairs agree with a median $\tau$ of 0.807 (quartiles 0.754 to 0.860).
For the four models decoded three more times on 300 instances (\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507},
\texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}), FAC varies by a
standard deviation of 0.003 to 0.016 across repeats, and the paraphrase change lies within the
spread of the repeats' changes for two of the four and beyond it for \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} and
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}.
The test bounds memorized wording, not memorized methods, since the numbers and the problem
structure are kept, and it covers the 114 templates that can be paraphrased without loss, which are
the less formula-bound ones.
