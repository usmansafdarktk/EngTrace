\section{Validation Against Expert Judgment}
\label{appendix:validation}

This section details the expert study behind~\autoref{sec:evaluation} and the domain experts'
readings of the evaluated models' responses; \autoref{tab:validation_results} collects the results.

\subsection{Expert Study}

The study draws four instances from each of 15 templates, 60 in all, one template per branch and
level: within each branch and level it takes the template whose answer kind is least represented so
far, breaking ties by a seeded hash, and it leaves out the four templates whose answers can be read
off the question's wording (\autoref{appendix:error_analysis}).
This rule favors symbolic, vector, and multipart answers, so only 3 of its templates have a single
scalar answer, against 89 of the 150, which makes it a hard test of answer checking.
Five LLMs outside the evaluated models (\texttt{GPT-5}~\citep{gpt5},
\texttt{Claude Opus 4.7}~\citep{claudeopus47}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Pro}~\citep{gemini31pro},
\texttt{DeepSeek-R1}~\citep{guo2025deepseekr1}, and
\texttt{Llama 3.1 70B}~\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}) answer each instance once, giving 300
responses.
Fifteen domain experts, three per branch, label each response of their branch behind an opaque code:
every step (correct, alternative correct, incorrect with an error type, or not a claim), every
milestone, the final answer (correct, partial, incorrect, or not stated), and the soundness of the
reasoning.
Three rounds follow: a written reason for each of the 1,042 incorrect step labels, a blind
re-labeling of seven responses per expert, and a blind adjudication of the 255 distinct steps on
which experts split.
The experts agree at Fleiss'~$\kappa$~\citep{fleiss1971} 0.781 on steps, 0.880 on milestones, and
0.966 on final answers, and an expert's two passes at Cohen's~$\kappa$~\citep{cohen1960} 0.828.

The study both guides and validates the evaluator.
Only $\epsilon$ is cross-fitted, fitted on one half of the responses and tested on the other; the
other settings of~\autoref{tab:scoring_settings} are compared with their alternatives on all 300
responses, so the agreement figures of~\autoref{tab:validation_results} are not an estimate on
unseen responses.
The experts' readings of the evaluated models' responses (\autoref{appendix:readings}) test the
evaluator on responses it was not designed on.

\subsection{Evaluator Designs}

We compare six designs on these labels.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Step matching with in-family judges}: matches response steps to gold steps by
    numerical agreement and semantic similarity, sends unmatched steps to a panel of \texttt{GPT-5},
    \texttt{Claude Opus 4.5}~\citep{claudeopus45}, and \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Pro} when the answer is
    correct but fewer than 80\% of the gold steps match and for a random fifth of wrong answers, and
    scores an F1 over the steps; we run it with two and with three judges voting.
    The panel's Claude judge is \texttt{Claude Opus 4.5}, a different model from the study's
    \texttt{Claude Opus 4.7} but of the same family, which is what makes the panel in-family.
    \item \textbf{Step matching with outside judges}: the same, with \texttt{Grok 4.6},
    \texttt{MiniMax M3}, and \texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro}.
    \item \textbf{Process reward models}: three open-weights models that assign each step a reward,
    with a step below 0.5 counted as incorrect (\texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B},
    \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B}~\citep{zhang2025prmlessons}, and
    \texttt{VersaPRM}~\citep{zeng2025versaprm}).
    The threshold of 0.5 is the models' default: choosing it on one half of the responses and
    testing on the other changes step F1 by $-0.006$, and inside correct answers no threshold
    reaches precision 0.80 on the held-out half.
    \item \textbf{Milestone matching alone}: credits each gold milestone that the response states
    within 0.5\% under a unit factor, in any step and order (\autoref{sec:evaluation}), with no
    judge.
    \item \textbf{Milestone matching with the arithmetic check}: milestone matching plus the
    arithmetic check, which recomputes each displayed calculation it can parse and flags a result
    that is not a correct rounding at its displayed precision.
    \item \textbf{Milestone matching with the judge}: milestone matching, with
    \texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro} ruling each milestone that matching misses reached, not needed, or
    missing, and only reached credited.
\end{itemize}
Our evaluator combines the last three with the final-answer check and the judged step check.

\paragraph{Main Findings.} Whether the answer is right nearly decides the experts' judgment of a
response's soundness (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, AUROC, 0.974), so
the soundness judgment cannot tell reasoning evaluators apart, and we compare them on steps and
milestones, where matching with the judge agrees best with the experts' milestone labels.
Of the 178 incorrect steps behind correct answers, 175 are calculation slips, which rarely change
milestone coverage; the arithmetic check is there to find them.
The best reward model ranks steps well (AUROC 0.825), but three of four of its flags inside correct
answers are false, and outside judges barely move the step-matching score (0.379 with two in-family
judges, 0.378 with three outside ones).

\subsection{Planted Defects}

Because the labels hold almost no conceptual error behind a correct answer, we plant one defect in
each of 120 clean responses, verified without the checker under test: 60 one-digit slips in a
displayed intermediate value and 60 conceptual defects that misstate a criterion, rule, or formula
without changing any digit.
Each LLM judge sees the planted step and, separately, the same step untouched, with the step prompt
of~\autoref{appendix:scoring}, and a defect counts as caught only when the judge flags the planted
step alone.
None of our deterministic checks flags a conceptual defect, and the four judges label at most about
a third of them an error, without false alarms (up to 33 of 60 when ``Other'' labels count); the
judged step check, which sends every unflagged step, catches more than step matching, whose panel
sees a conceptual defect no more often than the same step untouched.

\subsection{Judge Independence}

We use \texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro} because it shares no family with an evaluated model, as LLM judges can
favor their own family~\citep{panickssery2024selfrecognition, li2026preferenceleakage}, and catches
about as many planted defects as \texttt{GPT-5}.
Re-judging 201 sampled responses, 14 to 20 per model, with \texttt{Grok 4.6} from a third family
changes each model's coverage of them by $-0.018$ to $+0.019$, and every 95\% interval includes
zero.
In the step-matching panel, dropping a response model's own-family judge changes its score by at
most 0.006, as much as dropping one of the other two (the design detects changes of 0.003 to
0.025), yet each judge accepts 90\% to 97\% of the incorrect steps it judges outside
\texttt{Llama 3.1 70B}'s responses: an absence of self-preference does not make a judge accurate.

\subsection{Readings on the Evaluated Models}
\label{appendix:readings}

Because the study's responses come from models outside the eleven, domain experts also read samples
of the evaluated models' responses, without the model's name.
A domain expert reads up to 20 flagged calculations per model, drawn from all its answered
responses.
Two domain experts of the item's branch read each of 144 final-answer verdicts on the five strongest
models (83 correct, 43 incorrect, and 18 partial) and 97 judge rulings across all eleven models
(49 reached and 48 missing), and agree with each other on 97\% of answers and 88\% of milestones.
The readings cut both ways.
The experts call 18 of their 36 readings of partial verdicts fully correct and 9 incorrect, so
scoring a partial answer 0.5 is conservative on average, and in a third of their readings of
missing rulings the response's route does not need the milestone, so MC is conservative for a
response that takes another route; but 15 of the 98 readings of reached rulings do not confirm
them, and 8 of the 86 readings of incorrect verdicts call the answer correct.
The readings are reported per verdict category; the scorer's accuracy per model is not measured.

\autoref{tab:judged_steps} gives the judged step check's flag rates on the evaluated models.
On the expert study the check's precision is 0.640 over all steps and 0.467 inside correct answers
(7 of 15 flags), so about half of its flags on correct answers mark a real error.
