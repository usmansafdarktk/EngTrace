\section{Validation Against Expert Judgment}
\label{appendix:validation}

This section details the expert study behind~\autoref{sec:evaluation} and the domain experts'
readings of the evaluated models' responses; \autoref{tab:validation_results} collects the results.

\subsection{Expert Study}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Instances}: 60, four from each of 15 templates across the five branches and three
    levels; only 12 have a single scalar answer, against 89 of the 150 templates, which makes the
    study a hard test of answer checking.
    \item \textbf{Responses}: 300, from five LLMs outside the evaluated models (\texttt{GPT-5},
    \texttt{Claude Opus 4.7}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Pro}, \texttt{DeepSeek-R1}, and
    \texttt{Llama 3.1 70B}).
    \item \textbf{Labels}: 15 domain experts, three per branch, label each response of their branch
    behind an opaque code: every step (correct, alternative correct, incorrect with a type and a
    reason, or not a claim), every milestone, the final answer (correct, partial, incorrect, or not
    stated), and the soundness of the reasoning.
    \item \textbf{Quality rounds}: a reason on all 1,042 steps labeled incorrect, a blind
    re-labeling of seven responses per expert, and a blind adjudication of the 272 split steps.
    \item \textbf{Agreement}: Fleiss'~$\kappa$~\citep{fleiss1971} 0.781 on steps, 0.880 on
    milestones, and 0.966 on final answers; Cohen's~$\kappa$~\citep{cohen1960} 0.828 between an
    expert's two passes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluator Designs}

We compare six designs on these labels.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Step matching with in-family judges}: matches response steps to gold steps by
    numerical agreement and semantic similarity, sends unmatched steps to a panel of \texttt{GPT-5},
    \texttt{Claude Opus 4.5}, and \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Pro} when the answer is correct but fewer than
    80\% of the gold steps match and for a random fifth of wrong answers, and scores an F1 over the
    steps; we run it with two and with three judges voting.
    \item \textbf{Step matching with outside judges}: the same, with \texttt{Grok 4.6},
    \texttt{MiniMax M3}, and \texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro}.
    \item \textbf{Process reward models}: \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B},
    \texttt{Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B}~\citep{zhang2025prmlessons}, and
    \texttt{VersaPRM}~\citep{zeng2025versaprm}, which reward each step; a reward below 0.5 counts as
    incorrect.
    \item \textbf{Milestone matching alone}: as in~\autoref{sec:evaluation}, with no judge.
    \item \textbf{Milestone matching with the arithmetic check}.
    \item \textbf{Milestone matching with the judge}, crediting only reached.
\end{itemize}
Our evaluator combines the last three with the final-answer check and the judged step check.

\paragraph{Main Findings.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Whether the answer is right nearly decides the experts' verdict on a response's soundness
    (area under the ROC curve, AUROC, 0.974), so evaluators can only be told apart on steps and
    milestones.
    \item Milestone matching with the judge agrees best with the experts' milestone labels.
    \item Of the 178 incorrect steps behind correct answers, 175 are calculation slips, which rarely
    change milestone coverage; the arithmetic check is there to find them.
    \item The best reward model ranks steps well (AUROC 0.825) but over-flags inside correct
    answers.
    \item Outside judges leave the step-matching score unchanged (0.379 against 0.378).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Planted Defects}

Because the labels hold almost no conceptual error behind a correct answer, we plant one defect in
each of 120 clean responses, verified without the checker under test: 60 one-digit slips in a
displayed intermediate value and 60 conceptual defects that misstate a criterion, rule, or formula
without changing any digit; 60 untouched responses serve as controls.
Each LLM judge sees the planted step and, separately, the same step untouched, with the step prompt
of~\autoref{appendix:scoring}, and a defect counts as caught only when the judge flags the planted
step alone.
No deterministic check catches a conceptual defect, and the four judges catch at most about a third
of them without false alarms; the judged step check, which sends every unflagged step, catches more
than step matching, whose panel sees a conceptual defect no more often than the same step untouched.

\subsection{Judge Independence}

\texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro} shares no family with an evaluated model, since LLM judges can favor their
own family~\citep{panickssery2024selfrecognition, li2026preferenceleakage}, and catches about as
many planted defects as \texttt{GPT-5}.
Re-judging 220 sampled responses, 20 per model, with \texttt{Grok 4.6} from a third family changes
each model's coverage by $-0.017$ to $+0.020$, and every 95\% interval includes zero.
In the step-matching panel, dropping a response model's own-family judge changes its score by at
most 0.006, no more than dropping another family's judge, yet each judge accepts 65\% to 70\% of the
incorrect steps it judges: an absence of self-preference does not make a judge accurate.

\subsection{Readings on the Evaluated Models}

A domain expert reads up to 20 arithmetic flags per model, without the model's name.
Two domain experts of the item's branch read each of 150 final-answer verdicts on the five strongest
models (75 correct, 49 incorrect, and 26 partial) and 100 judge rulings across all eleven models
(50 reached and 50 missing), and agree with each other on 97\% of answers and 88\% of milestones.
Two readings bear on the scores: the experts call most partial verdicts fully correct, so scoring
them 0.5 is conservative, and a third of the missing rulings are milestones that the response's
route does not need, so MC is conservative for a response that takes another route.
