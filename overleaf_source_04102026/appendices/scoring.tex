\section{Scoring Details}
\label{appendix:scoring}

This section details the scoring of~\autoref{sec:evaluation}: the final-answer check, milestones and
the judge, the arithmetic check, and the judged step check.

\subsection{Final-Answer Check}

\paragraph{Reading the Final Answer.}
The check reads a response's final answer from a window of 700 characters that starts at its last
final-answer heading or, without one, at its answer marker; a response with neither is read from
its last mention of ``answer'' or from its end.
It reads numbers in scientific notation, including superscript exponents, in LaTeX, as fractions or
multiples of $\pi$, and with thousands separators, and a digit subscripted with an underscore, as in
\texttt{p\_1}, is not a value.
A final answer is readable when the response has a final-answer heading or an answer marker, or a
number or verdict word in its last 700 characters; an empty response is unreadable.

\paragraph{Targets.}
The targets are the values on the gold trace's final-answer line that the template computed, so a
restated input or a constant of a formula is not a target; for an instance without milestones, the
target is the last value on that line that the question does not give.
A scalar or array answer has one target, the last such value; a vector, multipart, symbolic, or
classification answer has one target per computed value it states.
A classification answer also has the gold's verdict word as a target, which the response matches
when its final answer's last mention of a word of the same family (e.g., laminar, turbulent, or
transitional), not counting negated ones, is that word; a labeled yes-or-no property (e.g.,
``causal: no'') is matched label by label.
\autoref{appendix:statistics} gives the answer kinds by template.

\paragraph{The Match Rule.}
The unit factors $\mathcal{C}$ of~\autoref{eq:match} are $10^{\pm 3}$, $10^{\pm 6}$, $10^{\pm 9}$,
and $10^{\pm 12}$ for metric prefixes, 100 and 0.01 for percentages, and 60, 3,600, and their
inverses for seconds, minutes, and hours.
A response's own last digit counts only when one unit of that digit is smaller than the value, so a
bare 0 or 1 cannot match a target through its last digit.
A value also matches in absolute value, since sign conventions differ, as for a deflection stated
as downward.
Where the question prescribes the rounding (e.g., ``to 4 decimals''), the digits must match
exactly, and a scalar gold answer that states one quantity in two units, such as radians and
degrees, accepts either.
On the 2,250 gold traces, the check scores every instance correct.

\paragraph{The Tolerance.}
The relative tolerance $\epsilon$ is the check's one fitted constant.
Fitted on one half of the expert study's responses it is 0.0015, and on the other half 0.0020;
scored on the half it was not fitted on, the three-way agreement with the domain experts is 0.908
and 0.939.
We use $\epsilon = 0.002$.

\subsection{Milestones and the Judge}

\paragraph{Milestones.}
For each instance, we run its template at the recorded seed and use the computed values only if
the regenerated question and gold trace equal the instance byte for byte.
A milestone is a computed value that the gold trace states within 0.5\% and that the question does
not give, even in other units.
We exclude values within $10^{-9}$ of 0, 1, or 2, which match almost any text; loop counters and
other bookkeeping variables; the intermediate states of an iterative scheme, which a response that
starts from another guess does not pass through; and the elements of containers such as lookup
tables.
A quantity stored under two names or in two units counts once.
Every gold trace reaches all of its milestones, and the 70 instances without a milestone are left
out of MC.

\paragraph{Milestone Matching.}
Milestone matching uses the templates' display tolerance of 0.5\% and the unit factors of the
final-answer check except $10^{\pm 12}$.
\autoref{tab:milestone_tolerance} shows how the tolerance and the unit factors affect coverage on
the expert study, against a baseline of chance matches: the coverage that a response reaches of the
milestones of the other instances of its template.
With unit factors, the separation between the two is 0.646 to 0.699 from 0.2\% to 1\%; unit factors
raise coverage at every tolerance and raise the separation at 0.5\% and below.

\paragraph{The Judge.}
The judge, \texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro} (Xiaomi), receives one call per response in which matching misses
a milestone, holding the question, the response, and each missed milestone's name (the template's
variable name) and value printed to six digits (e.g., 113.552), but never the gold trace.
We query it through OpenRouter at temperature 0, with JSON output and up to 16,384 output tokens,
and ask again, up to three attempts in all, when a reply is empty or malformed.
On the expert study's 300 responses, matching finds 951 of the 1,245 milestones and the judge
decides the other 294; on the evaluated models, the judge decides 11\% to 25\% of milestones.
The prompt is provided below.

\begin{tcolorbox}[
    colback=gray!5,
    colframe=gray!40,
    boxrule=0.4pt,
    arc=2pt,
    left=6pt,
    right=6pt,
    top=6pt,
    bottom=6pt,
    breakable
]
\footnotesize
\ttfamily
You are checking one student's worked solution to an engineering problem. \\

PROBLEM: \\
\{question\} \\

STUDENT SOLUTION: \\
\{trace\} \\

A correct solution to this problem obtains the intermediate quantities listed below.
An automatic checker could not find them in the student's solution by matching numbers.
For EACH one, decide: \\

- "REACHED": the student does obtain this quantity correctly - possibly in different units, a
different but equivalent form, or with different rounding. \\
- "NOT\_NEEDED": the student reaches a correct result by a valid route that does not pass through
this quantity at all. \\
- "MISSING": the student does not obtain this quantity, or obtains it incorrectly. \\

QUANTITIES: \\
\{quantities\} \\

Return a raw JSON object only: \\
\{"results": [\{"milestone": "\textless name\textgreater", "verdict": "REACHED" | "NOT\_NEEDED" |
"MISSING"\}]\}
\end{tcolorbox}

\paragraph{Validating the Judge.}
We credit only reached, a choice we validate on answers known without the judge
(\autoref{tab:judge_validation}): 88 milestones that matching finds in the expert study's responses,
shown once with their true values and once with every value multiplied by 1.37, which the response
never states.
The judge never rules a changed value reached, but it rules 22 of the 88 not needed, so crediting
not needed would excuse values that a response never states; it also rules 20 of the 88 true values
missing, so the strict reading is conservative.

\subsection{Arithmetic Check}

\paragraph{The Rule.}
The check splits each step into chains such as $a = b = c$, evaluates every numeric segment with
SymPy~\citep{meurer2017sympy}, and skips symbolic segments, which it cannot evaluate without values
for their symbols.
Each pair of consecutive segments is a calculation, which passes when its right side is a correct
rounding, at the precision the right side displays, of its left side recomputed from the response's
own displayed numbers: $0.92979^{2/3} = 0.95300$ is flagged, because the value is 0.95263 at five
decimals, whereas $0.92979^{2/3} = 0.9526$ passes.
A unit conversion is compared after its unit factor, and a result is not held to a precision that
its displayed operands cannot determine.

\paragraph{Validating the Rule.}
On the 2,250 gold traces, the check flags none of the 6,875 calculations it reads in their 9,391
steps.
\autoref{tab:arithmetic_readings} compares the rule with relative tolerances on the expert study's
labels: a tolerance of 1\%, which asks only whether the work shown could produce a value, finds
almost none of the slips that the domain experts mark inside correct-answer responses.

\subsection{Judged Step Check}

\paragraph{The Check.}
The judged step check splits a response into the steps on which the experts' labels are made and
sends every non-empty step that the arithmetic check does not flag to the judge, in one call per
response that holds the question, the gold trace's steps, the response's steps, and the indices of
the steps under review.
The judge labels each step a conceptual error, a calculation error, alternative correct, or other;
a step labeled an error is flagged, and other is recorded but not flagged.
We query it at the provider's defaults with up to 8,192 output tokens, ask again with 16,384 when a
reply is cut, and ask again, up to three attempts in all, when a reply cannot be parsed.
The prompt is provided below; the placeholders in braces hold the question, the two lists of steps,
and the indices under review.

\begin{tcolorbox}[
    colback=gray!5,
    colframe=gray!40,
    boxrule=0.4pt,
    arc=2pt,
    left=6pt,
    right=6pt,
    top=6pt,
    bottom=6pt,
    breakable
]
\footnotesize
\ttfamily
You are an expert engineering professor acting as an automated evaluator.
Your task is to analyze the Student's reasoning steps against the Ground Truth steps. \\

QUESTION: \\
\{question\} \\

GROUND TRUTH CHAIN: \\
\{gold steps\} \\

STUDENT CHAIN: \\
\{response steps\} \\

TASK: \\
Analyze the Student Steps at these specific indices (0-based): \{indices\}. \\
Compare them logically to the Ground Truth. \\

CRITICAL RULES: \\
1. If the step is factually correct but takes a different path than the Ground Truth, you MUST use
"Alternative Correct". \\
2. Do not contradict the category definitions below. \\
3. The final output must be a raw JSON object only. \\

ERROR CATEGORIES: \\
- "Conceptual Error": The model applied the wrong scientific principle or formula. \\
- "Calculation Error": The model used the correct formula but made a mathematical mistake. \\
- "Alternative Correct": The model's step is valid and logically sound, but follows a different
method/phrasing. \\
- "Other": Nonsensical, irrelevant, hallucination, or formatting errors. \\

OUTPUT FORMAT: \\
Return a single JSON object containing a "results" list. Each entry must have exactly two keys:
"step\_index" and "category". \\
\{"results": [\{"step\_index": \{first index\}, "category": "Alternative Correct"\}]\}
\end{tcolorbox}

\paragraph{Validating the Check.}
On the expert study, the judged step check, together with the arithmetic check as the study ran it,
finds incorrect steps with precision 0.707 and recall 0.603 over all steps, and 0.703 and 0.360
inside correct-answer responses; on the planted conceptual defects of~\autoref{appendix:validation},
it catches 19 of 60, with 2 false alarms on the 842 untouched steps judged with them.
