\section{Scoring Details}
\label{appendix:scoring}

This section details the four checks of~\autoref{sec:evaluation};
\autoref{tab:scoring_settings} tests each setting on the expert study
of~\autoref{appendix:validation}, and~\autoref{box:worked_example} scores one response end to end.
Every check reads the response a model returns, not the reasoning text that some endpoints return
separately.

\subsection{Final-Answer Check}

The check reads the final answer from the 700 characters that follow the response's last
final-answer heading or, failing that, its answer marker (the first when several lie within 700
characters of one another, otherwise the last) or its last mention of ``answer'', and otherwise
from its last 700 characters; a response with neither a heading or marker nor a number or label
word in its last 700 characters has no readable final answer.
It reads scientific notation, LaTeX, fractions, multiples of $\pi$, and thousands separators as
numbers, but not a digit subscripted with an underscore, as in \texttt{p\_1}.
The targets are the milestones on the gold trace's final-answer line, the last one for a scalar or
array answer and every one otherwise; where the line states no milestone, the target is its last
value that the question does not give, so a vector or multipart answer is then checked on that
value alone.
A classification answer also takes as targets any label word that its gold line states, which the
last non-negated word of the same family (e.g., laminar or turbulent) in the response's final answer
must match, and any yes-or-no property, matched label by label.

\autoref{eq:match} uses the unit factors $10^{\pm 3}$, $10^{\pm 6}$, $10^{\pm 9}$, $10^{\pm 12}$,
100, 60, 3,600, and their inverses, and $\epsilon$ is the check's one fitted constant.
A response's last digit counts only when one unit of it is smaller than the value, and its term
$c\,u(\hat{y})$ in~\autoref{eq:match} counts for at most 1\% of the target; values also match in
absolute value, a question that prescribes its rounding requires the exact digits, and a scalar gold
answer stated in two units accepts either.
Every one of the 2,250 gold traces scores correct.

\paragraph{Symbolic Answers.} Nine templates answer with an expression, such as a signal or a
velocity field, and the check scores them by the numbers the expression states.
On five of the nine templates with symbolic answers it also decides equivalence: it parses the gold
expression and every expression in the response's final answer with SymPy~\citep{meurer2017sympy},
and accepts the response when, at every sample point of the free variable, its value lies within
half a unit of the last digit of every decimal the gold writes, so an equal expression in another
form is correct and an answer coarser than the gold is not.
This step only raises an incorrect or partial verdict to correct and never lowers one.
Against domain experts' equivalence grades, it reaches precision 1.000 and recall 0.990 on the 264
verdicts the experts graded, which also served to settle the rule, and precision 1.000 and recall
0.840 on the 30 readings of these templates in the experts' readings of the evaluated models
(\autoref{appendix:validation} and~\autoref{appendix:error_analysis}), which the rule did not see.
The other four templates with symbolic answers stay scored by the numbers they state.

\paragraph{Prescribed Digits.} 17 templates prescribe the rounding of their answer and of the values
it depends on, as their procedures do in practice (an order quantity in whole units, a standard
normal value to four decimals), and the check then requires the prescribed digits: a value within
the tolerance but not at those digits is incorrect by the question's own terms.
We keep this requirement as part of the task; relaxing it to the tolerance raises 154 verdicts on 15
templates and keeps Kendall's $\tau$ with the ordering as scored at 0.964
(\autoref{tab:scoring_variants}).

\paragraph{Sensitivity of the Rule.} \autoref{tab:scoring_variants} re-applies the rule with one
clause changed at a time.
Turning off the absolute-value clause lowers 325 verdicts on 20 templates ($\tau = 0.927$); leaving
the last-digit term uncapped raises 68 verdicts on 36 templates ($\tau = 1.000$); and partial credit
in proportion to the parts matched, instead of one half, moves 109 verdicts on 14 templates
($\tau = 1.000$).
Of the 22,131 accepted numeric answers, 92.7\% lie within 0.2\% of their target, 6.9\% between
0.2\% and 1\%, which the last-digit terms admit, and 0.4\% further from it.

\subsection{Milestones and the Judge}

\paragraph{Milestones.} We rerun each template at the instance's seed and, if the regenerated
question and gold trace equal the instance byte for byte, take as milestones the computed values
that the gold trace states within 0.5\% and the question does not give under any unit factor.
We exclude values within $10^{-9}$ of 0, 1, or 2, bookkeeping variables, the intermediate states of
iterative schemes, and the elements of containers such as lookup tables, and count a quantity
stored twice once.
Matching uses the unit factors above except $10^{\pm 12}$, and MC leaves out the 57 instances
without milestones.
Matching checks the value alone: not the quantity's name, the unit written beside it, the step it
appears in, or the order and dependencies among steps, so a value that coincides with a milestone
counts, and the chance floor of~\autoref{tab:coverage}, the same responses matched against a
sibling instance's milestones, measures how often that happens.
The judge sees each milestone's name and value and rules on the response's own route; it is the
one check that can credit a quantity stated in another form or excuse one the route does not need.

\paragraph{The Judge.} \texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro} (Xiaomi) receives one call per response in which
matching misses a milestone, with the prompt below, at temperature 0, with JSON output and up to
three attempts; it decides 294 of the 1,245 milestones of the expert study.
We credit only reached because the judge calls none of the 88 unstated values of
\autoref{tab:scoring_settings} reached but excuses a quarter of them as not needed.

\subsection{Arithmetic Check}

The check evaluates each chain of numeric segments in a step, such as $a = b = c$, with
SymPy~\citep{meurer2017sympy}, skips symbolic segments, and flags a result that is not a correct
rounding, at its displayed precision, of the value recomputed from the response's displayed
operands: $0.92979^{2/3} = 0.95300$ is flagged (0.95263 at five decimals), whereas
$0.92979^{2/3} = 0.9526$ passes.
Unit conversions are compared after their factor, and no result is held to a precision that its
operands cannot determine.
It flags none of the 6,875 calculations it reads in the 2,250 gold traces.

\subsection{Judged Step Check}

The check sends the judge one call per response with the prompt below, at the provider's default
settings, reviewing every non-empty step that the arithmetic check does not flag, and flags a step
the judge labels a conceptual or calculation error.
Its flags are the judge's rulings, not verified errors: \autoref{appendix:validation} reports their
precision against the domain experts, and~\autoref{tab:judged_steps} their rates on the evaluated
models.

- "Calculation Error": The model used the correct formula but made a mathematical mistake. \\

- "Other": Nonsensical, irrelevant, hallucination, or formatting errors. \\
