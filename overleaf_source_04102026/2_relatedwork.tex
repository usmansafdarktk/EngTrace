\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Engineering and Science Benchmarks.} General suites such as
\textsc{SuperGPQA}~\citep{du2025supergpqa} and most engineering benchmarks, whether
multi-branch~\citep{zhou2025engibench, naser2026eri} or single-branch~\citep{li2025eeebench}, grade
final answers, and the few that assess reasoning rely on domain
experts~\citep{syed2024transportbench} or LLM judges~\citep{zheng2026tpscalcbench, wasiq2026engvqa}.
Closest to \ourdataset, \textsc{ThermoQA}~\citep{duzkar2026thermoqa} scores property values and
weighted solution steps against values computed with a property library, on a fixed set of
thermodynamics problems; \ourdataset instead draws its instances from a private seed across five
branches, recomputes the arithmetic a response displays, and has experts certify every template.
Science benchmarks such as \textsc{SciBench}~\citep{wang2023scibench},
\textsc{TheoremQA}~\citep{chen2023theoremqa}, \textsc{OlympiadBench}~\citep{he2024olympiadbench},
\textsc{UGPhysics}~\citep{xu2025ugphysics}, and \textsc{PHYBench}~\citep{qiu2025phybench} score
final answers or answer expressions, and \textsc{PRISM-Physics} checks steps against reference
formulas~\citep{zhao2026prismphysics}.

\paragraph{Symbolic and Dynamic Benchmarks.} Fixed problems can leak into training
data~\citep{zhang2024gsm1k}, so symbolic benchmarks generate varied instances from templates, often
built from existing problems to test whether a model has memorized the
original~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic, gulati2024putnamaxiom}, and dynamic and functional
benchmarks generate fresh problems from code or
grammars~\citep{srivastava2024functionalbench, zhu2024dyval, mishra2024mathcamps} or perturb
existing ones~\citep{li2024gsmplus}.
Some also generate gold solutions, as \textsc{SymPyBench} does in
physics~\citep{imani2025sympybench} and \textsc{HARDMath} in applied
mathematics~\citep{fan2024hardmath}, and in engineering \textsc{CIRCUIT} and \textsc{OPT-Engine}
generate problems with their answers~\citep{skelic2025circuit, chen2026optengine}.
\textsc{FinChain} pairs each templated financial problem with an executable gold reasoning chain
and scores a response by aligning its steps with that chain~\citep{xie2025finchain}; \ourdataset
adds the recomputation of displayed arithmetic, a judge that sees only unmatched milestones and
unflagged steps, expert certification of every template, and instances from a private seed.

\paragraph{Step-Level Evaluators.} Without exact references, step-level evaluation relies on process
reward models (PRMs), LLM judges, or reference-free measures.
PRMs learn from human step labels~\citep{lightman2023verify} or from labels estimated by
rollouts~\citep{wang2023mathshepherd}, which are noisy~\citep{zhang2025prmlessons}; they are
evaluated on annotated erroneous steps in
mathematics~\citep{zheng2024processbench, song2025prmbench} and generalize poorly to other
domains~\citep{zeng2025versaprm}.
Reference-free evaluators score each step's validity, informativeness, or redundancy without a gold
solution~\citep{golovneva2022roscoe, prasad2023receval, xia2024reasoneval}.
LLM judges make correlated errors~\citep{goel2025greatmodels} and agree with experts mainly on
questions they can answer themselves, unless given a correct
reference~\citep{krumdick2026nofreelabels}.
\ourdataset instead checks steps against its computed milestones and recomputes arithmetic, sending
only the unmatched milestones, with their expected values, and the unflagged steps to an LLM judge.
