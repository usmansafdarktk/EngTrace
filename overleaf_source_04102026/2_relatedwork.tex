\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Engineering Benchmarks.} General suites such as
\textsc{SuperGPQA}~\citep{du2025supergpqa} and most engineering benchmarks, whether
multi-branch~\citep{zhou2025engibench, naser2026eri} or single-branch~\citep{li2025eeebench}, grade
final answers.
The few benchmarks that assess reasoning mostly rely on domain
experts~\citep{syed2024transportbench} or LLM judges~\citep{zheng2026tpscalcbench, wasiq2026engvqa}.
Closest to \ourdataset, \textsc{ThermoQA} in thermodynamics~\citep{duzkar2026thermoqa} and
\textsc{PRISM-Physics} in physics~\citep{zhao2026prismphysics} check steps against computed values
and reference formulas, respectively, but on a fixed set of problems.

\paragraph{Symbolic Benchmarks.} Fixed problems can leak into training data~\citep{zhang2024gsm1k};
to mitigate this, symbolic benchmarks generate varied instances from templates.
Many build their templates from existing problems to test whether a model has memorized the
original~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic, gulati2024putnamaxiom}.
Some also generate gold solutions, as \textsc{SymPyBench} does in
physics~\citep{imani2025sympybench}, \textsc{HARDMath} in applied
mathematics~\citep{fan2024hardmath}, and \textsc{FinChain} in finance~\citep{xie2025finchain}.
In engineering, \textsc{CIRCUIT} and \textsc{OPT-Engine} generate circuit and optimization problems
with their answers~\citep{skelic2025circuit, chen2026optengine}.
Like these, \ourdataset's templates generate engineering problems, but they also encode the full
derivation, computing every intermediate quantity exactly from physically grounded parameters.

\paragraph{Step-Level Evaluators.} Without exact references, step-level evaluation relies on process
reward models or LLM judges.
Process reward models learn from human step labels~\citep{lightman2023verify} or from labels
estimated by rollouts~\citep{wang2023mathshepherd}, which are noisy~\citep{zhang2025prmlessons}.
Most use mathematical training data and generalize poorly to other domains~\citep{zeng2025versaprm}.
LLM judges make correlated errors~\citep{goel2025greatmodels} and agree with experts mainly on
questions they can answer themselves, unless given a correct
reference~\citep{krumdick2026nofreelabels}.
Even \textsc{FinChain} and \textsc{HARDMath} score responses by step alignment and rubric-based LLM
judging, respectively.
\ourdataset instead verifies steps against its computed milestones and recomputes arithmetic,
leaving only unmatched residual milestones to an LLM judge given their expected values.
