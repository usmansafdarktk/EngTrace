\section{\ourdataset Design and Methodology}
\label{sec:benchmark}



\subsection{Taxonomy and Content Selection}
\label{subsec:taxonomy}

The taxonomy of \ourdataset, illustrated in~\autoref{fig:engtrace-overview}, follows formal
engineering curricula.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figs/engtrace-overview-5branch.pdf}
    \caption{\ourdataset's \textbf{Hierarchical Taxonomy} spanning 15 domains and 42 areas across
    five branches:
    process equilibrium and dynamics (Chemical); field theory and signal transmission (Electrical);
    structures, soils, and water resources (Civil); production, quality, and stochastic operations
    (Industrial); and physical mechanics and material properties (Mechanical).}
    \label{fig:engtrace-overview}
\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Domain and Area Selection.}
We draw the candidate domains and areas of each branch from its curricular documents, the
accreditation criteria of ABET~\citep{abet2024criteria}, the Fundamentals of Engineering exam
specifications of NCEES for the five
branches~\citep{ncees2020fechemical, ncees2020fecivil, ncees2020feelectrical, ncees2020feindustrial, ncees2020femechanical},
and the bodies of knowledge of ASCE~\citep{asce2019cebok} and IISE~\citep{iise2021bok}, and from the
standard textbooks of each branch.
A panel of three LLMs from families outside the evaluated models, each prompted as a curriculum
designer, names the cornerstone domains of each branch and the fundamental areas of each domain,
and we finalize the 15 domains, three per branch, and their 42 areas by majority vote (prompts
in~\autoref{sec:appendix_domain_validation} and~\autoref{sec:appendix_area_validation}).

\paragraph{Template Selection.}
Domain experts hand-authored all symbolic Python templates, grounding them directly in foundational
textbooks and authoritative engineering
handbooks~\citep[e.g.,][]{fogler2020elements, ulaby2015fundamentals, hibbeler2020, montgomery2013}
(see~\autoref{sec:appendix_books} for the complete list of source texts).
The template distribution per area is non-uniform and follows a pedagogical significance score
(1--5) that the same three LLMs assigned to each area within its domain
(\autoref{sec:appendix_significance_scoring}); \autoref{tab:area} gives the templates per area and
level.
Each branch holds three domains by design, so core areas such as circuit analysis in electrical
engineering, thermodynamics and heat transfer in mechanical engineering, and transportation and
environmental engineering in civil engineering are not covered.

\paragraph{Difficulty Levels.}
The levels are the domain experts' labels, set when the templates were written and fixed before any
model ran: following a fixed protocol, the template's author and two other experts of its branch
rate it from 1 to 3 on conceptual complexity, mathematical sophistication, and procedural depth,
counting only what the solver must supply, and the sum of the median scores sets the level (3--4
Easy, 5--6 Intermediate, 7--9 Advanced).
\autoref{appendix:statistics} gives the levels by branch and the other statistics of the templates
and the evaluation set.




\subsection{Template Generation Pipeline}
\label{subsec:templates}

The core of \ourdataset is the symbolic template, a Python blueprint for generating physically
grounded engineering problems.
As shown in~\autoref{fig:template-generation}, it samples domain-aware parameters from
authoritative data sources (\autoref{sec:appendix_data_sources}) and fundamental principles
(\autoref{sec:appendix_param_details}), redraws them until the scenario is physically valid,
computes every quantity with the governing equations, and writes the question and the gold
reasoning trace.
Because both come from one computation, every intermediate quantity is known exactly, with its
unit; these are the milestones whose coverage our evaluator measures.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figs/template-generation.pdf}
    \caption{The \ourdataset \textbf{Template Generation Pipeline} maps domain-aware constraints to
    serialized question-solution pairs via physically validated computation, illustrated here using
    a CSTR volume calculation example.}
    \label{fig:template-generation}
\end{figure*}

Templates go beyond variable substitution: sampled parameters can change the physical regime, the
governing equation, and the reasoning path (\autoref{sec:appendix_template_examples}).
This diversity is physical rather than linguistic, since the question wording comes from the
template code; a paraphrase test (\autoref{appendix:paraphrase}) bounds what the fixed wording is
worth to a model and finds that it changes no model's score significantly.




\subsection{Template Certification}
\label{subsec:certification}

Because an error in a template recurs in every instance and gold trace, each template passes three
checks before it enters the benchmark (\autoref{appendix:certification}).

\paragraph{Automated Integrity Checks and LLM Screen.}
At 500 seeds per template, automated checks verify that every printed calculation follows from its
printed operands, that generation is deterministic, and that every instance keeps the output format;
all 150 templates pass.
Three LLM judges from families outside the evaluated models then score each template's code and
instances; after we revise the templates they flag, 147 of the 150 pass and none fails critically.
The screen guides revision but does not certify: it passes 15 templates that a majority of their
experts reject.

\paragraph{Expert Certification.}
Three domain experts per branch review its 30 templates mixed with four planted defects, without
knowing which items are planted, and solve one instance of each template by hand before seeing its
gold trace.
Every expert rejects all four planted defects of their branch (60 of 60 readings), 459 of the 504
hand checks that yield a comparable number match the gold value within 1\%, and agreement on
approval is high (Gwet's AC1 0.914~\citep{gwet2008}; Fleiss'~$\kappa$~\citep{fleiss1971} 0.589,
deflated because nearly every template is approved~\citep{feinstein1990high}).
The planted defects test numeric correctness, such as transposed digits or a flipped sign, and not
whether a question pins down a single reading.
A template that any of its experts rejects is revised and reviewed again; after six rounds, all 150
templates are certified by a unanimous vote of their three experts.
