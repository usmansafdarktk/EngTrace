\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
We evaluate eleven LLMs: eight open-weights models (\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash},
\texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, \texttt{GLM-5.3}, \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}, \texttt{gpt-oss-20b},
\texttt{Kimi K3}, \texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}, and \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}) and three closed models
(\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}).
One rule selects them: no model that wrote responses for the expert study
(\autoref{sec:evaluation}) and no model we use as a judge, so that the evaluator is validated on
other models' responses and no model judges its own.
Flagship models are either excluded by this rule or left out for cost; two flagships that the rule
admits, \texttt{GPT-5.4} and \texttt{DeepSeek V4 Pro}, serve as anchors on a fixed subset of 450
instances (three per template) that the further conditions below also use.

\paragraph{Decoding and Prompt.}
Each model answers each instance once, 24,750 responses in all, at its provider's default decoding
settings: we set no sampling parameters.
The output ceiling is 32,768 tokens (16,384 for \texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}, the most its endpoint
allows), and every open-weights model runs at 8-bit floating-point precision or higher.
All models receive the same zero-shot prompt, which asks for the governing equations, numbered
steps, and a final answer line, with no tools or retrieval.
At these defaults, four models return no reasoning tokens (\texttt{Gemma 4 26B},
\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, two of the
three closed models), while the other seven write a median of 718 to 2,473 reasoning tokens per
response; the models are therefore not compared at equal reasoning effort.
In all, 246 responses (1.0\%) contain no readable final answer and score 0; 243 of them are empty,
almost all at the output ceiling.

\paragraph{Statistical Protocol.}
The template is our unit of analysis, because the 15 instances of a template share one derivation
and their scores are correlated~\citep{miller2024errorbars}.
Every 95\% interval is a percentile bootstrap over templates with 10,000
resamples~\citep{efron1993bootstrap}.
We compare two models with a paired sign-flip permutation test on their per-template
differences~\citep{dror2018hitchhiker}, using 100,000 sign flips, and a model's Easy and Advanced
templates with Welch's $t$-test~\citep{welch1947generalization}, because scores vary more across
Advanced templates.
Within each family of tests, such as the 55 pairs of models, Holm's
correction~\citep{holm1979simple} keeps the probability of any false positive at 5\% or less.
We report every non-significant difference with the smallest difference the design detects at
80\% power.
\autoref{appendix:models} lists the served models, their settings and token use, the prompt, and
the statistical protocol in full.
