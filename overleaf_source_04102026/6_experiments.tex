\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.} We evaluate eleven LLMs: eight open-weights models,
\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}~\citep{deepseekv41}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}~\citep{gemma4},
\texttt{GLM-5.3}~\citep{glm5}, \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}~\citep{glm5},
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}~\citep{gptoss}, \texttt{Kimi K3}~\citep{kimik3},
\texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}~\citep{museglimmer}, and \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}~\citep{qwen3}; and three
closed models, \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}~\citep{claudesonnet5},
\texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}~\citep{gemini31flashlite}, and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}~\citep{gpt54}.
One rule selects them: no model whose responses we used to develop or validate the evaluator
(\autoref{sec:evaluation}) and no model we use as a judge.
For cost, two flagships the rule admits, \texttt{GPT-5.4}~\citep{gpt54} and
\texttt{DeepSeek V4 Pro}~\citep{deepseekv4}, run only as anchors on a fixed subset of 450 instances
(three per template), which the further conditions also use.

\paragraph{Decoding and Prompt.} Each model answers each instance once (24,750 responses) with the
same zero-shot prompt, which asks for the governing equations, numbered steps, and a final answer
line, without tools or retrieval.
We set no sampling parameters, so each model decodes at its provider's defaults, with an output
ceiling of 32,768 tokens (16,384 for \texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}, the most its endpoint allows).
At these defaults, four models return no reasoning tokens (\texttt{Gemma 4 26B},
\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, two of the
three closed models), so the models are not compared at equal reasoning effort.
In all, 246 responses (1.0\%) have no readable final answer and score 0, almost all of them empty
at the output ceiling.

\paragraph{Statistical Protocol.} The template is our unit of analysis, because its 15 instances
share one derivation and their scores are correlated~\citep{miller2024errorbars}; every 95\%
interval is therefore a bootstrap over templates~\citep{efron1993bootstrap}.
We compare two models with a sign-flip permutation test on their per-template
differences~\citep{dror2018hitchhiker}, and a model's Easy and Advanced templates with Welch's
$t$-test~\citep{welch1947generalization}, because scores vary more across Advanced templates.
Within each family of tests, such as the 55 pairs of models, Holm's
correction~\citep{holm1979simple} keeps the probability of any false positive at 5\% or less, and
we report every non-significant difference with the smallest difference the design detects at
80\% power.
\autoref{appendix:models}, \autoref{appendix:prompt}, and \autoref{appendix:protocol} give the
served models with their token use, the prompt, and the protocol in full.
