\autoref{tab:main_results} places the two answer measures beside the three derivation measures; we
read the final answer first and then what the derivation shows that the answer cannot.

\paragraph{Two Tiers, No Order Within the Top.} The final answer sorts the models into two tiers: a
top tier of five that lie within 0.009 of one another and a lower tier of five, each of which
differs from every model above it, with one model between the two.
No pair inside the top tier differs after Holm's correction, which tightens the 55 pairwise tests so
that the chance of any false positive among them stays at most 5\%, and the design detects
differences of one to three points between them.
Four of the top five are open-weights, and four of the lower five return no reasoning tokens at
their providers' defaults.
The tiers differ in reliability more than in reach: almost no template defeats a model on all 15
of its instances, but the top six solve 80\% to 87\% of the templates on every instance and the
lower five only 35\% to 63\%, so a weaker model fails a problem on some parameter values and solves
it on others, which a benchmark with one instance per problem would hide.

\paragraph{Derivations Behind Correct Answers.} The derivation separates models that the answer
ties, and it exposes slips that the answer passes.
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} leads on MC and \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} on FAC; FAC cannot tell the
two apart, MC can.
MC credits the intermediate values a response states, so it ranks how much of the derivation a
model shows, not how soundly it reasons.
Behind correct answers, the arithmetic check finds a displayed calculation that does not follow
from its own operands in up to 22.5\% of a model's responses, and of the 189 flags a domain expert
decided on, 171 are real slips (precision 0.905); the judged step check flags a further step in up
to 22.0\%.
These are flags, not counts: both checks miss more than half of the flawed steps the domain experts
marked, a model that displays more arithmetic exposes more to the check, and no deterministic check
sees a rule misstated behind a correct answer.

\paragraph{Derivations Behind Wrong Answers.} Most wrong answers are late failures of a derivation
that is largely in place, and the process measures locate the break.
On their wrong answers, the five lower-tier models still state 44\% to 59\% of the gold milestones,
against a chance floor of at most 20\%, and up to 26\% of these wrong answers are complete
derivations to a wrong value.
A milestone the judge rules missing marks most of the lower tier's wrong answers (59\% to 79\%)
but 24\% of \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}'s, whose wrong answers the domain experts mostly read as no
error at all (\autoref{subsec:error_analysis}).
The lower tier also fails on the numbers rather than the method: on the 58 templates whose
instances all follow one derivation, it solves some but not all instances of up to 57\% of the
templates, the top tier of at most 14\% (\autoref{appendix:results}).

\paragraph{Stability of the Tiers.} Neither paraphrase, nor the answer tolerance, nor decoding
noise moves a model across a tier.
Each of 277 instances over 115 templates has a paraphrase that keeps every number, unit, and symbol
in place and that a domain expert confirms is the same problem; no model's change in FAC between
the two is significant after correction, and for ten of the eleven models it is bounded within
$\pm 0.05$ at 90\% confidence (\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} reaches $-0.067$).
Halving or doubling the answer tolerance swaps no pair of models that differs, and decoding four
models three more times moves FAC by a standard deviation of at most 0.013.
The tiers therefore hold, but not the order inside the top tier, and the paraphrase bounds
memorized wording only, since the numbers and the problem structure stay
(\autoref{appendix:paraphrase}).

\subsubsection{Performance by Engineering Branch and Domain}
\label{subsec:branch_domain}

The top tier is uniform across engineering and the lower tier is not.
Each top-tier model's five branch means lie within 0.04 to 0.05 of each other and none of its 15
domain means falls below 0.87, whereas each lower-tier model's branch means spread by 0.11 to 0.23
and each falls below 0.73 in at least one domain.
No branch is hard for every model: with 30 templates per branch, differences below 7 to 19 points
cannot be told from sampling noise, and only one of the 110 within-model branch comparisons holds
after correction (\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, electrical above civil).
Thermodynamics is the lowest domain for eight of the eleven models, largely because it holds the
two Advanced chemical templates whose wording does not pin the answer
(\autoref{subsec:difficulty_level}); without them it is the lowest for one, so a low domain mean
points first at templates, not at a field (\autoref{appendix:branch_domain}).

Difficulty labels separate the lower tier; the depth of the gold trace lowers every model.
Every model scores lower on Advanced than on Easy templates (\autoref{fig:level_bars}), by 0.055 to
0.199.
The gap holds after correction for multiple comparisons only for the four lowest-scoring models,
three of which run without reasoning tokens, and for no model once the two Advanced chemical
templates whose wording does not pin the answer are set aside, as three chemical domain experts
read them (\autoref{appendix:error_analysis}).
Depth is a property of the instance, not a label, and it lowers every model: for all eleven, the
share of responses scored 0 is higher on instances with six or more gold milestones than on
instances with one, reaching 0.249 to 0.291 for the four lowest-scoring models against at most
0.074 for the top five (\autoref{appendix:results}).

\subsubsection{Reasoning, Equations, and Tools}
\label{subsec:reasoning_equations_tools}

Three experiments on a 450-instance subset each change one thing in the evaluation and are read on
both measures (\autoref{appendix:experiments}).
Reasoning effort changes the derivation, not only the answer, and it is the one change that moves a
model by more than any difference within a tier.
With reasoning at medium effort, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} gains 0.093 in FAC and 0.061 in MC while
its arithmetic flags on correct answers roughly halve, and the flagship \texttt{GPT-5.4}, which
also returns no reasoning tokens at its default, gains 0.039 and sheds most of its flags;
\texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} moves by less than the experiment detects.
The lower tier of~\autoref{tab:main_results} is therefore what the providers' defaults produce, not
a ceiling; nor does a larger model raise the ceiling, since two flagships run on the same subset lie
inside the top tier's intervals, one of them only with reasoning on.

Reference equations and a calculator do not move the top tier.
Given each template's governing equations with the question, every model states more milestones,
but only the weakest model answers more correctly ($+0.064$, partly because fewer of its responses
run out of room), and the two closed models stay within $\pm 0.05$.
Offered a Python interpreter, which \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} calls on two thirds of the instances,
neither closed model changes in FAC or MC beyond $\pm 0.05$.
On this benchmark, then, neither recall of the governing equations nor arithmetic execution bounds
the top tier; what moved scores was the reasoning setting, for one of the two models tested, and
recall, for the weakest model.
The experiments supply equations without data tables, offer the tool rather than require it, and
cover the two closed models in the tool experiment, since no admitted endpoint serves the
open-weights model's tool calls.

Three domain experts read the full response, the derivation and its final answer, of 160 wrong
answers, 40 from each of four models that contrast the tiers (480 readings), and assigned the first
category that applies in a six-category hierarchy from hallucination to calculation, or ``no
error'' (Fleiss'~$\kappa$ 0.930~\citep{fleiss1971}; \autoref{fig:error_categories}).
Execution errors dominate, and conceptual errors grow with difficulty.
Calculation is the majority label for \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} and \texttt{Gemma 4 26B} (28 of 40
wrong answers each) and the largest for \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} (17 of 40), the one model with many
conceptual errors (a hallucination, a wrong setup, or a wrong formula in 19 of 40); a hallucinated
value or equation appears in 13 of the 480 readings.
On Easy problems 88\% of the readings are calculation errors, and wrong formulas or principles rise
from 3\% of the readings on Easy problems to 18\% on Intermediate and Advanced ones.
For \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, 26 of 40 wrong answers are ``no error'' by majority, 25 of them on
three templates whose question or check is at issue.
The reading agrees with the experiments: \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, whose wrong answers are mostly
slips, is the model that reasoning effort lifted; \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, with the conceptual errors,
is the one the governing equations lifted; and an offered calculator lifted neither closed model.

The top tier's remaining incorrect verdicts are few and mostly a matter of question wording or
answer form.
Over 11,250 responses, the top five models lose 319 answer points: 90 to responses empty at the
output ceiling, 58 to 116 partial answers at half credit, and 171 to incorrect verdicts, of which 57
fall on the two chemical templates whose wording does not pin the answer, 38 are symbolic answers
scored by the numbers they state, and 27 lie within 0.2\% of a target whose digits the question
prescribes, leaving 49 incorrect verdicts on other templates.
The headroom at the top therefore lies in Advanced templates and long derivations, in prescribed
precision, in the output budget, and in the derivation, which the process measures still separate
when the answers tie; and since a response that misstates the rule it applies but computes the right
answer passes every deterministic check, the process measures flag where to read and the expert
reading is what certifies (\autoref{appendix:error_analysis}).
