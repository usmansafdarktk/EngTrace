\autoref{tab:main_results} places the answer measures beside the derivation measures; we read the
final answer first and then what the derivation shows that the answer cannot.

\paragraph{Tiers and a Ceiling.} At matched settings, the eleven models form a graded
order of eight overlapping groups, with FAC of 0.808 to 0.988, and 36 of the 55 pairs differ after
Holm's correction; only \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} differs from every other model, while
\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, \texttt{Kimi K3}, and
\texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash} differ from \texttt{GLM-5.3} and every model below it.
The first five lie within 0.019 of one another, and only three of their ten pairs differ, although
the design detects differences of one to two points between them; the three are
\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} above \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash} and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, and
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} above \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}.
On the final answer these five are near the ceiling of the evaluation set: even on Advanced
templates they score 0.94 to 0.98.
The models differ in reliability more than in reach: no model fails every instance of more than
five of the 150 templates, but the first five solve 72\% to 90\% of the templates on every instance
and the other six 31\% to 81\%, a difference that one instance per problem would hide.
Of the three models run with reasoning at medium effort, only \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} reaches the
first five, and \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, whose endpoint offers no reasoning setting, is alone in
answering without reasoning tokens.

\paragraph{Derivations Behind Correct Answers.} The derivation shows differences that the final
answer does not, and slips that it passes.
As scored, MC does not separate \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} from \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} after
Holm's correction (Holm-adjusted $p$ 0.069), nor does the final answer; matching alone does
(0.001), but not once the milestones the judge rules not needed leave the denominator (1.000;
\autoref{tab:coverage_variants}).
The difference lies in how closely each derivation follows the gold route, not in whether it
reaches the quantities its own route needs; this reading rests on the judge's not-needed rulings,
which the experts did not read.
MC credits the intermediate values a response states, so it ranks how much of the gold route a
model shows, not how soundly it reasons.
The arithmetic check flags a displayed calculation that does not follow from its own operands in up
to 23.2\% of a model's correct answers.
Of the 189 flags a domain expert decided on, 171 are real slips (precision 0.905), and a mechanical
check finds none of the 168 it can classify to be a rounding carried forward from unrounded values
earlier in the response.
The judged step check flags a further step in up to 20.6\% of correct-answer responses, but its
flags are judged rather than verified: in the expert study, 7 of the 15 it raises inside correct
answers are errors (precision 0.467).
These are flags, not counts: in the expert study, both checks together find 0.36 of the flawed
steps the domain experts marked inside correct answers, a model that displays more arithmetic
exposes more to the check, and no deterministic check sees a rule misstated behind a correct
answer.

\paragraph{Derivations Behind Wrong Answers.} Wrong answers keep much of their derivation, and the
judge's missing rulings point to the gold quantities a derivation does not reach.
On their wrong answers, the last four models still state 46\% to 59\% of the gold
milestones, against a chance floor of at most 17.5\% (\autoref{tab:coverage}).
Up to 25\% of these wrong answers reach every gold milestone yet end in an answer the check scores
incorrect.
A milestone the judge rules missing marks 63\% to 78\% of the last four's wrong answers
and 46\% to 83\% of the other models', but only 20\% of \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}'s, 55\% of which
reach every milestone.
On the 58 templates whose instances all follow one derivation, the first five solve some but not
all instances of 7\% to 34\% of the templates and the other six of 14\% to 59\%
(\autoref{tab:single_path}), so a low-scoring model often solves one instance of a derivation and
fails another, a pattern consistent with the expert reading, in which calculation errors dominate
(\autoref{subsec:error_analysis}).

\paragraph{Stability of the Tiers.} Neither paraphrase, nor the answer tolerance, nor decoding
noise, measured on the four lowest models, moves a model past one it differs from; the reasoning
setting does.
Each of 275 instances over 114 templates has a paraphrase that keeps every number, unit, and symbol
in place, confirmed by a domain expert.
No model's change in FAC between the two is significant after correction, and for nine of the
eleven models it is bounded within $\pm 0.05$ at 90\% confidence (\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} reaches
$-0.073$ and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} reaches $+0.059$).
Halving or doubling the answer tolerance swaps no pair of models that differs, and decoding four
models (\texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, \texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, and
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}) three more times on 300 instances, two per template, moves FAC by a standard
deviation of at most 0.008.
Restricting FAC to the responses that state an answer reorders only the first seven (Kendall's
$\tau$ 0.636 with the ordering as scored): \texttt{GLM-5.3}, whose 99 empty responses then drop
out, rises from seventh to first, so its place in~\autoref{tab:main_results} reflects the output
ceiling.
At matched settings, then, the pairs that differ survive paraphrase, tolerance, and decoding noise,
and the paraphrase bounds memorized wording only (\autoref{appendix:paraphrase}).

\paragraph{The Reasoning Setting.} At their providers' defaults, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini},
\texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} return no reasoning tokens and score
0.852, 0.872, and 0.878.
With reasoning at medium effort, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} gains $+0.118$, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B} gains
$+0.070$, and \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} gains $+0.038$ on all 2,250 instances
(\autoref{tab:matched}).
At these defaults, the final answer sorts the models into two tiers, an upper six each of which
differs from each of a lower five, and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} ranks tenth.
With reasoning on, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} rises to 0.969, level with \texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}, and
no longer differs from \texttt{Kimi K3} and \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}, so the two tiers are a result
of the providers' defaults.

\subsubsection{Performance by Engineering Branch and Domain}
\label{subsec:branch_domain}

Branch means spread less for the first five than for the other six, but the spreads carry no test.
For the first five, the spread between the highest and the lowest branch mean is 0.018 to 0.053,
and no domain mean falls below 0.88.
For the last five, the spread is 0.071 to 0.226, and each model falls below 0.81 in at least one
domain; \texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B} lies between, with a spread of 0.056 and a lowest domain mean of
0.880.
No branch is hard for every model: with 30 templates per branch, differences below 3 to 19 points
cannot be told from sampling noise, and only one of the 110 within-model branch comparisons holds
after correction (\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, electrical above civil).
Thermodynamics is the lowest domain for four of the eleven models, but the domain means carry no
test, so this ranks domains, not fields (\autoref{appendix:branch_domain}).

Difficulty labels separate few models after correction; the depth of the gold trace goes with more
wrong answers for every model.
Every model's mean is lower on Advanced than on Easy templates (\autoref{fig:level_bars}), by
0.006 to 0.181, with intervals that include zero for seven models.
The gap holds after Holm's correction for \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} alone under Welch's $t$-test, the
test we read because scores vary more across Advanced templates, and for four models under the
permutation of level labels (\autoref{tab:level_gap}).
For all eleven models, the share of responses scored 0 is higher on instances with six or more gold
milestones than on instances with one, reaching 0.104 to 0.254 for the last five against at most
0.061 for the first five (\texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}: 0.047).
Over readable responses, with the answer kind as a covariate and templates as clusters, each
further milestone multiplies the odds of a wrong answer by 1.21 (95\% interval 1.09 to 1.33) when
pooled over the models; per model, the slope holds after Holm's correction for three of the eleven
(\autoref{tab:depth_model}).

\subsubsection{Reasoning, Equations, and Tools}
\label{subsec:reasoning_equations_tools}

Four experiments on a 450-instance subset extend the evaluation, three changing one thing and one
adding two flagships as anchors (\autoref{appendix:experiments}); each of the three starts from the
providers' defaults and is paired with the default responses on the same instances.
Reasoning effort has the largest effect, and for \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} it changes the derivation,
not only the answer.
On the subset, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} gains 0.057 in MC with reasoning at medium effort, beside the
FAC gain above, while its arithmetic flags on correct answers roughly halve.
The flagship \texttt{GPT-5.4}, which also returns no reasoning tokens at its default, gains 0.046
and sheds most of its flags.
Nor does a larger model clearly raise the ceiling: on the same subset \texttt{DeepSeek V4 Pro}
scores 0.968 and \texttt{GPT-5.4} 0.951 at its default and 0.997 with reasoning, against 0.967 to
0.990 for the first five, and no test is run against the anchors.
The highest flagship score, \texttt{GPT-5.4}'s with reasoning, lies inside the interval of
\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, and on Advanced templates the flagships score 0.89 to 1.00 and the
first five 0.94 to 0.99.

Reference equations and an offered interpreter move no model of the first five beyond the margin.
Given each template's governing equations with the question, each of the three models given them
states more milestones, but only \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} answers more correctly ($+0.068$, partly
because fewer of its responses are empty at the output ceiling), and \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} and
\texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} stay within $\pm 0.05$.
Offered a Python interpreter, which \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} calls on two thirds of the instances,
neither \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} nor \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} changes in FAC or MC beyond
$\pm 0.05$, a bound that says little for \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, which is already near the
ceiling.
The experiments supply equations without data tables, offer the tool rather than require it, and
cover the two closed models in the tool experiment, since no admitted endpoint serves the
open-weights model's tool calls.

Three domain experts read the full response of 137 wrong answers from four models that span the
FAC order (19 from \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, 39 from \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, 39 from
\texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and 40 from \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}; 411 readings, all at the providers' default
settings) and assigned the first category that applies in a six-category hierarchy from
hallucination to calculation, or ``no error'' (Fleiss'~$\kappa$ 0.912~\citep{fleiss1971};
\autoref{fig:error_categories}).
Calculation errors dominate, and conceptual errors (a hallucination, a wrong setup, or a wrong
formula) are rarer on Easy problems than on the harder ones.
Calculation is the majority label for \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} (29 of 39, 28 of 39, and 12 of 19 wrong answers) and the largest for
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b} (18 of 40).
Conceptual errors are most common for \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}: a hallucination, a wrong setup, or a
wrong formula in 18 of its 40 wrong answers, against 5 of 19 for \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, 7 of 39
for \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, and 9 of 39 for \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}.
On Easy problems, 88\% of the 78 readings of 26 wrong answers are calculation errors, and wrong
formulas or principles rise from 3\% of the readings on Easy problems to 21\% on Intermediate and
Advanced ones.
The reading agrees with the reasoning setting and the experiments, each agreement resting on one
or two models: \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} and \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, whose wrong answers are mostly
calculation slips, gain 0.118 and 0.070 with reasoning (\texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, $+0.038$,
was not read), and the equations lift only \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, the model with the most
conceptual errors, partly through fewer responses empty at the output ceiling.

The first five lose few answer points, and the largest share goes to incorrect verdicts.
Over 11,250 responses, the first five models lose 231 answer points.
Of these, 71 go to responses with no readable final answer and 34 to 68 partial answers at half
credit.
Of their 126 incorrect verdicts, 38 lie within 0.2\% of a target whose digits the question
prescribes, which makes them incorrect by its terms, 5 are symbolic answers scored by the numbers
they state, and 83 fall in neither group (\autoref{appendix:error_analysis}).
On the final answer, then, the evaluation set is near ceiling for the first five, and most of what
still differs among them lies in the derivation: how closely it follows the gold route, and how
often its displayed arithmetic slips, which the arithmetic check flags in 0.6\% to 14.3\% of their
correct answers, at rates that depend on how much arithmetic each displays.
Since a response that misstates the rule it applies but computes the right answer passes every
deterministic check, the process measures mark where to read, and only a reader can confirm such a
rule.
