\paragraph{Two Tiers and a Ceiling.} At the providers' defaults, the final answer
(\autoref{tab:main_results}) sorts the models into an upper tier of six models with FAC of 0.950 to
0.988 and a lower tier of five with 0.808 to 0.899, each of which differs from each upper-tier model
after Holm's correction over the 55 pairwise tests.
The first five lie within 0.019 of one another, and only one of their ten pairs differs
(\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash} above \texttt{GLM-5.3-Flash}), although the design detects
differences of one to three points between them.
On the final answer these five are near the ceiling of the evaluation set: even on Advanced
templates they score 0.94 to 0.98.
The tiers differ in reliability more than in reach: no model fails every instance of more than 4\%
of the templates, but the upper six solve 81\% to 90\% of the templates on every instance and the
lower five only 31\% to 63\%, a difference that one instance per problem would hide.
Four of the lower five return no reasoning tokens at their providers' defaults.

\paragraph{Derivations Behind Correct Answers.} As scored, MC separates \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}
from \texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, which the final answer does not (Holm-adjusted $p$ 0.049), and so
does matching alone (0.001), but not once the milestones the judge rules not needed leave the
denominator (1.000): the difference lies in how closely each derivation follows the gold route, not
in how far it gets.
Behind correct answers, the arithmetic check flags a displayed calculation that does not follow
from its own operands in up to 23.2\% of a model's correct answers.
Of the 189 flags a domain expert decided on, 171 are real slips (precision 0.905), none of them a
rounding carried forward from unrounded values earlier in the response.
The judged step check flags a further step in up to 21.9\% of correct-answer responses, but its
flags are judged rather than verified: in the expert study, 7 of the 15 it raises inside correct
answers are errors (precision 0.467).
These are flags, not counts: both checks together miss more than half of the flawed steps the
domain experts marked, a model that displays more arithmetic exposes more to the check, and no
deterministic check sees a rule misstated behind a correct answer.

\paragraph{Derivations Behind Wrong Answers.} Wrong answers keep much of their derivation, and the
process measures locate the break.
On their wrong answers, the five lower-tier models still state 44\% to 59\% of the gold milestones,
against a chance floor of at most 17\%.
Up to 26\% of these wrong answers are complete derivations to a wrong value.
A milestone the judge rules missing marks most of the lower tier's wrong answers (63\% to 78\%) but
only 20\% of \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}'s.
On the 58 templates whose instances all follow one derivation, the lower tier solves some but not
all instances of up to 59\% of the templates and the upper tier of at most 22\%
(\autoref{tab:single_path}), so a lower-tier model often knows the method and misses the numbers.

\paragraph{Stability of the Tiers.} Neither paraphrase, nor the answer tolerance, nor decoding
noise moves a model across the tiers; the reasoning setting does.
Each of 275 instances over 114 templates has a paraphrase that keeps every number, unit, and symbol
in place, confirmed by a domain expert.
No model's change in FAC between the two is significant after correction, and for nine of the
eleven models it is bounded within $\pm 0.05$ at 90\% confidence (\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507} reaches
$-0.073$ and \texttt{gpt-oss-20b} reaches $+0.059$).
Halving or doubling the answer tolerance swaps no pair of models that differs, and decoding four
models (\texttt{Qwen3-235B-2507}, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}) three more times on 300 instances, two per template, moves FAC by a standard
deviation of at most 0.016.
Restricting FAC to the responses that state an answer reorders the upper tier only (Kendall's
$\tau$ 0.709 with the ordering as scored): \texttt{GLM-5.3}, whose 99 empty responses then drop
out, rises from sixth to first, so its place in~\autoref{tab:main_results} reflects the output
ceiling.
With reasoning at medium effort, \texttt{Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite} gains $+0.038$,
\texttt{Gemma 4 26B} gains $+0.070$, and \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} gains $+0.118$ on all 2,250
instances (\autoref{tab:matched}).
At these matched settings, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} rises to 0.969, level with
\texttt{Muse Glimmer 30B}, and the two tiers give way to a graded order: only \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}
differs from every other model, while the first four still differ from every model at or below
\texttt{GLM-5.3}.
The tiers therefore hold at the providers' defaults, though not the order among the first five; the
paraphrase bounds memorized wording only (\autoref{appendix:paraphrase}).

\subsubsection{Performance by Engineering Branch and Domain}
\label{subsec:branch_domain}

The first five are uniform across engineering and the lower tier is not: for the first five, the
spread between the highest and the lowest branch mean is 0.018 to 0.056, and no domain mean falls
below 0.88.
For the lower tier, the spread is 0.071 to 0.226, and each model falls below 0.79 in at least one
domain.
No branch is hard for every model: with 30 templates per branch, differences below 3 to 19 points
cannot be told from sampling noise, and only one of the 110 within-model branch comparisons holds
after correction (\texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, electrical above civil).
Thermodynamics is the lowest domain for five of the eleven models, but the domain means carry no
test (\autoref{appendix:branch_domain}).

Every model scores lower on Advanced than on Easy templates (\autoref{fig:level_bars}), by 0.008 to

\subsubsection{Reasoning, Equations, and Tools}
\label{subsec:reasoning_equations_tools}

Four experiments on a 450-instance subset each change one thing in the evaluation
(\autoref{appendix:experiments}); reasoning effort is the one change that moves a model by more than
any difference within a tier, and it changes the derivation, not only the answer.
On the subset, \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} gains 0.102 in FAC and 0.057 in MC with reasoning at medium
effort, while its arithmetic flags on correct answers roughly halve.
The flagship \texttt{GPT-5.4}, which also returns no reasoning tokens at its default, gains 0.046
and sheds most of its flags.
Nor does a larger model clearly raise the ceiling: on the same subset the two flagships score 0.951
to 0.997 against 0.959 to 0.990 for the first five.
The highest flagship score, \texttt{GPT-5.4}'s with reasoning, lies inside the interval of
\texttt{DeepSeek V4.1 Flash}, and on Advanced templates the flagships score 0.89 to 1.00 and the
first five 0.94 to 0.99.
Given each template's governing equations with the question, every model states more milestones,
but only the weakest model answers more correctly ($+0.068$, partly because fewer of its responses
run out of room), and the two closed models stay within $\pm 0.05$.
Offered a Python interpreter, which \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} calls on two thirds of the instances,
neither closed model changes in FAC or MC beyond $\pm 0.05$, a bound that says little for
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, which is already near the ceiling.
The experiments supply equations without data tables and offer the tool rather than require it.

Three domain experts read the full response of 137 wrong answers from four models that contrast the
tiers (19 from \texttt{Claude Sonnet 5}, 39 from \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, 39 from
\texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and 40 from \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}; 411 readings, all at the providers' default
settings) and assigned the first category that applies in a six-category hierarchy from
hallucination to calculation, or ``no error'' (Fleiss'~$\kappa$ 0.912~\citep{fleiss1971};
\autoref{fig:error_categories}).
Execution errors dominate, and conceptual errors grow with difficulty.
Calculation is the majority label for \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini}, \texttt{Gemma 4 26B}, and
\texttt{Claude Sonnet 5} (29 of 39, 28 of 39, and 12 of 19 wrong answers) and the largest for
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b} (18 of 40).
\texttt{gpt-oss-20b} is the one model with many conceptual errors: a hallucination, a wrong setup,
or a wrong formula in 18 of its 40.
On Easy problems, 88\% of the 78 readings of 26 wrong answers are calculation errors, and wrong
formulas or principles rise from 3\% of the readings on Easy problems to 21\% on Intermediate and
Advanced ones.
The reading agrees with the experiments, each agreement resting on one or two models: the mostly
slipping \texttt{GPT-5.4 mini} and \texttt{Gemma 4 26B} gain the most from reasoning, and the
equations lift only \texttt{gpt-oss-20b}, the one with conceptual errors.

Over 11,250 responses, the first five models lose 231.5 answer points.
Of these, 102 go to responses with no readable final answer and 30.5 to 61 partial answers at half
credit.
Of their 99 incorrect verdicts, 27 lie within 0.2\% of a target whose digits the question
prescribes and 3 are symbolic answers scored by the numbers they state, which leaves 69.
On the final answer, then, the evaluation set is near ceiling for the first five, and what still
differs among them lies in the derivation: how closely it follows the gold route, and how often its
displayed arithmetic slips, which the arithmetic check flags in 0.6\% to 14.3\% of their correct
answers.
Since a response that misstates the rule it applies but computes the right answer passes every
deterministic check, the process measures flag where to read, and the expert reading certifies
(\autoref{appendix:error_analysis}).
