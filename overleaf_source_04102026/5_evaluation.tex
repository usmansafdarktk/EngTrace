\section{Evaluation Framework}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Because each template computes every quantity of its gold trace, we check deterministically what can
be checked: the final value, the presence of the gold trace's intermediate values, and displayed
arithmetic; a judge decides what these checks leave open.
An expert study guides and validates this design: on 300 responses from five LLMs outside the
eleven we evaluate, each labeled step by step by three domain experts, the final-answer check
matches the experts' three-way verdict on 0.930 of responses, and milestone matching with the judge
reaches F1 0.958 (\autoref{appendix:validation}).
The study also informed the checks' settings, so these figures are in-sample, except that the
answer tolerance, the one fitted constant, is also fitted on one half of the responses and measured
on the other (\autoref{tab:scoring_settings}).
\autoref{box:worked_example} scores one response step by step.

\paragraph{Preliminaries.} An instance comprises a question, a gold trace, and milestones
$\mathcal{M}$: the values that the template computes, the gold trace states, and the question does
not give.
A response $r$ is a sequence of steps ending in a final answer.
The \emph{targets} of an instance are the milestones that its gold final answer states or, where it
states none, the answer's last value that the question does not give.
A classification answer also targets the label that the gold answer states, such as a flow regime.

\paragraph{Final Answer Accuracy.} Let $u(x)$ be one unit in the last digit that a value $x$
displays, $\mathcal{C}$ a fixed set of unit factors (e.g., m to mm), and $\epsilon = 0.002$.
A value $\hat{y}$ in the final answer matches a numerical target $y$ if, for some
$c \in \mathcal{C}$,
\begin{equation}
    |c\,\hat{y} - y| \le \max\bigl(\epsilon\,|y|,\; \min(c\,u(\hat{y}),\, 0.01\,|y|),\; u(y)\bigr),
    \label{eq:match}
\end{equation}
so a value within one unit of either value's last displayed digit matches, the response's own digit
up to 1\% of the target.
Symbolic answers are checked for equivalence on five templates and by their stated numbers on four
(\autoref{appendix:scoring}).
A response scores $v(r) = 1$ if every target matches, $0.5$ if only some do, and $0$ if none does or
no final answer is readable, since stating one within the output limit is part of the task.
\emph{Final Answer Accuracy} (FAC) is the mean of $v(r)$ over instances.

\paragraph{Milestone Coverage.} A milestone is reached if the response states its value within
0.5\% under a unit factor, in any step and order; 0.5\% is the tolerance at which the gold trace
counts as stating a milestone, not a fitted constant.
Matching, like every check, reads the response's visible text, not the reasoning text that some
endpoints return separately.
Milestones that matching misses go to an LLM judge from outside the evaluated families,
\texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro}~\citep{mimov25pro}, which sees the question, the response, and each
milestone's name and value, but not the gold trace, and rules each reached, not needed, or missing.
We credit only reached, since the judge also excuses unstated values as not needed
(\autoref{appendix:scoring}).
With $\mathcal{R}(r)$ the milestones matched or ruled reached,
\begin{equation}
    m(r) = |\mathcal{R}(r)| \,/\, |\mathcal{M}|,
    \label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
and \emph{Milestone Coverage} (MC) is the mean of $m(r)$ over instances with milestones.
The judge decides 11\% to 25\% of milestones, depending on the model.
MC credits the gold route: a valid route that bypasses a milestone is not credited, so MC measures
how far a derivation gets along that route, not whether it is free of error.

\paragraph{Step-Level Diagnostics.} Two diagnostics look for flawed steps behind a correct answer.
The arithmetic check recomputes each displayed calculation it can parse from its printed operands
and flags a result that is not a correct rounding at its printed precision.
The judged step check sends every step the arithmetic check does not flag to the same judge, at the
provider's default settings, with the question and the gold steps, and flags a step labeled a
conceptual or calculation error; its flags are judged, not verified.
\emph{Arithmetic flags} and \emph{judged step flags} are the shares of correct-answer responses with
at least one such flag.
They are flags with measured precision, not scores, since neither check sees every flawed step: on
60 planted misstated rules behind correct answers, none of our deterministic checks flags any, and
four LLM judges label at most about a third of them an error.
