\section{Evaluation Framework}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Because each template computes every quantity of its gold trace, we check deterministically what can
be checked: the final value, the presence of the gold trace's intermediate values, and displayed
arithmetic; a judge decides what these checks leave open.~\autoref{box:worked_example} scores one response step by step.

\paragraph{Preliminaries.} An instance comprises a question, a gold trace, and milestones
$\mathcal{M}$: the values that the template computes, the gold trace states, and the question does
not give.
A response $r$ is the text a model returns, a sequence of steps ending in a final answer; no check
reads the reasoning text that some endpoints return separately.

\paragraph{Final Answer Accuracy.} The \emph{targets} of an instance are the milestones that its
gold final answer states or, where it states none, the answer's last value that the question does
not give.
A classification answer also targets the label that the gold answer states, such as a flow regime.
Let $u(x)$ be one unit in the last digit that a value $x$ displays, $\mathcal{C}$ a fixed set of
unit factors (e.g., m to mm), and $\epsilon = 0.002$, the check's one fitted constant.
A value $\hat{y}$ in the final answer matches a numerical target $y$ if, for some
$c \in \mathcal{C}$,
\begin{equation}
    |c\,\hat{y} - y| \le \max\bigl(\epsilon\,|y|,\; \min(c\,u(\hat{y}),\, 0.01\,|y|),\; u(y)\bigr),
    \label{eq:match}
\end{equation}
so a value matches within one unit of the last digit that either value displays, the response's
unit counting only up to 1\% of the target.
Symbolic answers are checked for equivalence on five templates and by their stated numbers on four
(\autoref{appendix:scoring}).
A response scores $v(r) = 1$ if every target matches, $0.5$ if only some do, and $0$ if none does or
no final answer is readable, since stating one within the output limit is part of the task.
\emph{Final Answer Accuracy} (FAC) is the mean of $v(r)$ over instances.

\paragraph{Milestone Coverage.} A milestone is reached if the response states its value within
0.5\% under a unit factor; 0.5\% is the tolerance at which the gold trace counts as stating a
milestone, not a fitted constant.
Matching compares values only, not the quantity's name, the unit written beside it, the step it
appears in, or the order and dependencies among steps, so a coincidental value counts, which the
chance floor of~\autoref{tab:coverage} measures.
Milestones that matching misses go to an LLM judge from outside the evaluated families,
\texttt{MiMo-V2.5-Pro}~\citep{mimov25pro}, which sees the question, the response, and each
milestone's name and value, but not the gold trace, and rules each reached, not needed, or missing.
We credit only reached, since the judge also excuses unstated values as not needed
(\autoref{appendix:scoring}).
With $\mathcal{R}(r)$ the milestones matched or ruled reached,
\begin{equation}
    m(r) = |\mathcal{R}(r)| \,/\, |\mathcal{M}|,
    \label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
and \emph{Milestone Coverage} (MC) is the mean of $m(r)$ over instances with milestones.
The judge decides 11\% to 24\% of milestones, depending on the model.
MC credits the gold route: a valid route that bypasses a milestone is not credited (case A
of~\autoref{box:coverage_cases}), so MC measures how much of the gold route a derivation states,
not whether it is free of error (case B).

\paragraph{Step-Level Diagnostics.} Two diagnostics look for flawed steps behind a correct answer.
The arithmetic check recomputes each displayed calculation it can parse from its printed operands
and flags a result that is not a correct rounding at its printed precision.
The judged step check sends every step the arithmetic check does not flag to the same judge, at the
provider's default settings, with the question and the gold steps, and flags a step labeled a
conceptual or calculation error; its flags are judged, not verified.
\emph{Arithmetic flags} and \emph{judged step flags} are the shares of correct-answer responses with
at least one such flag; MC and the two flag rates together are the process measures of this paper.
They are flags with measured precision, not scores, since neither check sees every flawed step: on
60 planted misstated rules behind correct answers, none of our deterministic checks flags any, and
four LLM judges label at most about a third of them an error.

\paragraph{Validation.} An expert study guides and validates these checks: on 300 responses from
five LLMs outside the eleven we evaluate, each labeled step by step by three domain experts, the
final-answer check matches the experts' three-way verdict on 0.930 of responses, and milestone
matching reaches F1 0.926 alone and 0.958 with the judge (\autoref{appendix:validation}).
By answer kind, the final-answer check matches the experts' verdict on every scalar, array and
symbolic response of the study, and multipart responses agree least (\autoref{tab:answer_kinds}).
The study also informed the checks' settings, so these figures are in-sample, except that
$\epsilon$ is also fitted on one half of the responses and measured on the other
(\autoref{tab:scoring_settings}).
On the evaluated models, domain experts confirm 160 of 166 readings of correct verdicts, 75 of 86
of incorrect ones, and 83 of 98 readings of reached rulings (\autoref{appendix:readings}).
