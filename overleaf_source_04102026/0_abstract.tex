Engineering problems are solved by derivations whose intermediate quantities can be checked, yet
existing benchmarks such as \textsc{SuperGPQA} and \textsc{EEE-Bench} score only final answers, and
step-level ones, such as \textsc{TPS-CalcBench}, rely on large language model (LLM) judges or
human graders.
We present \ourdataset, a symbolic benchmark for verifiable process evaluation of engineering
reasoning, with 150 templates spanning five branches, 15 domains, and 42 areas.
Each template is expert-certified code that samples physically grounded parameters and computes
the answer and a gold reasoning trace together, so every intermediate quantity is known exactly.
An expert-validated evaluator is deterministic wherever a check exists: final answers of six kinds,
coverage of the gold trace's intermediate quantities (milestones), and displayed arithmetic, with
residual milestones sent to an LLM judge outside the evaluated families; a misstated rule behind a
correct answer escapes these checks.
Across 2,250 instances, eleven LLMs attain final-answer accuracies of 0.81 to 0.99, the five
strongest within 0.02 and near the ceiling.
Milestone coverage is similarly high (0.81 to 0.92), and wrong answers still state about half of
the gold milestones.
Errors on Easy problems are mostly calculation slips, while wrong formulas or principles appear
mostly on Intermediate and Advanced problems.
