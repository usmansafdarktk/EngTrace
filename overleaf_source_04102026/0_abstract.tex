Most engineering problems involve complex multi-step derivations through checkable intermediate
quantities.
Grading only the final answer misjudges LLMs, crediting correct answers reached through flawed steps
and penalizing wrong answers built on mostly sound ones.
Yet, existing benchmarks such as \textsc{SuperGPQA} and \textsc{EEE-Bench} score only final answers,
and step-level ones such as \textsc{TPS-CalcBench} rely on LLM judges or human graders, or cover a
single domain.
We therefore introduce \ourdataset, a symbolic benchmark for verifiable process evaluation of
engineering reasoning.
Its 150 expert-certified templates, spanning five branches, 15 domains, and 42 areas, generate each
problem with a gold reasoning trace, so that every intermediate quantity is known exactly.
We design and validate an evaluator that checks final answers, coverage of the gold trace's
intermediate quantities (\emph{milestones}), and displayed arithmetic, mostly without an LLM judge.
Across 11 LLMs, final answers barely separate the strongest models, while their derivations differ.
Wrong answers still state about half of the gold milestones, and domain experts trace most wrong
answers to calculation slips, with conceptual errors rarer on easy problems than on harder ones.
Overall, as LLMs near the final-answer ceiling, measuring their true progress will require checking
derivations, as \ourdataset does.
