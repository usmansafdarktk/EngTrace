Most engineering benchmarks for large language models (LLMs) score only final answers; those assessing steps rely on LLM judges or human graders.
We present \ourdataset, a benchmark of 150 symbolic templates spanning five engineering branches.
Each template is code that samples physically grounded parameters and computes the answer and its gold reasoning trace, so every intermediate quantity is known.
Three experts from each branch certified its templates and rejected every planted defect.
Deterministic checks score the final answer and intermediate-quantity coverage, and flag arithmetic slips; an LLM judge from outside the evaluated model families decides only what these checks leave open.
Against step-level labels from 15 experts on 300 responses, the answer check agrees on 93\% of verdicts and coverage reaches an F1 of 0.96.
Errors planted in responses show that no deterministic check detects a misstated rule behind a correct answer; LLM judges catch at most about a third.
On 2,250 instances generated from a private seed, eleven LLMs score 0.81 to 0.98 in final-answer accuracy; the top five cannot be separated.
Coverage orders the models differently from accuracy, and responses with wrong answers still reach 46\% to 85\% of the intermediate quantities.
