\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

As LLMs enter specialized domains such as engineering, it becomes essential to evaluate how they
reason, not only what they answer.
Engineering derivations are multi-step, governed by physical laws, and pass through checkable
intermediate quantities.
A correct final answer can still follow flawed reasoning~\citep{zheng2024processbench}, yet most
engineering benchmarks grade only the final
answer~\citep{du2025supergpqa, li2025eeebench, zhou2025engibench}, and those that assess
intermediate steps rely on LLM judges or human graders, or are limited to a single
domain~\citep{syed2024transportbench, zheng2026tpscalcbench, duzkar2026thermoqa}.
In our expert study on 300 responses from five LLMs outside the 11 we evaluate, correct answers
still come with 178 flawed steps, 175 of them calculation slips (\autoref{appendix:validation}).
What the final answer cannot show is therefore how much of the correct derivation a wrong final
answer keeps and which intermediate quantities it misses, the slips behind correct answers, and how
models whose answers tie differ.

To address these gaps, we introduce \ourdataset, a symbolic benchmark for verifiable process
evaluation of engineering reasoning.
Following symbolic-template benchmarks in mathematics~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic} and
finance~\citep{xie2025finchain}, \ourdataset represents each problem as a template: Python code
that samples physically grounded parameters and computes the answer with every intermediate
quantity of its gold reasoning trace (\autoref{fig:engtrace-template-cstr}).
The 150 templates span five engineering branches, 15 domains, and 42 areas at three difficulty
levels.
The benchmark covers a depth-oriented subset of engineering, three core domains per branch, and its
problems are examination-style by construction, since a gold trace must be computable.

Our expert-validated evaluator is deterministic wherever a check exists: it verifies final answers
of six kinds, measures the coverage of the gold trace's intermediate quantities (\emph{milestones}),
and recomputes the arithmetic a response displays.
An LLM judge from outside the evaluated model families decides the milestones that matching misses.
The deterministic checks cannot see a rule misstated behind a correct answer, a blind spot we
measure with planted defects.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We present \ourdataset, a benchmark of physically grounded symbolic templates for
          engineering reasoning, each certified by three domain experts from its own branch.
    \item We design an expert-validated evaluator that deterministically checks final answers of six
          kinds along with intermediate values and displayed arithmetic (three-way final-answer
          agreement 0.930; milestone F1 0.926 by matching alone, 0.958 with the judge).
    \item We evaluate 11 open-weights and closed LLMs on 2,250 template instances and show that the
          five strongest models lie within 0.02 of one another on final-answer accuracy, whereas
          milestone coverage and the arithmetic check show that their derivations still differ.
          Wrong answers still state about half of the gold milestones, and domain experts find that
          errors on Easy problems are mostly calculation slips, while incorrect formulas or
          principles are more frequent on Intermediate and Advanced problems.
\end{itemize}
