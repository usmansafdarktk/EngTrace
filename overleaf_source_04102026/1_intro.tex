\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

As large language models (LLMs) enter specialized domains such as engineering, it becomes
essential to evaluate how they reason, not only what they answer.
Engineering derivations are multi-step and physically grounded, and they pass through intermediate
quantities that can be checked.
A correct final answer can still follow flawed reasoning~\citep{zheng2024processbench}, yet most
engineering benchmarks grade the final answer rather than the steps that produce
it~\citep{du2025supergpqa, zhou2025engibench}, and those that assess steps rely on LLM judges or
human graders, or cover a single
domain~\citep{syed2024transportbench, zheng2026tpscalcbench, duzkar2026thermoqa}.
On examination-style engineering derivations, our expert study finds that the answer nearly settles
soundness: whether it is right nearly decides domain experts' verdict on a response, and 175 of the
178 flawed steps behind correct answers are arithmetic slips (\autoref{appendix:validation}).
What step-level checks add is therefore where a wrong derivation breaks, the slips behind right
answers, and a comparison of models whose answers tie.

To provide these checks, we introduce \ourdataset, a symbolic benchmark for verifiable process
evaluation of engineering reasoning.
Following symbolic-template benchmarks in mathematics~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic} and
finance~\citep{xie2025finchain}, \ourdataset represents each problem as a template: executable
Python code that samples physically grounded parameters and computes the answer together with every
intermediate quantity of its gold reasoning trace.
The 150 templates span five engineering branches, 15 domains, and 42 areas at three difficulty
levels, and we draw the evaluated instances with a private seed, so that no instance was public when
the models answered.
The benchmark covers a depth-oriented subset of engineering, three core domains per branch, and its
problems are examination-style by construction, since a gold trace must be computable.

Our evaluator checks deterministically what can be checked: the final value of six answer kinds,
the gold trace's intermediate quantities, which we call \emph{milestones}, among the values a
response states, and the arithmetic it displays.
An LLM judge decides only the milestones that matching misses, and it comes from outside the
evaluated model families, because LLM judges can favor their own outputs and those of related
models~\citep{panickssery2024selfrecognition, li2026preferenceleakage}.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \ourdataset, 150 physically grounded symbolic templates spanning five engineering
          branches, 15 domains, and 42 areas, each certified by three domain experts from its own
          branch, with evaluation instances drawn from a private seed.
    \item An evaluator that checks final values, intermediate values, and displayed arithmetic
          deterministically, validated against domain experts' step labels (three-way final-answer
          agreement 0.930, milestone F1 0.958), with what it cannot see: a rule misstated behind a
          correct answer passes every deterministic check.
    \item An evaluation of eleven LLMs on 2,250 instances, at the providers' defaults and at matched
          reasoning settings: on the final answer the strongest models are near the ceiling of these
          problems, and Milestone Coverage, the step checks, and an expert reading of wrong answers
          show what lies beyond it.
\end{itemize}
