\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Many engineering problems are solved by derivations whose intermediate quantities can be checked, each feeding the next.
A score on the final answer alone cannot show where such a derivation goes wrong or how far a wrong one got, and a correct answer can still rest on flawed reasoning~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}: in \textsc{ProcessBench}, the share of correct-answer solutions with a process error rises from 3.5\% on \textsc{GSM8K} to 51.8\% on \textsc{Omni-MATH}~\citep{zheng2024processbench}.

Yet most engineering benchmarks for large language models (LLMs) score only the final answer.
General suites test engineering mostly through multiple choice: \textsc{SuperGPQA} scores its 7,892 engineering questions on the option chosen, although more than half of them require calculation~\citep{du2025supergpqa}.
Dedicated benchmarks typically cover one branch~\citep{syed2024transportbench, kevian2024controlbench, li2025eeebench}, and the multi-branch \textsc{EngiBench} grades answers with LLM evaluators~\citep{zhou2025engibench}.
A few look past the answer: experts read the reasoning~\citep{syed2024transportbench, kevian2024controlbench}, an LLM judge scores it~\citep{zheng2026tpscalcbench, wasiq2026engvqa}, or steps are checked against computed values within one domain, as in \textsc{ThermoQA}~\citep{duzkar2026thermoqa}.
Expert reading is costly to repeat, and LLM judges can favour their own outputs and those of related models~\citep{panickssery2024selfrecognition, spiliopoulou2025playfavorites}.
Checking each step by computation avoids both problems, but it requires the exact value of every intermediate quantity.

We present \ourdataset, a symbolic benchmark for verifiable process supervision of engineering reasoning.
As in \textsc{GSM-Symbolic} and \textsc{FinChain}~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic, xie2025finchain}, symbolic templates generate the instances, and as in \textsc{FinChain}, the gold reasoning trace comes from the code that computes the answer (\autoref{fig:example}), so every intermediate quantity is known exactly.
The 150 templates span five branches (chemical, civil, electrical, industrial and mechanical), 15 domains and 42 areas.
Every template passes automated integrity checks and an LLM screen, and three experts of its own branch certify it after working an instance by hand; each expert rejects all four defects planted among their branch's templates.
The evaluation set holds 2,250 instances, 15 per template, drawn with a private seed, so none was public when the models ran.
\ourdataset tests multi-step derivation on curriculum topics, not the breadth of engineering practice: its problems are examination-style by construction, as verifiable gold traces require, so ill-posed problems and real-world artefacts such as technical drawings are out of scope.

A model's response is scored deterministically wherever a check exists.
A final-answer check covers six answer kinds; milestone coverage is the share of the gold trace's intermediate quantities (milestones) that the response reaches, matched by value, in any order and after unit conversion; and an arithmetic check recomputes displayed calculations from their printed operands.
For milestone coverage, only the milestones that matching does not find, 11\% to 25\% of them across models, go to an LLM judge from a model family outside the evaluated ones.
Against step-level labels from 15 engineering experts on 300 responses of five models outside the evaluated set, and compared with six other evaluators including a panel of LLM judges and process reward models, the final-answer check agrees with the experts' final-answer verdict on over 90\% of responses, and milestone coverage reaches an F1 of 0.96 (0.92 without the judge).
When a response states the wrong rule but computes the right answer, however, no deterministic check notices, and the four LLM judges we tested catch 13\% to 37\% of such planted errors.

We evaluate eleven LLMs, eight open-weight and three closed, at their providers' default decoding settings, under which four write no reasoning tokens, with template-resampled intervals and corrected tests.
Final-answer accuracy ranges from 0.81 to 0.98, with the five strongest models within 0.01 of one another, closer than the evaluation set can resolve.
Milestone coverage (0.82 to 0.92) orders the models differently (Kendall's $\tau = 0.71$, 95\% CI 0.51 to 0.86) and separates one pair of the five strongest that final answers cannot.
Every model errs more often when the gold trace has six or more milestones than when it has one; in an expert reading of 160 wrong answers from four models, those on easy problems are mostly calculation slips, while wrong formulas or principles appear in 18\% of readings on harder problems against 3\% on easy ones.
For ten of the eleven models, the 90\% interval of the change under expert-checked rewording of 115 templates lies within five points, a bound on memorised wording rather than methods.
On a 450-instance subset, a Python tool moves neither closed model tested by more than five points, while medium reasoning effort lifts GPT-5.4 mini from 0.86 to 0.95.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We build and certify \ourdataset, 150 symbolic templates across five engineering branches whose gold traces are computed by code (\autoref{sec:benchmark}).
    \item We design an evaluator that is deterministic wherever a check exists, validate it against 15 experts' step-level labels, and measure what it misses (\autoref{sec:evaluation}).
    \item We evaluate eleven LLMs with template-resampled intervals and corrected tests, under rewording, reasoning effort, supplied equations and a code tool (\autoref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
