\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Engineering problems are solved by derivations whose intermediate quantities can be checked: a governing equation is chosen, the inputs are brought to consistent units, and each intermediate value feeds the next.
A correct final answer can still rest on flawed reasoning~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, more often on harder problems: in \textsc{ProcessBench}, the share of correct-answer solutions with a process error rises from 3.5\% on \textsc{GSM8K} to 51.8\% on olympiad-level problems~\citep{zheng2024processbench}.
A score on the answer alone also cannot show where a derivation goes wrong, or how far a wrong one got.

Yet engineering benchmarks for large language models (LLMs) mostly score the final answer.
General suites test engineering through multiple choice: the 7,892 engineering questions of \textsc{SuperGPQA} often require calculation but are scored on the option chosen~\citep{du2025supergpqa}.
Dedicated benchmarks typically cover one branch~\citep{syed2024transportbench, kevian2024controlbench, li2025eeebench}, and the multi-branch \textsc{EngiBench} grades answers with LLM evaluators~\citep{zhou2025engibench}.
The few that look past the answer have experts read the reasoning~\citep{syed2024transportbench, kevian2024controlbench}, have an LLM judge score it~\citep{zheng2026tpscalcbench, wasiq2026engvqa}, or check steps against computed values within one domain, as \textsc{ThermoQA} does for thermodynamics~\citep{duzkar2026thermoqa}.
Expert reading is costly to repeat, and LLM judges favour their own outputs and those of related models~\citep{panickssery2024selfrecognition, spiliopoulou2025playfavorites}.

We present \ourdataset, a symbolic benchmark for verifiable process supervision of engineering reasoning.
Following symbolic-template benchmarks in mathematics and finance~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic, xie2025finchain}, each of its 150 templates is executable code that samples physically grounded parameters, computes the answer, and writes the gold reasoning trace from the same computation (\autoref{fig:example}), so every intermediate quantity is known exactly.
The templates span five branches (chemical, civil, electrical, industrial and mechanical), 15 domains and 42 areas.
Every template passes automated integrity checks, is screened by three LLM judges from outside the evaluated model families, and is certified by three experts of its own branch after a hand check; each expert also rejected all four defects planted among their branch's templates.
The 2,250 evaluated instances, 15 per template, are drawn with a private seed, so no evaluated instance was public when the models ran.

A model's response is scored deterministically wherever a check exists.
A final-answer check covers six answer kinds; milestone coverage is the share of the gold trace's intermediate quantities (milestones) that the response reaches; and an arithmetic check recomputes displayed calculations from their printed operands.
Only the milestones that matching cannot find go to an LLM judge, drawn from a model family outside the evaluated ones.
We chose this design by comparing seven evaluators, among them a panel of LLM judges and three process reward models, against step-level labels from 15 engineering experts on 300 responses of five models outside the evaluated set: the final-answer check agrees with the experts' verdict on 93\% of responses, and milestone coverage reaches an F1 of 0.96.
The same study marks the limit of verification: when a response states the wrong rule but still computes the right answer, no deterministic check notices, and LLM judges catch at most about a third of such planted errors.

We evaluate eleven LLMs, eight open-weight and three closed, at their providers' default settings, with 95\% intervals and corrected tests that treat the template as the unit.
Final-answer accuracy ranges from 0.81 to 0.98, and the five strongest models lie within 0.01 of one another, closer than the evaluation set can resolve.
Milestone coverage orders the models differently (Kendall's $\tau = 0.71$), and responses with wrong answers still reach 46\% to 85\% of the milestones, against 10\% to 22\% by chance.
Every model's wrong-answer rate is highest on the longest derivations; in an expert error analysis, errors on easy problems are almost entirely calculation slips, while wrong formulas or principles appear mostly on intermediate and advanced problems.
On expert-checked paraphrases of 115 templates, a drop of five points or more from rewording alone is ruled out for ten of the eleven models.

\ourdataset is a depth-oriented evaluation of a curriculum-based subset of engineering.
Its problems are examination-style by construction, which is what verifiable gold traces require, so ill-posed problems and real-world artefacts such as technical drawings are out of scope.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We build and certify \ourdataset, 150 symbolic templates across five engineering branches whose gold traces are computed by the template code, with 2,250 instances drawn from a private seed (\autoref{sec:benchmark}).
    \item We design a deterministic-first evaluator, validate it against 15 experts' step-level labels beside six alternative designs, and report what no check can see (\autoref{sec:evaluation}).
    \item We evaluate eleven LLMs with template-level statistics, reporting tiers rather than a ranking at the top, what milestone coverage adds to the final answer, where models fail, a bound on the effect of rewording, and what reasoning effort, the governing equations and a code tool change (\autoref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
