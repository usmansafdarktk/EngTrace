\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

As LLMs enter specialized domains such as engineering, it becomes essential to evaluate how they
reason, not only what they answer.
Engineering reasoning requires physically grounded, multi-step problem solving, in which scientific
principles, quantitative modeling, and practical constraints must converge, and in which
intermediate steps are as consequential as the final result.
A correct final answer can still follow flawed reasoning~\citep{zheng2024processbench}, yet current
benchmarks rarely evaluate engineering reasoning beyond the answer.
General benchmarks such as \textsc{MMLU}~\citep{hendrycks2021mmlu},
\textsc{MATH}~\citep{hendrycks2021math}, and \textsc{HumanEval}~\citep{chen2021humaneval} test
knowledge, mathematical problem solving, or code generation in isolation, and most engineering
benchmarks grade the final answer rather than the steps that produce
it~\citep{du2025supergpqa, zhou2025engibench}.

To address these gaps, we introduce \ourdataset, a symbolic benchmark for verifiable process
supervision of engineering reasoning.
Following symbolic-template benchmarks in mathematics~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic} and
finance~\citep{xie2025finchain}, \ourdataset represents each problem as a template: executable
Python code that samples physically grounded parameters and computes the answer together with every
intermediate quantity of its gold reasoning trace.
The 150 templates span five engineering branches, 15 domains, and 42 areas and are stratified across
varying difficulty levels.
Every template undergoes automated integrity checks and an LLM screen before three domain experts
from its own branch certify it.
We draw the evaluated instances with a private seed, so that no instance was public when the models
ran.

Benchmarks that assess intermediate steps mostly rely on LLM judges or human graders, or cover a
single domain~\citep{syed2024transportbench, zheng2026tpscalcbench, duzkar2026thermoqa}; \ourdataset
instead uses its gold traces to score each response with an expert-validated evaluator that is
deterministic wherever a check exists.
The evaluator verifies final answers of six kinds, measures the coverage of the gold trace's
intermediate quantities, which we call \emph{milestones}, and recomputes the arithmetic that the
response displays.
For the residual milestones that deterministic matching cannot find, it consults an LLM judge from
outside the evaluated model families, because LLM judges can favor their own outputs and those of
related models~\citep{panickssery2024selfrecognition, li2026preferenceleakage}.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We introduce \ourdataset, 150 physically grounded symbolic templates spanning five
          engineering branches, 15 domains, and 42 areas and stratified by difficulty, each of which
          passes automated integrity checks, an LLM screen, and certification by three domain experts
          from its own branch.
    \item We design an evaluator that is deterministic wherever a check exists and validate it
          against step-level labels from domain experts: its final-answer check agrees with the
          experts on over 90\% of responses, and its milestone coverage reaches an F1 of 0.96.
    \item We evaluate eleven open-weights and closed LLMs on 2,250 template instances and show that
          final-answer accuracy alone cannot separate the strongest models, whereas milestone
          coverage and an expert error analysis reveal how far their derivations get and where they
          fail.
\end{itemize}
