\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

As LLMs enter specialized domains such as engineering, it becomes essential to evaluate how they
reason, not only what they answer.
Engineering problems are solved through \emph{derivations}, multi-step chains of reasoning that are
governed by physical laws and pass through checkable intermediate quantities to a final answer.
A correct final answer can still follow flawed reasoning, more so on harder problems: in competition
mathematics, as many as half of LLMs' correct answers rest on erroneous
steps~\citep{zheng2024processbench, zhang2025prmlessons}.
In engineering, the mismatch between answer and derivation runs both ways: LLMs reach correct
answers through flawed reasoning, and sound derivations miss the answer through a minor arithmetic
slip~\citep{syed2024transportbench, zheng2026tpscalcbench}.
Yet, most engineering benchmarks grade only the final
answer~\citep{du2025supergpqa, li2025eeebench, zhou2025engibench}, and those that assess
intermediate steps rely on LLM judges or human graders, or are limited to a single
domain~\citep{syed2024transportbench, zheng2026tpscalcbench, duzkar2026thermoqa}.
What the final answer cannot show is therefore how much of the correct derivation a wrong final
answer keeps, which intermediate quantities it misses, which slips a correct answer hides, and how
models with tied answers differ.

To address these gaps, we introduce \ourdataset, a symbolic benchmark for verifiable process
evaluation of engineering reasoning across five branches.
Following symbolic-template benchmarks in mathematics~\citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic} and
finance~\citep{xie2025finchain}, \ourdataset represents each problem as a template: Python code
that samples physically grounded parameters and computes the answer together with every
intermediate quantity of its gold reasoning trace (\autoref{fig:engtrace-template-cstr}).
The benchmark covers a depth-oriented subset of engineering, three core domains per branch, and its
problems are examination-style by construction, since a gold trace must be computable.
Our expert-validated evaluator deterministically checks a response's final answer, its coverage of
the gold trace's intermediate quantities (\emph{milestones}), and its displayed arithmetic, leaving
the milestones it cannot match to an LLM judge from outside the evaluated model families.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We present and release \ourdataset, whose 150 expert-certified templates across five
          branches generate each problem with a gold reasoning trace.
    \item We design an expert-validated evaluator that deterministically checks final answers,
          milestones, and displayed arithmetic, and leaves to an LLM judge only what these checks
          cannot decide.
    \item We evaluate 11 LLMs and show that final answers barely separate the strongest models,
          while their derivations differ.
          Domain experts trace most wrong answers to calculation slips, with conceptual errors
          rarer on Easy problems than on harder ones.
\end{itemize}
