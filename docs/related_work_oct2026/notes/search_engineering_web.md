## What changes for the Related Work

1. **The May draft's last engineering sentence no longer holds.** It says: "Evidently, existing benchmarks remain limited to outcome matching and fail to validate the physically grounded reasoning required for engineering." Since then, several engineering benchmarks have started grading the reasoning, not just the answer:
   - **EngVQA** (wasiq2026engvqa) scores 8 solution stages with an LLM judge checked against human grades.
   - **TPS-CalcBench** (zheng2026tpscalcbench) scores an 8-dimension reasoning rubric with a judge audited by an expert.
   - **ThermoQA** (duzkar2026thermoqa) scores weighted intermediate values against values computed with CoolProp.
   - **CircuChain** (ravishankara2026circuchain) uses solvers and SPICE, then an LLM judge assigns each failure to a cause.
   - **StructureClaw** and **EngiWorld** check intermediate artifacts.
   - **VEHBench** scores each design stage against an analytical physics model.
2. **EngTrace's design choices each have precedents now.** None of them is unique on its own:
   - Private-seed generators: the Power Systems Agent Benchmark, which uses them as a contamination defence.
   - Gold intermediate values computed by code: ThermoQA.
   - Stage-wise judges validated against humans: EngVQA and TPS-CalcBench.
   - Multi-branch scope: ERI and EngiBench.

   What no paper I found combines is all of the following:
   - five branches;
   - generation from parameterised templates with a private seed;
   - deterministic checks of gold intermediate values derived from code (milestones plus per-claim arithmetic);
   - a residual judge validated against 15 experts' step labels;
   - paraphrase and template-exploitation probes;
   - template-level bootstrap intervals;
   - a comparison with off-the-shelf process reward models.

   I would claim the combination, not the individual pieces.
3. **The sentence on generalist multiple-choice suites still holds**, but the paragraph should add ERI (naser2026eri):
   - It is a nine-discipline suite of 57,750 records.
   - The records and reference answers are generated by an LLM, and scoring is by a panel of LLM judges.
   - It cites EngTrace as an "entirely synthetic" benchmark.

   Its references being generated by a model is the clean contrast with EngTrace's gold answers, which are computed by code.
4. **Material for the judge-validity paragraph.** Each of these reports how far an engineering judge agrees with humans:
   - TPS-CalcBench: weighted κ of 0.68 to 0.82 between judge and expert. The judge is lenient on calculation accuracy and on physical plausibility.
   - PSE-Bench (sukpancharoen2026psebench): a five-judge ensemble, with a human check reporting +0.85 leniency on a 0 to 7 scale. This is from the abstract snippet only.
   - EngVQA: Pearson 0.975 against 9 students' grades.

   Several other new benchmarks grade answers with an LLM judge and report no human agreement:
   - MechReason uses Kimi K2.6.
   - AstroMind uses DeepSeek-R1.
   - CircuitSense uses Gemini.
   - MSQA uses GPT-4.
5. **Two of the May references are wrong.** The ElecBench first author is Zhou, not Cheng. The "Liu et al., 2025" CIRCUIT entry merges two different papers. See "Updates to existing citations".

### Must-cite (5)

**wasiq2026engvqa**: *Do VLMs Reason Like Engineers? A Benchmark and a Stage-wise Evaluation* (Wasiq, Tawseeq, Bangde, Roy; arXiv 2606.10833; 9 June 2026).
- **What it is.** EngVQA is a static multimodal benchmark of 696 textbook-style problems with technical diagrams. They cover five mechanical-engineering subjects: fluid mechanics, heat and mass transfer, dynamics, mechanics of materials and thermodynamics.
- **Evaluation.** Its evaluator, EngJudge, asks for a structured step-wise solution. An LLM then scores eight dependent stages, from diagram interpretation and assumptions through equation selection and algebra to the final answer. It penalises missing stages and models how errors propagate.
- **Human validation.** Nine engineering students graded solutions; agreement with EngJudge was Pearson 0.975, MAE 0.67 on a 10-point scale.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** It directly contradicts the May "outcome matching" sentence. EngTrace's difference is now in how steps are checked:
  - intermediate values derived from code, checked deterministically;
  - a residual judge validated against 15 experts;
  - generated, text-only instances across five branches.

**zheng2026tpscalcbench**: *TPS-CalcBench* (Zheng, Qiao, Huang; arXiv 2604.17966; 20 April 2026).
- **What it is.** A diagnostic benchmark of closed-form calculations in hypersonic aerothermodynamics, that is, engineering of thermal protection systems. It was built from Anderson's textbook through a human-AI pipeline: 420 core items and 810 pre-gating items, from 4,560 raw items. It is static.
- **Evaluation.** There are two tracks:
  - result accuracy, by bands of relative error;
  - reasoning quality, on an 8-dimension rubric scored by a calibrated LLM judge.
- **Human validation.** One domain expert audited 62 items. Weighted κ between judge and expert was 0.68 to 0.82 per dimension, and the judge was slightly lenient on calculation accuracy and physical plausibility. The benchmark is built to expose "right answer, wrong reasoning".
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** It contradicts the outcome-matching claim for engineering calculation. It also gives the judge-validity paragraph a second set of judge-expert agreement figures beside EngTrace's own.

**trashchenkov2026powersystemsagent**: *Power Systems Agent Benchmark: Executable Evaluation of AI Agents in Electric Power Engineering* (Trashchenkov; arXiv 2606.20950; 18 June 2026, v2 2 July 2026).
- **What it is.** An executable benchmark of 41 task families across eight power-engineering areas: power flow and protection, stability, microgrids, reliability, power quality, forecasting and others.
- **Evaluation.** Agents return structured solutions. Deterministic "surrogate" evaluators check feasibility, score and constraint violations.
- **Static or generated.** The public split is released. Held-out cases are generated on demand by a generator for each task family from private seeds, explicitly to limit contamination. Public and held-out performance are reported as broadly consistent.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** One engineering branch now independently uses private-seed generators. EngTrace should present its contamination resistance as shared practice, citing this paper with ABench-Physics and GSM-Symbolic, and claim novelty for the multi-branch, trace-level combination instead.

**naser2026eri**: *Engineering Reasoning and Instruction (ERI) Benchmark* (Naser et al.; Computers & Industrial Engineering 2026, doi:10.1016/j.cie.2026.112333; arXiv 2603.02239; 16 February 2026).
- **What it is.** A dataset of 57,750 records built from a taxonomy: nine fields (civil, mechanical, electrical, chemical, environmental, aerospace, materials, fire, industrial), 55 subdomains, seven intent types and three difficulty tiers.
- **Static or generated.** Records and reference answers were generated by an LLM (GPT-5 Mini) under the taxonomy. So the set is synthetic, but nothing is computed.
- **Evaluation.** Seven models were scored on a stratified 10% subset by a panel of LLM judges, on a five-point rubric. A "convergent validation protocol" bounds the rate of hallucinated references at 1.7%.
- **It cites EngTrace** as [29], an "entirely synthetic" benchmark.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** This is the most direct multi-branch competitor. The contrast to draw is model-generated references with judge-only scoring, against EngTrace's gold traces derived from code and its deterministic checks.

**duzkar2026thermoqa**: *ThermoQA: A Three-Tier Benchmark for Evaluating Thermodynamic Reasoning in Large Language Models* (Düzkar; arXiv 2604.19758; v1 25 March 2026).
- **What it is.** 293 open-ended engineering-thermodynamics problems in three tiers: 110 property lookups, 101 component analyses and 82 full-cycle analyses. The fluids are water, R-134a and air with variable specific heat. Ground truth is computed with CoolProp 7.2.0. The set is static.
- **Evaluation.** Tiers 2 and 3 are scored step by step:
  - intermediate values are extracted (regex, then gpt-4.1-mini);
  - each is checked against the computed value to within ±2% or ±0.5;
  - steps are weighted by engineering importance.

  Six models were run three times each, and run-to-run variation is reported.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** This is the closest precedent for EngTrace's milestone coverage. The revision must acknowledge it and draw the difference: multi-branch scope, generated instances, per-claim arithmetic checks, a judge validated by experts, and paraphrase tests.

**PRIME** (arXiv 2602.11570) also came up in this search, and I would rate it must-cite for the PRM comparison. I left it out of this file because `process_supervision.json` already lists it under the same key, `wang2026prime`, and a duplicate key here would shadow that entry in full fetch runs.
- **What it is.** 2,530 college-level STEM samples annotated by experts. It shows that model-based verifiers miss flawed derivations behind correct final answers.
- **Caveat.** Its results table has Math, Physics, Chemistry and Biology columns, so its engineering share is unclear.

### Should-cite (16)

**ravishankara2026circuchain**: *CircuChain: Disentangling Competence and Compliance in LLM Circuit Analysis* (Ravishankara; IEEE SoutheastCon 2026; arXiv 2602.15037; 29 January 2026).
- **What it is.** 100 circuit-analysis tasks per model, built as counterbalanced Control/Trap pairs over five canonical topologies. The pairs vary sign conventions, current directions and polarity definitions.
- **Evaluation.** Symbolic solvers and SPICE simulation verify the answers. For failed subtasks, a GPT-5 judge with the reference in hand assigns one cause: convention, physics, arithmetic or hallucination.
- **Finding.** The strongest model reasons correctly about the physics but breaks the requested conventions under Trap conditions.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** An electrical-branch example of attributing errors to steps, which supports the process paragraph. Its perturbation probes are akin to EngTrace's template-exploitation tests.

**wang2026mechreason**: *MechReason: Benchmarking Multi-Image Multi-Hop Reasoning in Mechanical Engineering* (Wang et al.; arXiv 2609.16012; v1 21 August 2026).
- **What it is.** 12k QA pairs with 21k images (charts, drawings, CAD, micrographs, simulation images). They come from mechanical-engineering research papers via a four-stage pipeline:
  - claims are decomposed into premises, reasoning, conclusions and evidence;
  - the verification information is masked to prevent shortcuts.

  Every item carries an annotated reasoning chain.
- **Evaluation.** Answers are matched by an LLM judge (Kimi K2.6); the best accuracy is 62.89%. The set is static and multimodal.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** A new mechanical-branch benchmark that the May draft could not cite. The reasoning chains are annotated but not used to score steps, so EngTrace's step checking stays distinct.

**gao2026engiworld**: *EngiWorld: What Can Frontier Agents Deliver in Professional Engineering Environments?* (Gao et al.; arXiv 2609.37686; 29 September 2026).
- **What it is.** 1,301 expert-curated agent tasks across CAD, CAE, CAM, BIM, EDA and 3D visualisation, run on 26 professional software platforms through GUI and command line.
- **Evaluation.** A suite of domain verifiers checks geometric validity, physical feasibility and rule compliance of final and intermediate artifacts. Design tasks are scored continuously. The best EngiScore is 44.3, and 3.6% of multi-software attempts succeed.
- **Static or generated.** The task set is static. The abstract reports no agreement between the metric and humans.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** It updates the "software proficiency / design generation" sentence (FEABench, EngDesign) with the largest current suite of engineering-agent tasks. It tests tool use, not written reasoning.

**qin2026structureclaw**: *StructureClaw: Traceable LLM Agents and an Executable Benchmark for Structural Engineering Workflows* (Qin et al.; arXiv 2607.14896; 16 July 2026, v2 3 August 2026).
- **What it is.** An agent workbench plus StructureClaw-Bench: 150 controlled structural-engineering scenarios covering standard workflows, interactive robustness and multimodal model reconstruction.
- **Evaluation.** A trial succeeds only if every required assertion passes. That means either one-to-one matching of the structural model and numerical agreement with frozen solver responses, or correct clarification with safe non-execution.
- **Finding.** Generic execution passed the model-artifact check 87.0% of the time but succeeded end to end only 22.0% of the time.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** Civil and structural evidence for EngTrace's argument that plausible outputs can hide broken intermediate work. It is agentic and static.

**wan2025som1k**: *SoM-1K: A Thousand-Problem Benchmark Dataset for Strength of Materials* (Wan et al.; arXiv 2509.21079; 25 September 2025, v2 1 August 2026).
- **What it is.** 1,065 annotated strength-of-materials problems with schematics.
- **Evaluation.** Eight LLMs and VLMs were run with either expert-written descriptions of the images ("DoI") or the images themselves. The best accuracy is 56.6%. Scoring is on final answers, plus an error analysis. The set is static.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** An example of a siloed mechanical/civil benchmark, which supports the "narrow sub-disciplines" sentence. Its low scores also support EngTrace's motivation.

**kevian2024controlbench**: *Capabilities of Large Language Models in Control Engineering: A Benchmark Study on GPT-4, Claude 3 Opus, and Gemini 1.0 Ultra* (Kevian et al.; arXiv 2404.03647; 4 April 2024).
- **What it is.** ControlBench: 147 undergraduate control problems, from Schaum's outline and from Michigan and UIUC courses. GPT-4, Claude 3 Opus and Gemini 1.0 Ultra answered them.
- **Evaluation.** A panel of human experts graded accuracy and reasoning. A multiple-choice subset, ControlBench-C, allows automatic scoring. The set is static.
- **Authors.** Same group as the cited TransportBench and EngDesign (Hu, Seiler, Dullerud, Qin).
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** The standard control-engineering reference that reviewers will expect next to TransportBench. It also shows costly human grading as the alternative to verifiable checks.

**akbari2025circuitsense**: *CircuitSense: A Hierarchical MLLM Benchmark Bridging Visual Comprehension and Symbolic Reasoning in Engineering Design Process* (Akbari et al.; arXiv 2509.22339; 26 September 2025, v2 28 February 2026).
- **What it is.** 8,006+ problems, from component schematics to system block diagrams, covering perception, analysis and design.
- **Static or generated.** Generated by a hierarchical synthetic pipeline (grid-based schematic generator, block-diagram generator) with symbolic equation labels derived automatically.
- **Evaluation.** Numeric and short answers are graded by an LLM judge (Gemini). Closed models score above 85% on perception but below 19% on symbolic derivation.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** A generated-instance engineering benchmark: it supports EngTrace's "programmatic generation" framing and shows that framing is not unique. It also shows derivation, not recognition, is the bottleneck.

**chen2026optengine**: *OPT-Engine: Benchmarking the Limits of LLMs in Optimization Modeling via Complexity Scaling* (Chen et al.; ICML 2026, PMLR 306; arXiv 2601.19924; 9 January 2026).
- **What it is.** An extensible generator over ten canonical operations-research problem classes, with complexity controllable from LP to MIP. The instances are generated.
- **What it tests.** Three modes: chain-of-thought in plain text, text reasoning with tools, and reasoning with an integrated solver.
- **Findings.** Plain-text reasoning loses robustness as complexity grows. Constraint formulation is the bottleneck for the solver-integrated mode.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** The OR/industrial analogue of EngTrace's parameterised generation. Its arithmetic failures in text reasoning also support the per-claim arithmetic check.

**guan2026supchainbench**: *SupChain-Bench: Benchmarking Large Language Models for Real-World Supply Chain Management* (Guan, Liu, Cao; Findings of ACL 2026, pp. 7526–7550; arXiv 2602.07342; 7 February 2026).
- **What it is.** 435 supply-chain knowledge questions (141 multiple choice, 147 single choice, 147 true/false) plus long-horizon tool orchestration grounded in standard operating procedures. It is evaluated on execution reliability, and the paper also proposes SupChain-ReAct. The set is static.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** An industrial-engineering benchmark at an ACL venue. EngTrace's industrial branch should be positioned against it: knowledge plus agentic tool use, versus quantitative reasoning traces.

**li2025wirelessmathbench**: *WirelessMathBench: A Mathematical Modeling Benchmark for LLMs in Wireless Communications* (Li et al.; Findings of ACL 2025; arXiv 2505.14354; 20 May 2025).
- **What it is.** 587 questions curated from 40 wireless-communications papers: multiple choice, and partial or full equation completion under physical and dimensional constraints. The set is static.
- **Results.** The best model (DeepSeek-R1) averages 38.05%, and reaches 7.83% on full completion.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** Covers communications and signal processing at an ACL venue. Equation completion is a different target from EngTrace's numeric checks of steps.

**colle2025telemath**: *TeleMath: A Benchmark for Large Language Models in Telecom Mathematical Problem Solving* (Colle et al.; arXiv 2506.10674; 12 June 2025).
- **What it is.** 500 telecom maths problems with numeric answers, covering signal processing, network optimisation and performance analysis.
- **Static or generated.** A QnA generation pipeline expanded seed problems written by subject-matter experts.
- **Evaluation.** pass@1 and cons@16 on final answers only.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** A semi-synthetic electrical-branch benchmark that still scores only outcomes. It supports the "outcome-only" description of most domain benchmarks.

**liu2026astromind**: *AstroMind: A High-Fidelity Benchmark for Spacecraft Behavior Reasoning Based on Large Language Models* (Liu, Yang, Hu, Li; arXiv 2605.24573; 23 May 2026).
- **What it is.** High-fidelity astrodynamics simulations with realistic sensing noise, turned into verifiable problems about spacecraft behaviour: intent inference, manoeuvre-parameter estimation and threat assessment. The instances are derived from simulation.
- **Evaluation.** Scoring combines semantic correctness, relative error on parsed quantities, and reasoning quality judged by a fixed DeepSeek-R1 judge. Only open-weight models were tested.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** It extends the cited APBench line in aerospace and is another engineering benchmark with generated instances.

**somasekharan2025cfdllmbench**: *CFDLLMBench: A Benchmark Suite for Evaluating Large Language Models in Computational Fluid Dynamics* (Somasekharan et al.; Journal of Data-centric Machine Learning Research 2026; arXiv 2509.20374; 19 September 2025).
- **What it is.** Three static components:
  - CFDQuery: graduate CFD knowledge;
  - CFDCodeBench: numerical code written from problem descriptions;
  - FoamBench: OpenFOAM case set-up from natural language.
- **Evaluation.** Code executability, solution accuracy and numerical convergence.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** The main simulation-agent benchmark to cite beside FEABench. It belongs in the "software proficiency" sentence.

**sukpancharoen2026psebench**: *Benchmarking AI prompt performance of large language models across chemical process systems engineering domains using multi-judge evaluation* ("PSE-Bench"; Sukpancharoen, Srinophakun; Chemical Engineering Journal Advances 27 (2026) 101375; open access, CC BY-NC-ND).
- **Source caveat.** The description below comes from the abstract as shown in search results. The publisher page was blocked, so I have not checked it against the full text.
- **What it is.** 200 open-ended, practice-oriented questions across four domains of process systems engineering: modelling and simulation, optimisation, machine learning for chemical processes, and process design. The set is static.
- **Evaluation.** Five commercial LLMs answered. Five independent LLM judges scored the answers against 7-element rubrics. Human validation found the judges lenient by +0.85 on the 0 to 7 scale.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** A chemical-engineering benchmark that measures LLM-judge leniency. Useful for the judge-validity paragraph, next to EngTrace's expert-validated residual judge.

**silwal2026pecivilbench**: *PE Civil Bench: Advancing Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation Performance of Large Language Models on Exam Problems and Automated Component Design* (Silwal, Gao, Varghese, Hoskere; Computer-Aided Civil and Infrastructure Engineering 49 (2026) 100126; open access).
- **Source caveat.** From the abstract snippet and Crossref metadata only; I could not read the full text.
- **What it is.** A standardised benchmark of Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) and PE Civil exam questions for frontier and open-weight LLMs. It is combined with an agentic retrieval pipeline for automated component design that complies with building codes. The exam items are static and are presumably scored on the answer, which I have not verified.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** The current reference for professional-licensure exams. It supports the claim that exam-style benchmarks test answers, not traces.

**chen2025circuithomeworkbench**: *Benchmarking Large Language Models on Homework Assessment in Circuit Analysis* (Chen et al.; arXiv 2506.06390; 5 June 2025).
- **What it is.** A dataset of reference and student solutions to circuit-analysis homework, in LaTeX.
- **What it tests.** GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4o and Llama 3 70B acting as graders, on five criteria: completeness, method, final answer, arithmetic error and units. The set is static.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** This is the engineering-education grading line. Its criteria parallel EngTrace's step labels (arithmetic, units). It comes from the same group as the paper that the May reference list cites as "Liu et al., 2025" (see below), so the revision may cite one or both.

**loubet2025thermodynamics**: *Using Large Language Models for Solving Thermodynamic Problems* (Loubet et al.; Computers & Chemical Engineering 204 (2026) 109333; arXiv 2502.05195; 27 January 2025).
- **What it is.** 22 simple and advanced chemical-engineering thermodynamics problems, answered by five LLMs. The set is static.
- **Evaluation.** Trained human experts graded the answers like exam scripts, and repeated runs measured consistency.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** A small precedent for experts grading full solutions. Its small scale is one argument for EngTrace's automated checks validated by experts.

**cheung2025msqa**: *MSQA* (Cheung et al.; arXiv 2505.23982; 29 May 2025).
- **What it is.** 1,757 graduate materials-science questions across seven sub-fields, in explanatory and true/false formats. The set is static.
- **Evaluation.** GPT-4 judges the long-form answers.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** Covers the materials branch; another case of LLM-judge scoring with no check of the process.

**su2026vehbench**: *VEHBench: A Stage-Local Diagnostic Benchmark for LLM-Assisted Vibration Energy Harvester Design* (Su, Luo, Hu; arXiv 2607.18181; 20 July 2026).
- **What it is.** 763 design tasks for vibration energy harvesters, grounded in the literature. The set is static.
- **Evaluation.** An analytical physics model scores the tasks deterministically across four design roles: specification triage, verifier-guided search, recovery from a corrupted state, and policy-conditioned selection.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** Another 2026 benchmark that rejects scoring only the final artifact and diagnoses each stage instead.

**ye2025llms4all**: *LLMs4All: A Review of Large Language Models Across Academic Disciplines* (Ye et al.; arXiv 2509.19580; v5 23 November 2025).
- **What it is.** A 304-page review of LLMs across disciplines, with sections on mechanical, chemical, civil and electrical engineering.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** An optional single survey pointer; I found no dedicated survey of LLMs across all of engineering on arXiv.

**neema2025eagievaluation**: *On the Evaluation of Engineering Artificial General Intelligence* (Neema et al.; arXiv 2505.10653; 15 May 2025).
- **What it is.** A framework, built on Bloom's taxonomy, for evaluating "engineering AGI": physical systems and control, excluding software. Its questions range from methodological knowledge to open design problems, and it supports structured artifacts such as CAD and SysML models.
- **Evidence.** A framework paper; the abstract reports no results on a dataset.
- **Bearing on EngTrace.** A framing citation that pairs with the cited "Toward Engineering AGI" (EngDesign).

## What I could not verify

  - AECBench, Civil-Eval and ChemEBench;

Each run loads `MANIFEST.json` at start and rewrites it at the end, so a concurrent run can drop another run's records. This already happened to `wang2026prime`. My 28 records were present at 17:2x UTC. If any go missing, re-running the `--file engineering_web.json` command restores them from the PDFs on disk.
