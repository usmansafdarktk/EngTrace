\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reasoning benchmarks and what they score.}
Mathematical and coding benchmarks such as GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, MATH \citep{hendrycks2021math}, HumanEval \citep{chen2021humaneval}, MBPP \citep{austin2021mbpp} and SWE-bench \citep{jimenez2024swebench} score a final answer or a test outcome, and broad suites such as MMLU \citep{hendrycks2021mmlu} and SuperGPQA \citep{du2025supergpqa} score a chosen option.
Yet a correct final answer can follow flawed reasoning \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}, increasingly so on harder problems: in ProcessBench, the share of correct-answer solutions that contain a process error rises from 3.5\% on GSM8K to 51.8\% on Omni-MATH \citep{zheng2024processbench}.
Most physics benchmarks likewise grade final answers or expressions \citep{xu2025ugphysics,qiu2025phybench,zhang2025abenchphysics,wang2023scibench}, while PhysReason \citep{zhang2025physreason}, PRISM-Physics \citep{zhao2026prismphysics} and HiPhO \citep{yu2026hipho} score steps against reference solutions, with an LLM judge, rule-based formula matching, and official marking schemes applied by an LLM judge, respectively.

\paragraph{Generated instances and contamination.}
Static test sets saturate \citep{ott2022saturation,akhtar2026benchmarksaturation} and leak into training data \citep{deng2024contamination,zhang2024gsm1k}, and neither private test sets nor templating has measurably slowed saturation \citep{akhtar2026benchmarksaturation}.
Functional, symbolic and perturbed variants of existing problems test whether a model has memorised the original instance or its method \citep{srivastava2024functionalbench,mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic,gulati2024putnamaxiom,huang2025mathperturb}, although GSM-Symbolic's drops do not always survive re-analysis \citep{dlugosz2026gsmsymbolicreeval}, randomly sampled variants can overstate robustness \citep{mondorf2026lpds}, and on multiple-choice benchmarks paraphrasing lowers absolute scores while leaving model rankings relatively stable \citep{lunardi2025paraphraserobustness}.
Generation has also been paired with gold solutions: HARDMath produces applied-mathematics problems with numerically validated step-by-step solutions \citep{fan2024hardmath}; FinChain pairs each templated financial problem with an executable gold reasoning chain and scores a model's steps against it \citep{xie2025finchain}; and SymPyBench \citep{imani2025sympybench}, AtmosSci-Bench \citep{li2025atmosscibench} and Sci-$\rho$ \citep{azmi2026scirho} instantiate science problems from code templates.

\paragraph{Engineering benchmarks.}
General suites test engineering mostly through multiple choice; SuperGPQA's 7,892 engineering questions often require calculation but are scored on the option chosen \citep{du2025supergpqa}.
Dedicated benchmarks typically cover one branch: transportation \citep{syed2024transportbench}, control \citep{kevian2024controlbench}, power dispatch \citep{zhou2024elecbench}, electrical and electronics engineering \citep{li2025eeebench}, circuits \citep{skelic2025circuit}, astrodynamics \citep{wu2025apbench}, thermodynamics \citep{duzkar2026thermoqa}, thermal-protection design \citep{zheng2026tpscalcbench} or operations research \citep{mostajabdaveh2025orqa,chen2026optengine}.
Multi-branch benchmarks grade answers with LLM judges: EngiBench \citep{zhou2025engibench} with two LLM evaluators, plus rubrics for its open-ended level, and ERI \citep{naser2026eri} against reference answers that an LLM wrote along with the questions.

Other work evaluates design \citep{guo2025engdesign}, multiphysics simulation \citep{mudur2025feabench} and questions from industrial practice \citep{heesch2025realworld}, the last questioning evaluations built from examination materials whose correctness is easily verifiable.
A few benchmarks look past the answer: experts examined the reasoning in TransportBench and ControlBench, LLM judges score it in ElecBench, TPS-CalcBench and EngVQA \citep{wasiq2026engvqa}, and ThermoQA scores property values and weighted solution steps against values computed with a thermodynamic property library.
Fewer generate their instances: CIRCUIT instantiates circuit templates with several numerical setups, OPT-Engine \citep{chen2026optengine} generates optimisation problems of controlled complexity, and a power-systems agent benchmark draws held-out cases from privately seeded generators \citep{trashchenkov2026psab}.
To our knowledge, none combines generated instances across engineering branches with gold traces, deterministic checks of intermediate values, and an evaluator validated against experts' step-level labels.

\paragraph{Process supervision and the validity of evaluators.}
Outcome supervision can match process supervision on final-answer error, but correct reasoning steps required process-based supervision or reward models that emulate it \citep{uesato2022processoutcome}.
Process reward models (PRMs) learn from human step labels \citep{lightman2023verify} or from labels estimated by rollouts \citep{wang2023mathshepherd}, which are noisy \citep{zhang2025prmlessons}, and are evaluated on human-annotated first-error benchmarks in mathematics \citep{zheng2024processbench,song2025prmbench}; PRMs trained on mathematics perform poorly in other domains \citep{zeng2025versaprm}.
Beyond mathematics, PRIME tests whether verifiers catch answers that the reasoning does not support, in college-level STEM including engineering \citep{wang2026prime}, and ChemCoTBench-V2 checks chemistry steps with deterministic rules \citep{guo2026chemcotbenchv2}.
LLM judges are the common alternative, but they favour their own outputs and those of related models \citep{zheng2023mtbench,panickssery2024selfrecognition,spiliopoulou2025playfavorites,li2026preferenceleakage}, even on objective rubric items \citep{pombal2026rubricselfpreference}; their errors are correlated across models \citep{goel2025greatmodels,kim2025correlatederrors}, which limits what a panel of judges \citep{verga2024poll} gains by voting; and on difficult objective comparisons many do little better than chance \citep{tan2025judgebench}, agreeing with experts mainly on questions they can answer themselves unless given a correct reference \citep{krumdick2026nofreelabels}.
Rule-based checkers avoid these biases but can reject correct answers given in unexpected formats \citep{huang2026verifierrobustness,ansari2026physicsregrading}.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
EngTrace brings these lines together.
As in GSM-Symbolic and FinChain, symbolic templates generate the instances, here across five engineering branches and from a private seed, and as in FinChain the gold trace comes from the code that computes the answer.
Intermediate values are checked deterministically against that trace and arithmetic by recomputation; an LLM judge from a model family outside the evaluated roster decides only what these checks leave open, given each milestone's expected value; and the full evaluator, with off-the-shelf PRMs as baselines, is validated against step labels from 15 domain experts.
Expert-checked paraphrases test whether models exploit template wording.

\paragraph{Further related work.}
\textit{Engineering.} Recent benchmarks extend coverage to agents working in professional engineering software \citep{gao2026engiworld}, strength of materials \citep{wan2025som1k}, circuit analysis from schematics with synthetically generated problems \citep{akbari2025circuitsense}, telecommunications mathematics \citep{colle2025telemath}, executable structural analysis \citep{qin2026structureclaw}, finite-element programming \citep{mohammadzadeh2025fembench}, computational fluid dynamics \citep{somasekharan2026cfdllmbench}, civil-engineering licensure questions \citep{silwal2026pecivilbench} and process systems engineering judged by several LLMs \citep{sukpancharoen2026psebench}.
\textit{Science.} OlympiadBench pairs olympiad problems with expert step-by-step solutions but scores final answers \citep{he2024olympiadbench}; SciDA randomises numerical parameters at each evaluation \citep{zhou2025scida}; NEWTON generates physical-commonsense questions from templates \citep{wang2023newton}.
\textit{Contamination.} VeRA turns benchmark items into executable task families \citep{cheng2026vera}, GSM-Plus perturbs GSM8K problems \citep{li2024gsmplus}, \citet{chen2025staticdynamicsurvey} survey the move from static to dynamic benchmarks, and \citet{xiao2026contaminationrankings} measure contamination as the gap between original and paraphrased items.
\textit{Step verification.} Error-detection benchmarks extend beyond mathematics \citep{tyen2023bigbenchmistake,zeng2024mrben,he2025deltabench,sun2026lsrben}, physics now has a process reward model \citep{dong2026physprm}, and GenPRM verifies each step with code \citep{zhao2025genprm}; reference-free step metrics include ROSCOE \citep{golovneva2022roscoe} and ReasonEval \citep{xia2024reasoneval}.
Closest to EngTrace's milestones, \citet{wang2026milestoneoracles} grade mathematical reasoning against milestone roadmaps with a deterministic symbolic verifier, though their milestones are written by a teacher model rather than computed by the problem's own code.
\textit{Judges.} \citet{thakur2025judgingjudges} and \citet{ye2025justiceprejudice} document the unreliability and biases of LLM judges; judges accept valid outputs but reject few invalid ones \citep{jain2025agreeableness}, their agreement with human markers in physics depends on the task \citep{yeadon2026physicsjudge}, and they should be validated against human judgments before use \citep{bavaresco2025llmsinsteadofhumans}.
\citet{roytburg2026narcissists} re-examine self-preference findings. \citet{jung2025trustescalate} escalate only uncertain items to stronger judges, and \citet{huang2026verifierrobustness} ask a model only about answers a rule-based verifier rejects, the pattern EngTrace's residual judge follows.
