1. Judge overlap — the AI Tribunal certifies templates and adjudicates reasoning, yet GPT-5 is also an evaluated model and Claude Opus 4.5 / Gemini 3 are close siblings of evaluated models.
Response
We thank the reviewer for raising this. It isolates a testable mechanism, and we address it in three parts. 
First, the overlap is not uniform: GPT-5 is an exact judge/judged match, while Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 are version-siblings of, but not identical to, Claude Opus 4.7 and Gemini {3.1 Pro, 3 Pro} (§3.3, Lines 220–223; §5.1, Lines 399–406).
Second, existing evidence bears on this specific concern. The human-alignment validation set (Appendix M, Lines 1375–1386) includes Gemini 3 Pro, one of the models with the closest judge-family relationship, and the Tribunal-based pipeline still tracked independent human expert judgments closely on this sample (ρ = 0.632 vs. 0.325 for standard metrics; Appendix M.2, Table 12, Lines 1417–1426), with no indication of inflated favorability toward this particular model.
Third, removing the Tribunal from the pipeline degrades human alignment rather than improving it (ρ = 0.632 → 0.411; Table 12), indicating the Tribunal's net effect is to improve agreement with an external, non-model source of ground truth rather than to amplify model self-preference.
We do however commit to a direct test of the mechanism itself for the camera-ready version. For GPT-5 and, separately, for the Claude and Gemini families, we will recompute Tier 2 verification with the corresponding judge excluded from the panel, using majority consensus over the two remaining judges and the existing conservative tie-break rule on disagreement. We will report the resulting shift in Reasoning F1 against two controls on the same sample: a placebo condition (a non-family judge excluded instead) and the untouched models (DeepSeek, the open-weights models, and the math-specialized models, none of which share a family with any Tribunal judge), which serve as a built-in check that any shift is attributable to family overlap specifically rather than to panel size alone.
2. No variance or confidence-interval reporting across the 15 seeded instances per template.
Response
We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. The 15 seeded instances per template were already generated for this purpose (§5.2, Lines 428–431); in response to this review we ran the corresponding variance and significance analysis on the existing evaluation data.
We would note one clarification on how to read the resulting numbers: the per-template standard deviations reported below describe seed-to-seed variability within a single template, not the uncertainty of the Table 1 aggregate score itself. Each Table 1 figure is a mean over 90 templates, not a single template, so the aggregate is considerably more stable than the per-template spread alone would suggest.
Across the 25 models with complete records, mean per-template standard deviation is 20.1 pp on Final Answer Accuracy (range 9.8–25.7 pp) and 15.6 pp on Recovered F1 (range 4.4–18.3 pp); we will add this as a column to Table 1 in the camera-ready version.

5. Causal claims under-supported: the "math pre-training does not transfer" conclusion is drawn from a small set of math-enhanced 7B models, confounding specialization with scale and model family.
Response
We thank the reviewer for this observation. The underperformance pattern is directionally consistent across all four math-specialized models in Table 1: each scores well below general-purpose models of comparable or smaller scale, for example Mathstral 7B (19.70% FAC) and WizardMath 7B (8.22%) against Qwen2.5-7B (35.63%) and Llama 3.1 8B (23.70%).
Table 1 also contains one controlled, same-family, same-scale comparison directly relevant to this concern: Qwen2.5-Math-7B (FAC 23.04%) underperforms its own base model, Qwen2.5-7B (FAC 35.63%), at identical architecture and parameter count, speaking against the gap being a scale or family artifact. Appendix U further shows Mathstral 7B and WizardMath 7B have conceptual error rates (58.0% and 69.5%; Table 18) well above any frontier model, with Mathstral's errors concentrated in Formula/Principle selection rather than arithmetic (§U.1, Lines 1749-1757), pointing toward a knowledge gap rather than a scale confound.
We will extend this with branch-level FAC for all four math-specialized models, including MetaMath-7B, for the camera-ready version.

1. Breadth is limited to three engineering branches, which is still far from the scope of real-world engineering reasoning.
Response
We thank the reviewer for this observation. The three branches were selected as a principled starting point rather than an arbitrary limit: each is a core, well-defined discipline with established curricula and textbook grounding suitable for the symbolic template pipeline that verifiability depends on (§1, Lines 85-91). We commit to extending the taxonomy with a fourth engineering branch (for example Civil or Aerospace Engineering, to be finalized in consultation with domain experts), authored through the same textbook-grounded template process described in (§3.1, Lines 179-193) and Appendix C, for the camera-ready version. 
2. The benchmark is generated entirely from synthetic symbolic templates, creating a gap with real-world engineering cases.
Response
We thank the reviewer for this observation. The synthetic design is a deliberate tradeoff rather than an oversight: verifiable, gold-standard reasoning traces, the paper's central contribution, are only guaranteed through controlled symbolic generation (§1, Lines 85-91), and Appendix G shows that templates vary structurally, not just parametrically, the sampled parameters can change the governing equation and required reasoning path entirely, not just the numbers involved.
Linguistic and contextual diversity, paraphrased problem wording and non-textual inputs such as diagrams or schematics, is a separate axis from this structural diversity, and one we have not yet addressed. This is stated directly in the Limitations section (Lines 585-595), with multi-modal, real-world-artifact-seeded generation named as a direction we are pursuing.
3. The benchmark lacks tool-augmented baselines, even though tool use is natural in real-world engineering practice.
Response
We thank the reviewer for this observation. The current tool-free setting is a deliberate design choice to isolate intrinsic reasoning ability from tool-use competence (§5.2, Lines 439-442), and it has already produced a diagnostic result relevant to this question: the error taxonomy in §5.4 shows frontier-model failures are overwhelmingly arithmetic rather than conceptual (e.g., Gemini 3.1 Pro: 79.5% Calculation Errors vs. 14.5% conceptual), separating execution failures from reasoning failures within the tool-free setting itself.
Tool-augmented and retrieval-augmented evaluation is already named as future work in both the Conclusion (Lines 570-575) and the Limitations section (Lines 576-584).
